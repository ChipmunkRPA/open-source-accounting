## One market price, different measurement questions

A market quotation, an amortized-cost balance and recognized income answer different questions. The selected Federal Reserve reporting instructions distinguish held-to-maturity (HTM) measurement from available-for-sale (AFS) measurement. OCC's staff discussion separately addresses credit losses and noncredit changes. Neither source is a substitute for the current Codification or an entity-specific category assessment.

This exercise begins *after* an appropriate classification decision. It does not permit management to move securities between categories whenever a presentation becomes inconvenient. It also does not establish that a market decline is unrelated to credit merely because interest rates moved.

## Explicit assumptions for an original comparison

Two fictional, otherwise comparable reporting entities each purchase a different but economically identical fixed-rate debt security at its $75,000 par amount. Each receives a $4,500 annual coupon. One investment is already appropriately classified AFS; the other is already appropriately classified HTM. The comparison is between two hypothetical holders, not two classifications of the same holding in one ledger.

Assume no premium, discount, transaction cost, foreign currency, hedge, transfer, sale, credit loss or income tax. The HTM holder has the requisite intent and ability, established outside this exercise. The AFS holder neither intends to sell nor is more likely than not required to sell before recovering amortized cost. The stipulated valuation changes arise wholly from noncredit factors. Both coupons are received on schedule, and valuations are observed immediately afterward.

The fair value falls to $66,000 at the first year-end, then rises to $70,800 at the second. These inputs are original teaching assumptions, not actual valuation evidence.

## Keep the two ledgers separate

Each holder initially records debit investment $75,000 and credit cash $75,000. Each annual coupon produces debit cash $4,500 and credit interest income $4,500. Under the stipulated par-purchase facts, coupon income does not change the principal's amortized cost.

| After the annual coupon | AFS, year 1 | HTM, year 1 | AFS, year 2 | HTM, year 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Amortized cost | $75,000 | $75,000 | $75,000 | $75,000 |
| Observed fair value | $66,000 | $66,000 | $70,800 | $70,800 |
| Recognized investment balance | $66,000 | $75,000 | $70,800 | $75,000 |
| Annual interest income | $4,500 | $4,500 | $4,500 | $4,500 |
| Annual OCI movement | $(9,000) | $0 | $4,800 | $0 |
| Accumulated OCI from this holding | $(9,000) | $0 | $(4,200) | $0 |

For the first AFS valuation, the illustrative entry is debit unrealized loss in OCI $9,000 and credit the investment's fair-value adjustment $9,000. The investment bridge is $75,000 minus $9,000 equals $66,000. OCI is the period's movement; accumulated OCI is its cumulative equity balance.

For the second valuation, debit the fair-value adjustment $4,800 and credit unrealized gain in OCI $4,800. The cumulative adjustment is now negative $4,200: negative $9,000 plus $4,800. Thus $75,000 minus $4,200 equals $70,800. The rebound is not a second coupon, and the $4,800 gain does not erase the remaining $4,200 cumulative loss.

For HTM, this stipulated noncredit market movement creates no corresponding fair-value adjustment entry. That does not make the quotation irrelevant to disclosure, risk management or a credit assessment. Those are separate workstreams.

## A cross-statement reasonableness check

Across both years, AFS coupon income totals $9,000. Cumulative OCI is negative $4,200, so cumulative comprehensive income from this isolated position is $4,800. The same amount emerges from ending investment $70,800 plus coupons collected $9,000 minus purchase cash $75,000.

HTM's two-year recognized income is $9,000 under the assumptions. That difference does not imply that the two holdings generated different cash coupons. Their recognition bases differ.

## Changed facts require a new analysis

Suppose adverse issuer information makes part of the decline credit-related. The all-OCI premise has failed; an unsupported label such as “temporary market loss” cannot restore it. Separately, intended sale or a more-likely-than-not required sale before recovery changes the AFS analysis described by OCC. Do not reuse this table unchanged for either variation.

A real handoff needs the classification assessment, trade records, coupon reconciliation, valuation support, issuer credit evidence, sale-intent documentation and tax analysis. Arithmetic can validate the bridge without validating any of those inputs.

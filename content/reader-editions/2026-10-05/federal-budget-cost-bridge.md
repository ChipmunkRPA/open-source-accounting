# Federal accounting evidence workbook: budget authority, obligations, payments and accrued costs

**Reader edition 2026-10-05.1** · Wording changes are limited to labels and notices. Earlier check statements refer to the original article revision. No new professional review or source verification is claimed.

> **Editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Version 2.0.0, 2026-10-04. All entities, records, amounts and transaction events below are fictional. This free educational workbook teaches reconciliation under supplied assumptions. It does not establish federal accounting requirements, legal spending authority, a filing position or approved Agent evidence.

## Scope and the question to answer

The fictional Alder Federal Service Unit is preparing an internal evidence worksheet for the period ending September 30, 2026. Its preparer sees three numbers: a 100,000 authority amount, 70,000 of obligations and 30,000 of payments. Another extract reports 45,000 of consumed services. Which numbers answer different questions, and what must be investigated before anyone uses them in an actual report?

The setting is U.S. federal accounting research. It is not a state/local GASB example, a private-company accounting policy or an international public-sector comparison. The word **FASAB** in the library's framework filter identifies the research setting. Selected official sources were checked for the limited statements below; that is not approval of Alder's accounting treatment.

Keep the administrative labels, the original arithmetic and authoritative treatment separate. In this exercise, “authority” and “valid obligations” are supplied labels and assumptions. The workbook has not determined that an appropriation exists, that funds are legally available for a purpose or period, or that an order creates a legally valid obligation. An invoice, payment or worksheet label alone cannot answer those missing questions.

## Fictional fact packet and simplifying assumptions

All amounts are whole USD, with positive amounts displayed as comparison magnitudes. This is not a debit/credit convention. The exercise supplies:

- One entity, one explicitly defined order population and the same September 30 cutoff across all extracts
- An opening authority bucket of **100,000**, with no further changes in the exercise
- **70,000** of valid current-period obligations in that population, assumed solely for the teaching case; no cancellation, adjustment, uncertainty or disputed scope
- No opening obligations, unpaid balances or prepayments in this population
- **45,000** of services delivered, accepted and fully consumed in the period; the exercise stipulates that these services form the accrued service-cost measure
- **30,000** paid against those same delivered services; every payment is matched to a unique transaction and none settles an earlier-period item
- No equipment, inventory, advance payments, financing, taxes, discounts, refunds, nonexchange activity or other cost components

These restrictions make the arithmetic possible. In a real file, each would need evidence or an explicit unresolved state. They do not describe every federal entity or every kind of federal transaction.

### Transaction chronology

The fictional record IDs below are teaching labels, not authentic agreements or evidence that procedures were performed.

| Date or cutoff | Fictional record and event | Amount | What this supplies for this exercise |
|---|---|---:|---|
| September 1 | AUTH-A: opening authority amount supplied | 100,000 | Starting comparison bucket only; legal basis is not evaluated |
| September 2 | ORD-A/B/C: obligations supplied for three service lots | 70,000 | Lot A 30,000, Lot B 15,000 and Lot C 25,000; validity is an exercise assumption |
| September 12 | ACCEPT-A: Lot A delivered, accepted and consumed | 30,000 | Current-period service consumption, separate from the order date |
| September 25 | PAY-A: payment matched to Lot A | 30,000 | Settlement of the same population's delivered services |
| September 29 | ACCEPT-B: Lot B delivered, accepted and consumed | 15,000 | Additional current-period service consumption; still unpaid at cutoff |
| September 30 | STATUS-C: Lot C not delivered or paid by cutoff | 25,000 | Remaining undelivered portion of the supplied obligations |

The order date, service event and payment date are different facts. Do not substitute one for another because it is easier to obtain. For example, a payment dated September 25 does not by itself establish when a service was delivered or consumed.

## Four results, four meanings

| Exercise result | Reproducible calculation | Meaning under the supplied assumptions |
|---|---|---|
| Authority amount not assigned to the supplied obligations | 100,000 − 70,000 = **30,000** | A remainder in this worksheet; it is not a determination of legally available funds |
| Undelivered portion of the supplied obligations | 70,000 − 45,000 = **25,000** | Lot C remains undelivered; no current service consumption is supplied for that lot |
| Delivered-but-unpaid service balance | 45,000 − 30,000 = **15,000** | Lot B has been consumed but not paid under this exercise's facts |
| Current-period accrued service cost | 30,000 + 15,000 = **45,000** | Consumed services under the stipulated cost measure, including the unpaid lot |

Two cross-checks help keep the populations visible:

- Supplied obligations: **30,000 paid + 15,000 delivered but unpaid + 25,000 undelivered = 70,000**
- Outstanding, unpaid portion of those obligations: **70,000 − 30,000 = 40,000 = 15,000 delivered but unpaid + 25,000 undelivered**

Do not add 70,000 obligations to 45,000 cost and 30,000 payments to claim 145,000 of separate activity. The same transactions appear in different measures and stages. Similarly, the two 30,000 results are equal only because of the fictional inputs: one is the authority remainder, the other is cash paid. Neither establishes the other.

**A supportable statement about this exercise:** “The supplied 70,000 obligation population comprises 30,000 paid for consumed services, 15,000 consumed but unpaid and 25,000 undelivered. The stipulated accrued service-cost measure is 45,000. The 30,000 authority remainder has not been evaluated for legal availability.”

An unsupported statement would be: “Only 30,000 has been spent, so the current-period cost is 30,000 and another 70,000 can be spent freely.” It substitutes cash for the supplied cost measure and makes a legal conclusion the packet does not support.

These identities are a simple event bridge, not an official reconciliation format. They do not supply SF-133, Statement of Budgetary Resources, GTAS, USSGL account, Treasury attribute or financial-statement line mappings. Real populations may require other elements and classifications; do not force them into this five-number example.

## Counterexamples: change the assumptions before reusing the formulas

Each variant below is separate. Do not combine it with another variant or silently replace the main fact packet.

### 1. A payment settles an earlier-period liability

An alternative packet identifies **10,000** of the 30,000 payments as settlement of a prior-period service balance. It supplies an opening 10,000 unpaid balance, current-period consumed services of 45,000 and only **20,000** of payments matched to those current services.

The current services still contribute **45,000** to the stipulated current-period cost measure. Their unpaid portion is **45,000 − 20,000 = 25,000**. A combined payable-style teaching rollforward is **10,000 opening + 45,000 current services − 30,000 payments = 25,000 closing**. The main case's zero-opening assumption and 15,000 unpaid result no longer apply.

This rollforward does not decide the budgetary treatment of the earlier obligation or which reporting lines contain it. Retain the prior/current split and obtain its source and applicability evidence. Equal total cash does not establish equal current-period cost.

### 2. A payment precedes delivery

Another alternative packet identifies **20,000** paid for current consumed services and **10,000** paid in advance for a separate future delivery. Current consumed services remain 45,000. Only 20,000 is matched to that consumption, leaving **25,000** of those services unpaid. The separate 10,000 advance has its own delivery, recovery and classification questions.

Do not subtract all 30,000 from the current consumed-service population and report 15,000 unpaid. Equally, do not count the advance as consumed services solely because cash moved. This workbook does not decide whether the advance is permitted, recoverable or recognized as a particular asset. Its relationship to the supplied obligation population must be established before reusing the obligations bridge.

### 3. Delivery includes equipment rather than only consumed services

A third packet replaces 20,000 of the 45,000 delivered amount with equipment received near cutoff. It supplies only **25,000** of consumed services. The previous “every delivered amount is a consumed service” assumption is false.

The 25,000 service component can still be identified from the stated facts. Total current-period cost cannot be determined from this packet: equipment classification, capitalization policy, placement in service, useful-life estimates and any period allocation remain unresolved. Neither zero equipment cost nor an immediate 20,000 cost follows from receipt alone. Do not invent a threshold, depreciation rule or journal entry to complete the table.

### 4. A proposed cancellation or disputed acceptance changes the evidence

A preparer proposes canceling 5,000 from undelivered Lot C. A proposal alone does not revise the supplied 70,000 obligation record. Keep the original extract and label the 5,000 as proposed until the transaction's status, authority, effective date and recorded change are established.

For arithmetic comparison only, a **separate revised fictional dataset** supplies 65,000 obligations and 20,000 undelivered, with delivered services and payments unchanged. Its identities become **30,000 + 15,000 + 20,000 = 65,000** and **100,000 − 65,000 = 35,000**. That 35,000 remainder still does not establish spending availability. The revised scenario is not evidence that the cancellation happened in the original scenario.

If Lot B's acceptance is disputed instead, do not silently remove its 15,000 or assert that it must remain accrued. Retain the documents, dispute and timing, identify alternative factual outcomes, and defer the unsupported recognition conclusion. A missing acceptance record is not the same fact as proof of nonperformance.

Use one row per transaction event and retain the original extracts separately. A source link cannot substantiate an entity-specific transaction.

## From an event bridge to an accrual-to-cash explanation

The main example separates stages of a transaction. A different question asks why the period's cost differs from the period's cash payments. To answer it, first decide exactly which two measures are being reconciled. A bridge to cash is not automatically the official budget and accrual reconciliation: real net outlays, financing activity, collections and reporting classifications require their own evidence.

Here is a second, independent fictional dataset. All amounts are USD; balances are stated as positive magnitudes. Assume these are the only activities, all records cover the same unit and year, and the equipment's capitalization and period depreciation have already been validly determined elsewhere:

| Supplied fact | Amount | What is in this teaching model |
|---|---:|---|
| Current consumed services | 84,000 | Included in current cost |
| Opening service payable | 9,000 | Cost belonged to an earlier period |
| Closing service payable | 21,000 | Current services not yet paid |
| Equipment bought and paid for this year | 48,000 | Included in cash; not expensed on purchase under the supplied policy |
| Current depreciation | 6,000 | Included in current cost; no separate current cash payment |

Service cash is **9,000 + 84,000 − 21,000 = 72,000**. Total cash payments are **72,000 + 48,000 = 120,000**. Total modeled cost is **84,000 + 6,000 = 90,000**. Starting with that cost produces the same cash result:

**90,000 − 6,000 − (21,000 − 9,000) + 48,000 = 120,000.**

The payable increased by 12,000, meaning that more service cost remained unpaid at year end than at the beginning. Subtract that increase to move from service cost toward service cash. Equipment purchase cash is added because the whole purchase was not included in the assumed period cost; depreciation is removed because it was included in cost without a separate cash payment. These sign explanations are about this defined model, not account-posting instructions.

### Reconcile both ends before explaining the middle

A balanced equation can conceal two wrong endpoints. Recalculate the cost total from the source cost population and the cash total from the complete payment population independently. Then connect each reconciling item to a rollforward or transaction population. If the cost ledger shows 91,000 rather than 90,000, retain a 1,000 unresolved residual. Do not rename it “timing” without locating a timing difference.

If 8,000 of the equipment purchase was unpaid, change the facts rather than only changing the answer: equipment cash becomes 40,000 and total cash becomes 112,000. The equipment payable is separate from the service payable above. Subtracting an all-purpose 20,000 payable increase while also using the already-net 40,000 equipment cash would count the equipment payable effect twice. A clear population definition prevents that error.

### A reviewer-ready explanation

A useful draft conclusion identifies the two endpoints, explains each sign in ordinary language, states which balances were independently tied out, and lists unresolved items. “Cash exceeded cost because equipment cash exceeded depreciation after the unpaid-service change” is more informative than “the reconciliation balances.” Neither sentence establishes fund availability, obligations, a Treasury classification, or the official net-outlay amount.

Before adapting this calculation, ask: Are there collections or refunds? Asset purchases without cash? Payments of prior liabilities? Advances? Noncash outlays? Transfers or financing activity? Different entity perimeters? Those questions tell the preparer where this simplified model may fail; they do not prescribe a classification.

## Five study questions with draft expectations

1. **Why are both payments and the authority remainder 30,000?** The fictional inputs happen to produce equal numbers. Payments are 30,000; the other amount is 100,000 minus 70,000. They answer different questions, and the remainder does not establish legal availability.
2. **What makes up the 40,000 unpaid obligation portion in the main case?** The supplied facts separate 15,000 of consumed-but-unpaid services and 25,000 undelivered. Treating the full 40,000 as current-period service cost would disregard that distinction.
3. **If 10,000 of total payments settles the supplied opening liability, what is the current service balance left unpaid?** With 45,000 current consumed services and 20,000 matched current payments, it is 25,000. The separately supplied opening 10,000 is settled; the main case's zero-opening assumption no longer holds.
4. **Can the equipment variant retain the main case's 45,000 current cost conclusion?** No. Only 25,000 of consumed services is supplied, and the equipment's treatment remains unresolved. Receipt and payment do not fill in the missing policy and applicability evidence.
5. **Does a balanced bridge establish the correct official account, report line or spending authority?** No. It verifies an arithmetic relationship under stated assumptions. Source rights, complete facts, legal/accounting applicability, real review and any posting or filing authorization remain separate.

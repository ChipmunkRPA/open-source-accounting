# Expected credit losses: build a pooled estimate and explain its movement

## The accounting idea

An allowance is a balance-sheet estimate; the provision is a period expense. They are related, but they are not the same number. In the CECL setting, estimating what will not be collected involves the exposure's remaining life and evidence about credit conditions. A percentage calculated from last year's write-offs is an input to investigate, not a self-validating conclusion.

The Federal Reserve's interagency policy discusses similar-risk pooling, historical information, current conditions and supportable forecasts in the Topic 326 setting. This is banking-agency guidance; do not extend every supervisory instruction to all companies. The FDIC link below is a discovery reference only; direct retrieval was denied.

## Original worked example

Suppose a lender has two already-validated pools of amortized-cost receivables at year-end. Pool Cedar is $240,000 and Pool Harbor is $160,000. For this exercise only, assume management has supported lifetime loss rates of 1.25% and 2.50%, respectively. These are invented inputs, not benchmarks or recommended rates.

- Cedar: $240,000 × 1.25% = $3,000
- Harbor: $160,000 × 2.50% = $4,000
- Required closing allowance under these assumptions: $7,000
- Net receivables: $400,000 − $7,000 = $393,000

Now add the year's history. The opening allowance was $5,500, actual write-offs were $2,000, recoveries credited to the allowance were $500, and there were no acquisitions, disposals or currency effects. Before this year's provision, the allowance is $4,000. The provision needed to reach $7,000 is therefore $3,000:

$5,500 + $3,000 − $2,000 + $500 = $7,000.

The illustrative provision entry is a $3,000 debit to credit-loss expense and a $3,000 credit to the allowance. Charging $7,000 to expense would ignore the balance already present.

## Why the model can be wrong even when it adds up

If Harbor includes secured loans and unsecured loans whose credit behavior differs, one pool may conceal information. Conversely, isolating every delinquent account without a reason can remove the losses from the population used to estimate a pool's risk. Compare the total exposure before and after every segmentation change.

A 0.50 percentage-point increase in Harbor's assumed lifetime loss rate adds $800 to the allowance. It does not add 50% to the entire receivables balance. Keep sensitivities visibly separate from the selected estimate.

## What a useful conclusion contains

Explain the population, the reason the two pools are sensible, the remaining contractual period, forecast evidence and the reconciliation to the ledger. State which inputs are assumed and which are observed. Investigate loan commitments, lease receivables, securities, acquired loans and other instruments separately before reusing this simple model.

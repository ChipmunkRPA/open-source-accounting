# Audit, review, compilation and preparation: begin with the report the user needs

## The service distinction

Financial-statement preparation, compilation, review and audit do not describe four cosmetic versions of the same report. Preparation helps produce statements without assurance. A compilation also provides no assurance, but is a distinct engagement with an accountant's report. A review provides limited assurance; an audit seeks reasonable assurance and results in an opinion. Reasonable assurance is high assurance, not a promise that every error or fraud will be discovered.

For nonissuer engagements, research the applicable AICPA AU-C or AR-C framework and current amendments. A public-company or other specialized engagement may have different governing requirements. This article supplies an original service-selection explanation, not illustrative report language or a complete standard checklist.

## Original decision scenario

A fictional bakery, Juniper Oven, has three requests:

1. Its owner wants monthly statements to manage payroll and inventory
2. Its lender's signed agreement requires annual reviewed financial statements
3. A potential investor asks for audited annual statements before investing

Producing the monthly statements does not by itself satisfy the lender's review condition. Paying for a compilation would not turn it into limited assurance. Likewise, a review would not supply the investor's requested audit opinion. The bakery needs to verify which requests are actual obligations, their deadlines and whether one properly scoped annual engagement can serve more than one user.

Suppose all three versions show annual revenue of $720,000 and net income of $54,000. Identical totals do not imply identical evidence or assurance. The distinction lies in the engagement, work performed, responsibilities and report, not in whether the figures are printed on an accountant's letterhead.

## Independence and management responsibility

Independence is required for audits and reviews. A compilation can be performed in circumstances involving lack of independence, but the report implications require attention. Do not assume an accountant is independent merely because the accountant is not an employee, or that preparing books always determines the conclusion either way. The applicable independence analysis must be performed.

Management remains responsible for the financial statements and the information supplied. Outsourcing statement preparation does not transfer all responsibility to the CPA, and the service choice does not relieve anyone from keeping usable records.

## What to ask before signing an engagement

Identify the reporting entity, period, accounting framework, intended users, exact deliverable, deadline and any statutory, lender or grant requirements. Ask the CPA which service is appropriate and what information must be ready. Do not draft a report by replacing “compiled” with “reviewed” in a template.

Louisiana's official guide illustrates distinctions among audit, review/attestation and compilation in its own statutory reporting system. Its combined review/attestation category and monetary thresholds are jurisdiction-specific and are not used here as general rules.

# Capital impairment and insurance: separate the asset loss from the recovery timeline

## Three questions need three evidence files

After an asset is damaged, the government may have an impairment, an insurance claim and restoration work. They can arise from the same event without having the same amount or recognition date. The insurance estimate is not automatically the asset loss, and the repair invoice is not automatically the impairment measurement.

Texas Comptroller guidance describes impairment in terms of permanent loss of service utility and distinguishes it from reduced use of available capacity. It also says a recognized impairment is not reversed merely because service utility later returns. [Texas AFR impairment overview, including Temporary Impairments](https://fmx.cpa.texas.gov/fmx/pubs/afrrptreq/cap_assets/index.php?page=impairment&section=impairment).

The companion recovery guidance distinguishes government-wide and proprietary reporting from governmental-fund reporting. For the former, it addresses a realized or realizable recovery in the loss year, separate reporting when recovery is recognized later, and separation from restoration transactions. [Texas AFR Insurance Recoveries](https://fmx.cpa.texas.gov/fmx/pubs/afrrptreq/cap_assets/index.php?page=recoveries&section=impairment).

The exercise below uses only the government-wide accrual perspective and an ordinary impairment, with unusual/infrequent-item classification excluded. It does not adopt Texas account codes or determine a reporting entity's applicable GASB 103 transition.

## Synthetic Year 1: record supported amounts, not the claim request

A fictional government operates a water-control facility. Immediately before an unexpected damaging event, the facility has $900,000 of recorded cost and $300,000 of accumulated depreciation. All depreciation through the example's measurement point has already been recorded. Opening carrying value for this exercise is therefore $600,000.

Assume a separate technical assessment establishes a permanent, significant loss of service utility and an independently measured impairment of $165,000. The facility will continue in use. Neither the measurement method nor the evidence supporting that amount is supplied by this article.

The insurance file contains three distinct items at the reporting date:

- $55,000 has been received and qualifies for recovery recognition
- Another $40,000 is independently established as realizable and qualifies for recognition, although collection will occur later
- A further $25,000 is disputed and does not qualify for recognition at this date

The request totals $120,000, but the supported recovery is $95,000. Recognizing the entire request would overstate the demonstrated recovery and receivable by $25,000. Calling the unsupported amount zero for this closing entry is not a conclusion that the claim has no possible value; its status remains unresolved.

## Reconcile asset measurement and recovery separately

The impairment reduces the facility's $600,000 carrying value to $435,000. Illustratively, debit impairment loss $165,000 and credit the capital-asset carrying-value reduction account $165,000, using the government's supported detailed register convention.

Separately, debit cash $55,000 and insurance receivable $40,000 and credit recovery $95,000. In this stipulated ordinary, same-year government-wide presentation, the $165,000 loss and $95,000 recovery produce a $70,000 net impairment expense.

The recovery does not increase the facility back to $530,000. The facility remains $435,000 before later work. The $95,000 is represented by $55,000 of cash and $40,000 receivable. Combining those different assets may help reconcile total net position, but does not change the damaged facility's carrying amount.

## Restoration is another transaction

During the same closing interval, the government pays $22,000 for work independently assessed as repairs and maintenance. It also pays $48,000 for a separate improvement that independently satisfies the applicable capitalization criteria. The split is a supplied conclusion, not something inferred from the contractor's invoice label. There are no other balances or movements, and subsequent depreciation is outside this short interval.

The repairs debit expense and credit cash $22,000. The improvement debits capital assets and credits cash $48,000. Closing capital-asset carrying value is $435,000 + $48,000 = $483,000. Cash changes by $55,000 − $22,000 − $48,000 = a $15,000 decrease. The insurance receivable is $40,000.

The changes reconcile: capital assets decrease $117,000, cash decreases $15,000 and receivables increase $40,000. Total net assets decrease $92,000, matching the $70,000 net impairment expense plus $22,000 repair expense. The $48,000 improvement is neither a second impairment expense nor an automatic reversal of the original loss.

## Year 2: distinguish collection from new recognition

When the previously recognized $40,000 is collected, debit cash and credit the receivable. There is no new recovery income from collecting that already recorded amount.

Assume genuinely new Year 2 evidence resolves the previously unrecognized $25,000 dispute for $18,000, which is then received and qualifies for recognition. The later $18,000 recovery is recorded separately in Year 2. It does not establish a Year 1 receivable retrospectively under these stipulated facts. If the new evidence instead shows Year 1 information was mishandled, an error or subsequent-event analysis would be required; this example does not decide that different case.

Before omitted depreciation or other activity, the two-year expense less recovery is $92,000 − $18,000 = $74,000. The uncollected $7,000 difference from the disputed request is not a write-off of a recorded receivable, because that disputed $25,000 was never recognized here.

## Parallel timing case

Change the facts so none of the otherwise qualifying $95,000 is realized or realizable in Year 1, and all of it first qualifies and is collected in Year 2. Keep the $165,000 impairment, $22,000 repairs and $48,000 improvement unchanged; omit the separate disputed claim for this comparison.

Year 1 expense is then $187,000 and Year 2 recovery is $95,000. Across the two years the net expense remains $92,000, but the annual presentation differs sharply. A matching cumulative total does not justify recording a receivable before the stipulated recognition condition is met.

## What the bank balance cannot explain

Money held for others needs an accounting trail that explains both the resources held and the events requiring distribution. A cash register alone cannot distinguish an uncollected addition from an unpaid deduction. This original exercise connects those two timing differences without deciding whether an actual arrangement qualifies as a custodial activity.

The selected Texas guidance uses an economic-resources focus and accrual accounting for fiduciary reporting. The statement of fiduciary net position presents balances; the statement of changes in fiduciary net position presents additions and deductions. These are separate statements. The latter is not a cash-flow statement. A beneficiary liability follows an event compelling disbursement, rather than an automatic assumption that every dollar received is immediately payable.

## Original scenario and supplied conclusions

A fictional regional government holds resources for three beneficiary organizations: Harbor, Mesa and Vale. Assume a documented classification analysis has already concluded that the arrangement belongs in a custodial fund. The government is not a beneficiary. The example stipulates recognition of the additions below, collectibility of all receivables, and the specific events compelling the recorded distributions. Those are inputs, not conclusions inferred from these organization names or the existence of a bank account.

At January 1, the custodial records show $48,000 cash, $7,000 receivables and $19,000 beneficiary payables. Opening fiduciary net position is therefore $36,000: $48,000 plus $7,000 minus $19,000. There are no other opening balances or deferred resource items.

The following current-year activity is independently invented. “Paid” in the final column concerns only current-year distribution obligations.

| Beneficiary | Recognized additions | Cash collected against those additions | Distribution deductions incurred | Current-year distributions paid |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Harbor | $100,000 | $95,000 | $88,000 | $84,000 |
| Mesa | $72,000 | $65,000 | $64,000 | $60,000 |
| Vale | $48,000 | $45,000 | $44,000 | $40,000 |
| Total | $220,000 | $205,000 | $196,000 | $184,000 |

Separately, the government collects the entire opening $7,000 receivable and pays all opening $19,000 beneficiary payables. It incurs $4,500 of administrative costs attributable to this activity, pays $3,500 and owes $1,000 at year-end. Assume these costs properly belong in this custodial fund. There are no investment returns, transfers or other transactions.

## Build the balance statement from subsidiary records

Current-year uncollected additions total $15,000: $220,000 minus $205,000. Harbor accounts for $5,000, Mesa $7,000 and Vale $3,000. Collection of the opening receivable does not create another addition; that resource was recognized previously.

Unpaid current-year beneficiary obligations are $12,000: $196,000 minus $184,000, comprising $4,000 for each beneficiary. Together with the administrative payable of $1,000, total closing liabilities are $13,000. Paying the opening liability does not create a second current-year deduction.

The cash bridge is $48,000 opening cash, plus $205,000 current collections, plus $7,000 opening-receivable collections, less $184,000 current distributions, less $19,000 opening-payable settlements, less $3,500 administrative payments. Closing cash is $53,500.

The statement of fiduciary net position consequently has $68,500 of assets, consisting of $53,500 cash and $15,000 receivables. Subtracting $13,000 liabilities leaves $55,500 of fiduciary net position. Assume the applicable classification of that balance is restricted for the beneficiaries. It is not a resource available to finance the government's unrelated operations.

## Reconcile the changes statement independently

Additions are $220,000. Deductions comprise $196,000 beneficiary distributions incurred and $4,500 administrative costs, totaling $200,500. The increase in fiduciary net position is $19,500. Opening $36,000 plus that increase equals closing $55,500, matching the balance calculation.

Cash increased only $5,500. The $14,000 difference from the $19,500 accrual increase has two parts: receivables increased $8,000, from $7,000 to $15,000, and liabilities decreased $6,000, from $19,000 to $13,000. The arithmetic confirms that collections and payments cannot substitute for additions and deductions here.

For teaching, summarize current additions as debits of $205,000 to cash and $15,000 to receivables, with a $220,000 credit to additions. Record beneficiary deductions with a $196,000 debit to deductions and credit to beneficiary payables; debit payables and credit cash for $184,000 of current settlements. Opening-balance settlements use the same asset or liability accounts without repeating additions or deductions. Administrative deductions debit $4,500, with credits of $3,500 cash and $1,000 payable. Actual posting dates follow the underlying events.

## Changed fact and closing evidence

Suppose Harbor's event compelling an unpaid $4,000 distribution occurs in January of the following year, rather than before December 31. Assume this changes neither additions nor cash. Current beneficiary deductions fall to $192,000 and beneficiary payables to $8,000. Total deductions become $196,500; the increase in net position is $23,500. Closing net position rises to $59,500, while closing cash remains $53,500. This sensitivity changes one stipulated event date; it does not establish a legal trigger for another arrangement.

For a real close, attach receivable support, dated trigger evidence, beneficiary-level payable reconciliations and bank settlement records. A balanced spreadsheet does not prove that the recognition dates are right. Nor should governmental-fund revenue-availability conventions be inserted into this stipulated fiduciary accrual exercise.

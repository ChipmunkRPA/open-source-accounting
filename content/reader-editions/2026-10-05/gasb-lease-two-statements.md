# Government leases: follow one payment through the liability and the fund

## The accounting idea

A lease-payment invoice combines financing and asset-use questions. For a recognized government lease, the government-wide liability tracks the unpaid financing amount, while the right-to-use asset tracks the resource being consumed. Governmental-fund reporting adds a separate current-financial-resources perspective.

Texas Comptroller's lessee guidance illustrates initial capital outlay and other financing sources in governmental funds, conversion entries, principal/interest separation and right-to-use amortization. Its account codes and large example are state-specific. None of those original numbers are used below.

## Original worked example

Assume a government has completed lease identification and measurement. At commencement, a qualifying four-year lease has an initial liability of $200,000 and an equal right-to-use asset. These amounts are supplied to this case; the case does not independently calculate present value. Assume no prepaid rent, incentives, direct costs, impairment, residual value or later remeasurement. The relevant annual discount rate is assumed to be 4%.

During year one, the government makes a single $56,000 payment at year-end:

- Interest: $200,000 × 4% = $8,000
- Principal reduction: $56,000 − $8,000 = $48,000
- Closing liability: $200,000 − $48,000 = $152,000
- Straight-line asset amortization over the assumed four-year term: $50,000
- Closing net right-to-use asset: $150,000

Government-wide year-one expense in this simplified case totals $58,000: $8,000 interest plus $50,000 amortization. It differs from the $56,000 cash payment. The $2,000 difference is not an unexplained error; it follows from the two measurement patterns.

Do not assume that the remaining three cash payments are also $56,000. The example supplies only the first payment, and a full schedule must reconcile all contractual payments to the initial liability.

## The governmental-fund lens

Under the stipulated recognized-lease model, commencement produces capital-outlay expenditure and an offsetting other financing source for the financed amount. Later payments are separated into debt-service principal and interest expenditures. Conversion to government-wide reporting removes the financing-source/capital-outlay effects and recognizes the asset and liability.

For year one, the fund's debt-service expenditure is $56,000 while the illustrated government-wide expense is $58,000. Starting with fund results, add back $48,000 of principal and subtract $50,000 amortization. Interest is already represented in both views under the assumptions.

## What can change the schedule

Term options, variable payments, nonlease components, short-term exceptions, discount-rate evidence, commencement dates and modifications require separate decisions. A signed contract date need not be the date the asset is available for use. Do not import a private-company finance/operating label into the government model without checking the correct framework.

# Debt and equity-linked instruments: connect the terms to the cash

**Reader edition 2026-10-05.1** · Wording changes are limited to labels and notices. Earlier check statements refer to the original article revision. No new professional review or source verification is claimed.

**Educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · Editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

Build a financing evidence file that keeps contractual amounts, settlement cash, fees and conditional settlement calculations distinct. An instrument's name and a bank receipt do not supply its complete terms. This original fictional exercise develops the existing guide's promised term matrix and proceeds reconciliation; it does not classify an instrument or select an accounting model.

## Scope and authority map

## 1. Define the fictional packet before calculating

All identifiers, clause labels and amounts below are invented for this exercise. They are not real documents, executed agreements, source citations, review receipts or evidence that a financing exists. Monetary amounts are USD; share counts are a separate unit. No taxes, foreign exchange, additional instruments or other cash movements are included in the stipulated arithmetic. Those omissions are limits to investigate in a real engagement.

The fictional main-document extract A describes principal of 1000000. A fictional closing statement C records that gross amount, a lender withholding of 20000 and a resulting bank wire of 980000. A separate fictional bank-payment record B describes a 10000 adviser payment after the wire. These are three views of the same closing, not three funding receipts. The example stipulates the stated purposes of the two costs; it does not establish their accounting treatment or recoverability.

| Packet label | Stipulated observation | Coverage still missing |
|---|---|---|
| A §1 | Principal 1000000; stated maturity at the end of month 12 | Complete definitions, conditions, amendments and original execution record |
| A §2 | Simple coupon illustration uses principal × annual rate 0.12 × fraction 3/12 | Actual payment calendar, day-count convention, accrual and settlement provisions |
| A §4 | Hypothetical principal-only conversion calculation uses USD 5 per share | Who can elect settlement, when, alternatives, adjustments and every condition |
| C lines 1–3 | Gross amount 1000000; withholding 20000; wire 980000 | Complete closing statement, invoice basis and independent bank confirmation |
| B payment 1 | Adviser payment 10000 after the wire | Payee, invoice, authorization and whether the cost belongs to this arrangement |
| SL-B | A separate document marked draft proposes USD 4 per share | Complete status, execution, chronology, consistency and legal effect |

These are incomplete extracts by design. The draft label on SL-B is an observation, not a legal opinion. Keep the original packet and the alternative separate until an authorized reviewer establishes the applicable terms. A missing side letter is not evidence that no side letter exists.

## 2. Reconcile the closing without changing the principal

The signed numbers in the following table are changes in the cash bridge, not debits, credits or approved expenses. The running amount after the withholding is the stipulated incoming wire; the final amount is the net bank movement after the separate payment.

| Bridge row | Amount added or subtracted | Running amount | Fictional record |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Gross closing amount | 1000000 | 1000000 | C line 1; compare A §1 |
| Lender withholding | -20000 | 980000 | C line 2 |
| Adviser payment | -10000 | 970000 | B payment 1 |

The independent bank-view reconciliation is **980000 received − 10000 paid = 970000 net movement**. The two stipulated costs total **30000**, which explains the difference between the 1000000 gross amount and the 970000 net movement. This arithmetic does not make 970000 the contractual principal, a carrying amount or an amount to record in a journal.

Two deliberately wrong paths expose different errors:

- Starting with the already-net 980000 wire and subtracting both 20000 and 10000 again gives 950000. That double-counts the withholding and understates this stipulated net movement by 20000
- Treating the 1000000 closing amount and the 980000 bank wire as separate receipts, then subtracting 10000, gives 1970000. That duplicates the same financing and overstates net movement by 1000000

If the payment record cannot be substantiated, flag the 10000 as unresolved; do not invent an invoice or silently remove the observed payment. Confirm complete populations and other cash movements before carrying this small exercise into a real reconciliation.

## 3. Show what the conditional calculations do and do not mean

The coupon formula is stipulated solely to make its inputs inspectable. It is not a claim about a real contract's accrual convention, an effective interest rate or recognized interest expense. No date-derived day count is computed.

| Coupon scenario | Base amount | Annual rate | Stipulated fraction | Calculated amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stipulated principal basis | 1000000 | 0.12 | 3/12 | 30000 |
| Deliberately wrong net-cash basis | 970000 | 0.12 | 3/12 | 29100 |

The wrong base reduces the result by 900. A numerically tidy calculation cannot resolve the choice of contract, accounting model or cost treatment. Keep the 30000 cost total in section 2 separate from the 30000 coupon result; their equality is coincidental and they must not be substituted for one another.

The following is a **conditional share-count sensitivity**, not a conclusion that conversion is available, required or correctly classified. Assume for each row that exactly 1000000 of principal, and no unpaid coupon or other amount, is divided by the stated USD-per-share figure. Fractional-share handling is outside this whole-number example.

| Separate hypothetical branch | Principal numerator | USD per share | Shares |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A calculation at 5 | 1000000 | 5 | 200000 |
| Alternative calculation at 4 | 1000000 | 4 | 250000 |

The alternative increases the conditional count by 50000 shares, or 25% of the first branch. Do not overwrite A with SL-B or treat the latter as an operative amendment. The two calculations merely show why the unresolved term matters. Using net cash of 970000 at 5 would instead produce 194000 shares, 6000 fewer than the stipulated principal-only calculation. A ledger balance, a cash amount and a conversion numerator must each have their own supported definition.

## 5. Study questions and bounded answers

These questions have not been professionally adjudicated. They are embedded in this guide and remain outside the unchanged 90-record question bank.

1. **Why do the gross amount and bank wire differ?** The exercise stipulates a 20000 withholding. Verify both records; do not invent a second receipt or infer cost treatment from the reconciliation.
2. **Why is 950000 wrong for this packet's net cash movement?** Starting from the 980000 wire already reflects the withholding. Subtracting it again duplicates that reduction; only the separate 10000 payment remains in the stipulated bank view.
3. **Can the equal 30000 cost and coupon totals be combined into one conclusion?** No. They come from different records, formulas and unresolved accounting questions. Matching numbers do not establish matching meaning.
4. **What does the 25% share-count sensitivity prove?** Only the arithmetic effect of changing the stipulated denominator from 5 to 4 with the same 1000000 numerator. It does not prove that an amendment is operative or any settlement/classification conclusion.
5. **What stops a final instrument classification?** The packet intentionally omits complete operative terms, applicable primary guidance, and qualified review of legal, accounting and valuation questions. The financing label and cash tie do not close those gaps.

## Completed editorial checks and a retained limit

The gross-to-net cash bridge, both deliberate error paths, coupon bases, share-count branches and five answers were checked. Equal USD 30000 totals for fees and the stipulated coupon have different origins; they are not duplicate evidence or one combined accounting conclusion.

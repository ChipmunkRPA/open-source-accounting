# Mutual funds: reconcile periodic marks and a partial sale

## Source boundary

This guide uses narrowly examined OCC staff commentary, not acquired FASB Codification text. OCC generally places mutual funds in equity-investment measurement and recognizes changes in earnings, including changes before sale. [OCC Bank Accounting Advisory Series, Topic 1A, questions 4–5](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/banker-education/files/pub-bank-accounting-advisory-series.pdf#page=6).

That is the complete source-derived premise for the exercise. The register, dates expressed as periods, quantities, prices, entries and checking procedures below are original. The guide does not reproduce a publisher example or establish which model applies to a real instrument.

## Fix the unit and the perimeter

A fictional bank owns 8,000 units of a plain mutual fund. Classification within ASC 321 is stipulated, rather than inferred from the fund's name or underlying holdings. The fund is outside consolidation and equity-method accounting. All units have the same economic terms. There are no distributions, transaction costs, currency changes or income taxes in this exercise.

At the start of Quarter 1, each unit has an already recorded carrying value of $12.50. This is also the invented acquisition cost, so there are no earlier gains to track. Total carrying value is $100,000. The bank makes no purchases during either quarter.

The supplied per-unit fair values are $13.20 at the end of Quarter 1, $12.80 immediately before a sale in Quarter 2, and $13.40 at the end of Quarter 2. The values are independently stipulated for teaching arithmetic; the exercise does not demonstrate a valuation technique. The bank sells 2,500 units for $12.80 each, immediately after the sale-date mark. The remaining 5,500 units stay in the account.

## Keep the three measurements separate

At the first reporting date, 8,000 × $13.20 = $105,600. The $5,600 increase is the Quarter 1 earnings effect. The illustrative entry debits the investment asset and credits fair-value gain, each for $5,600.

Immediately before the sale, all 8,000 units are measured at $12.80. Their aggregate amount is $102,400. The $3,200 decrease from $105,600 is a Quarter 2 loss. Debit fair-value loss and credit the investment asset for $3,200.

The sale then produces $32,000 cash: 2,500 × $12.80. Those units also have a $32,000 carrying amount immediately before disposal. Debit cash $32,000 and credit the investment asset $32,000. The separate disposal entry has no further gain. It does not reverse any gain or loss already recognized.

After the sale, the remaining asset is $70,400, equal to 5,500 × $12.80. At the second reporting date it becomes $73,700, equal to 5,500 × $13.40. Debit the investment asset and credit fair-value gain for the $3,300 increase.

| Register movement | Investment change | Cash change | Earnings effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Opening amount | $100,000 balance | — | — |
| Quarter 1 measurement | +$5,600 | $0 | +$5,600 |
| Quarter 2 sale-date measurement | −$3,200 | $0 | −$3,200 |
| Sale of 2,500 units | −$32,000 | +$32,000 | $0 |
| Quarter 2 closing measurement | +$3,300 | $0 | +$3,300 |
| Closing amount / cumulative movement | $73,700 balance | +$32,000 | +$5,700 |

Quarter 2 earnings are only $100: the $3,300 closing increase less the $3,200 earlier decrease. The cumulative two-quarter gain is $5,700. Calling the $32,000 receipt “income” would confuse proceeds with the economic result.

## Check the answer by ownership history

The units sold had an original cost of 2,500 × $12.50 = $31,250. Their cumulative gain is $750. The retained units had an original cost of $68,750 and finish at $73,700, a $4,950 cumulative gain. Together $750 + $4,950 = $5,700.

A second independent check adds the two resources now held: $32,000 cash plus $73,700 investment, or $105,700. Subtract the opening $100,000 to obtain the same $5,700. This works because there are no other cash flows, liabilities or transactions in the stipulated perimeter.

These two checks are useful when an investment subledger reports both periodic marks and a “realized” result. If the sale entry separately credits $750 after all the entries above, cumulative income would become $6,450. The extra $750 has already passed through the unit marks. The error is duplicate recognition, not an objection to using realized/unrealized labels for a properly reconciled analytical report.

## Change only the last price

Consider an independent alternative: the Quarter 2 closing price is $11.90 rather than $13.40. Keep the opening units, first reporting date and sale unchanged. The final asset becomes 5,500 × $11.90 = $65,450. Compared with the $70,400 held immediately after the sale, that creates a $4,950 closing loss.

Quarter 2 now has an $8,150 loss: $3,200 + $4,950. Across both quarters, $5,600 − $8,150 = a $2,550 loss. The resource check again agrees: $32,000 + $65,450 − $100,000 = −$2,550. This is a parallel scenario, not a proposed retrospective replacement of a previously issued measurement.

## Assemble the close file

Retain dated unit statements, the supported pricing evidence, the sale confirmation, cash settlement and a movement register that separates opening balances, purchases, disposals and measurements. Tie quantities before reconciling monetary balances: 8,000 − 2,500 = 5,500. Identify a sale-date mark already imported by the custody system before adding another adjustment.

A mathematically balanced register does not establish reliable fair value, classification, tax treatment or disclosure sufficiency. Distributions, restrictions, unsettled trades, fees and a different legal form require their own analysis. Do not extend this exercise to every fund interest or directly held debt security merely because the holdings appear similar.

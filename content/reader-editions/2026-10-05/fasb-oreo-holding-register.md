# Foreclosed property held for sale: reconcile carrying value and holding activity

## Give each property its own measurement record

A property register should explain both the asset remaining at period end and the income earned or costs incurred while holding it. Combining those questions can conceal a valuation loss or turn routine security bills into unsupported asset additions.

OCC's selected staff guidance measures held-for-sale OREO property by property, using a cost ceiling and fair value less estimated selling costs. Holding income and expenses are separate. An allowance can decrease when supported; a previous direct write-off cannot simply be restored. These are bank-specific application premises, not a complete reproduction of FASB requirements. [OCC, Topic 5A, questions 1 and 6–9](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/banker-education/files/pub-bank-accounting-advisory-series.pdf#page=85).

## Supply the opening balances before calculating the close

A fictional bank already holds two completed commercial properties for sale. Classification, recognition dates and initial measurement have been resolved independently. This original exercise begins with their established balances; it does not derive property cost from a foreclosure loan.

North Arcade has a $264,000 cost basis and a $19,000 opening valuation allowance. West Depot has a $176,000 cost basis and no opening allowance. Neither property has undergone development, construction, improvements, a sale or a change of use during the quarter. There are no direct write-offs in this main scenario.

The closing estimates below are independently stipulated current measurements. Estimated selling costs have not yet been paid. They are measurement inputs, distinct from the paid holding bills discussed later.

| Property | Cost basis | Opening allowance | Opening net asset | Closing fair value | Estimated selling costs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North Arcade | $264,000 | $19,000 | $245,000 | $302,000 | $14,000 |
| West Depot | $176,000 | $0 | $176,000 | $181,000 | $21,000 |
| Total | $440,000 | $19,000 | $421,000 | $483,000 | $35,000 |

## Reconcile the valuation columns

North's net selling-value estimate is $302,000 − $14,000 = $288,000. Its cost ceiling remains $264,000. The required closing allowance is zero, so the existing $19,000 allowance is released. The additional $24,000 above cost produces no carrying-value increase in this exercise.

West's corresponding estimate is $181,000 − $21,000 = $160,000. Against its $176,000 cost, the required allowance is $16,000. North's favorable estimate does not absorb West's required reduction.

| Property | Required closing allowance | Allowance movement | Closing net asset | Current valuation income effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| North Arcade | $0 | $19,000 decrease | $264,000 | $19,000 gain |
| West Depot | $16,000 | $16,000 increase | $160,000 | $16,000 loss |
| Total | $16,000 | $3,000 decrease | $424,000 | $3,000 net gain |

The control equation is $421,000 opening net assets + $19,000 reversal − $16,000 loss = $424,000 closing net assets. Independently, $440,000 total cost − $16,000 closing allowance gives the same answer.

The illustrative adjustments debit North's allowance and credit valuation gain $19,000; then debit valuation loss and credit West's allowance $16,000. Actual account mapping requires the bank's reporting instructions. These educational entries are not authority to post to a ledger.

A portfolio shortcut would compare $440,000 aggregate cost with $448,000 aggregate fair value less selling costs. That shortcut would incorrectly leave $440,000 on the balance sheet, overstating this register by $16,000. The property-level columns explain the difference.

## Keep holding cash flows outside property cost

During the quarter North earns and collects $9,600 of rent. Its ordinary holding expenses are property tax $4,200, insurance $900 and security $1,500, totaling $6,600. West incurs property tax $2,100, insurance $600 and security $1,800, totaling $4,500. All relate to this quarter, are paid immediately and meet none of the excluded development circumstances.

For these supplied facts, debit cash and credit rental income $9,600. Debit holding expenses and credit cash $11,100. Retain the expense categories in supporting records rather than presenting the $1,500 net outflow as an unexplained property movement. No depreciation is included for the stipulated held-for-sale properties.

Holding activity reduces income by $1,500. The $3,000 valuation gain then makes the combined pretax income effect positive $1,500. Cash nevertheless decreases $1,500. The $3,000 difference is entirely noncash allowance movement. None of the $35,000 estimated selling costs is a current-quarter cash payment.

## Change the evidence and rerun the affected column

If West's supported selling-cost estimate instead becomes $25,000, its net estimate falls to $156,000. Its allowance becomes $20,000 and the combined closing asset becomes $420,000. Relative to the main scenario, the additional $4,000 loss changes combined income from a $1,500 gain to a $2,500 loss; holding cash remains unchanged.

A different historical fact produces a different ceiling. Suppose valid records instead establish that North previously took a $19,000 direct write-off, leaving a $245,000 cost basis and no allowance. North's $288,000 closing estimate would not recreate the written-off amount. With West's main-scenario $160,000 closing value, total property assets would be $405,000. This alternative is not permission to reclassify history or ignore a suspected accounting error.

Before using either register, retain dated valuations, selling-cost support, opening-balance lineage and expense-period evidence. A spreadsheet can reconcile supplied numbers without proving those inputs or establishing the correct classification.

## Required intake
Specific issue, entities, separate framework periods, access to each authoritative source, known elections and transition facts.

## Execution plan
Analyze each framework independently before comparing. Do not infer IFRS conclusions from a U.S. GAAP guide or vice versa. Use columns for scope, recognition, measurement, presentation, disclosures, and transition only where supported. Mark an entire comparison cell unavailable when its primary support is missing rather than manufacturing symmetry.

## Expected deliverable
Two-framework evidence table; verified similarities/differences; applicability conditions; explicit source gaps.

## Stop or qualify the answer
The initial pack has no IFRS standard corpus and no verified IFRS requirements. This playbook specifies a future workflow, not an IFRS analysis.

## Build two analyses before making one comparison

Start with one stable factual packet: entity, transaction, dates, contracts, amounts, judgments already made and facts still unknown. Give it an immutable revision. Then create two independent authority/applicability records, one per framework and reporting period. A difference in the output may result from different facts, different permitted elections, different transition dates or different requirements; do not label every difference a framework difference.

For each issue, distinguish scope, unit of account, recognition, initial measurement, subsequent measurement, presentation, disclosure and transition. A source that supports recognition does not automatically support disclosure or the rate used in a calculation. Mark unsupported cells as unknown. A visually complete table can be less honest than one with explicit gaps.

## A fictional comparison exercise with deliberately invented policies

The following policies are teaching assumptions named Policy A and Policy B. They are not ASC or IFRS rules. Assume one USD 12,000 expenditure and no tax, impairment, residual value or partial-year timing. Policy A recognizes all 12,000 in the first period. Policy B allocates the amount evenly over three periods, 4,000 per period.

| Period | Policy A expense | Policy B expense | A minus B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12,000 | 4,000 | 8,000 |
| 2 | 0 | 4,000 | (4,000) |
| 3 | 0 | 4,000 | (4,000) |
| Total | 12,000 | 12,000 | 0 |

The first-period difference is 8,000, while total expense across the stipulated life is equal. The example demonstrates timing under the invented policies. It does not establish that either policy is permitted, that the expenditure is an asset, or which financial-statement lines would contain the amounts.

If one side of an actual comparison uses a different useful life, inception date or transaction population, first explain those factual differences. Otherwise the comparison could falsely attribute a fact mismatch to different standards. A reconciled cumulative total also does not establish identical presentation, disclosures or interim effects.

## Claims that require evidence from both sides

“Same treatment” requires support for the relevant dimensions in each framework. A missing source on one side is not evidence of silence or permission. “Framework B is more conservative” is too broad without a defined metric, population and time horizon. An isolated lower asset or higher first-year expense does not justify a universal characterization.

A proposed reconciliation entry should identify the originating difference, opening versus current-period effect, taxes if relevant, presentation and actual authority. Do not manufacture journal entries merely to make the two columns equal. Keep analytical reconciliation distinct from authorized posting and local statutory requirements.

## Decision-ready deliverable

For each potential difference, give the supported conclusion or abstention, exact source/version/locator, applicability conditions, common facts, elections, arithmetic, contrary evidence and unanswered question. Identify whether a conclusion would change if an uncertain fact changed. The reviewer should be able to trace a sentence to both independent analyses without trusting an ungrounded comparison summary.

This revision has no acquired IFRS standard corpus and does not verify any IFRS requirement. It deliberately adds original reasoning and a fictional policy exercise rather than presenting protected text or unsupported equivalences.

## Reviewer handoff
Each decisive difference needs valid support from both frameworks. Treat an unvalidated cross-framework conclusion as provisional until permitted sources, applicability analysis and responsible professional evaluation support its intended use.

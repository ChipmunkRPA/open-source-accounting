# Deferred tax assets: connect a future deduction to a realizability assessment

## The accounting idea

Book expense and tax deduction may occur in different periods. A future deduction can create a deferred tax asset, but its arithmetic measurement and its realizability are separate decisions. A recorded deferred tax asset does not mean the tax authority owes an immediately collectible refund.

OCC's August 2026 Bank Accounting Advisory Series, section 7A, explains deferred tax measurement using applicable enacted rates and consideration of positive and negative evidence. Its discussion is banking context; this article's warranty scenario is independently invented and does not assert that a specific tax jurisdiction permits the assumed deduction.

## Original worked example

Assume a company has recorded a $120,000 warranty liability and corresponding expense in its financial statements. For teaching purposes, its tax regime permits deduction only when claims are paid. Assume all of the amount will be deductible, the relevant enacted rate for reversal is 25%, and there are no uncertain tax positions, permanent differences or other deferred balances.

The deductible temporary difference is $120,000. Gross deferred tax asset: $120,000 × 25% = $30,000. The simplified initial entry is a $30,000 debit to deferred tax asset and a $30,000 credit to income-tax benefit.

Now stipulate that a completed, supportable realizability analysis concludes only $90,000 of those future deductions can be used. The supported tax benefit is $22,500. In this exercise, a $7,500 valuation allowance reduces the $30,000 gross asset to $22,500 net. The related charge offsets part of the original tax benefit.

The $90,000 is a conclusion supplied to the example, not a rule equating one budget year's profit to realizable deductions. In actual work, scheduling, carryback/carryforward rules, reversals, sources of taxable income and jurisdiction matter.

## A change in rate is not a change in the underlying liability

For a separate sensitivity, assume the applicable enacted reversal rate becomes 22%, while the $120,000 deductible difference and the supported $90,000 deduction remain unchanged. Gross deferred tax becomes $26,400, the valuation allowance becomes $6,600, and the net asset becomes $19,800. The warranty liability itself remains $120,000 in this calculation.

Do not use a proposed rate as though it were enacted. Do not describe the $2,700 decline in net deferred tax as a cash payment. The rate case is a separate stipulated exercise, not an assertion about any actual 2026 tax change.

## What the reviewer should challenge

Separate objectively supported future taxable income from growth targets. Reconcile opening and closing temporary differences to the underlying book balances. Show the gross deferred asset, valuation allowance and net presentation rather than hiding offsetting changes. Record the reversal years and legal taxpayer; profits earned by a different entity or in a different jurisdiction may not support use of this deduction.

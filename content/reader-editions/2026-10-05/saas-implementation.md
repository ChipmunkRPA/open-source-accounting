# SaaS implementation: investigate the output rather than rely on the fee label

**Reader edition 2026-10-05.1** · Wording changes are limited to labels and notices. Earlier check statements refer to the original article revision. No new professional review or source verification is claimed.

**Educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · Editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## The question to investigate

A fee called implementation does not explain what the customer obtains or when. Identify the actual activities, contractual promises, dependencies and customer-facing output. Whether those facts meet applicable accounting criteria remains an open primary-source research task; current ASC text was not accessed. [fasb-asc]

Do not turn either “implementation is always separate” or “implementation is always deferred” into a rule. This original article organizes the evidence needed to examine the question.

## Two fictional evidence packets

Packet A describes migration work producing a file the customer can retain and use in another environment. Packet B describes the supplier's internal account activation, with no separate output described beyond later platform access. These are exercise premises, not conclusions about any real service.

The packets justify different questions. For A, obtain the delivered file, usability evidence, dependencies, acceptance terms and complete agreement. For B, ask what the supplier actually promised, whether any other deliverable was omitted and how the fee relates to the remaining arrangement. A retained output or its absence in a short description is not a complete accounting test.

## Original invoice comparison

Both invented packets carry USD 60000 for stated annual access and USD 6000 labeled implementation. The common billed total is USD 66000. The fee is 10% of the access label and approximately 9.09% of the combined invoice.

Those two percentages describe denominators, not a materiality threshold or allocation. Equal billing does not establish equal economic substance; different operations do not by themselves select different accounting. No recognition schedule follows merely by dividing the combined bill over twelve months.

## Ask operations to describe the work without accounting labels

Distinguish migration, training, customization, internal configuration, integrations and other activities. Record who can use an output, when, in what environment and subject to what dependencies. Separate technical feasibility, contractual rights and current practice rather than treating them as interchangeable evidence.

Preserve records of platform access, delivery, failures, acceptance and amendments. A schedule saying a project is 100% complete does not by itself show what transferred to the customer. Likewise, hours spent or internal cost may describe effort without establishing the appropriate recognition pattern.

## Build a fact-sensitive conclusion

Use a matrix with the claimed activity, evidence, contrary evidence, accounting question, authorized primary source needed and the remaining decision. Identify the specific missing fact that could change the proposed analysis.

Example draft statement: “The available description does not establish the accounting unit or transfer pattern. Obtain the complete scope and delivery evidence, and verify the applicable criteria before selecting a treatment.” This is an honest research status, not an accounting answer disguised as a disclaimer.

## Review boundary

Request a chronology, complete-document questions, source-access gaps and alternative analyses. A billing label, separate invoice or operational interview cannot replace the actual applicable requirements. No current standard paragraph, real performance obligation or final revenue amount is approved here.

## Scope and the date of the evidence

This guide explains selected SEC disclosure requirements and creates original examples for a hypothetical domestic operating-company registrant. It does not determine a real company's filing status, exemptions, materiality, control effectiveness or legal obligations. For the Rule 13a-15 controls discussion, assume a class of securities registered under Exchange Act Section 12 and no relevant scope exception. A Section 15(d)-only issuer requires separate review of Rule 15d-15. Foreign private issuers, registered investment companies, asset-backed issuers and other special cases require their own scope analysis.

The official eCFR pages for Items 303, 307 and 308 and Exchange Act Rule 13a-15 were inspected on October 4, 2026. Their canonical pages displayed Title 17 as up to date through **October 1, 2026**. The displayed title-wide amendment date is not an amendment date for every provision. The eCFR is continuously updated but is not the official annual legal edition. No developments after the displayed date or all cross-referenced provisions have been reconciled here.

The library's April 1, 2025 annual CFR readings remain historical. Their wording, provenance and dates are not silently replaced by this newer research. A current reporting workpaper should identify which dated source supports each claim.

## Item 303: explain the business effects behind the numbers

The inspected Item 303 distinguishes annual and interim analysis. Its annual discussion addresses liquidity and capital resources, operating results and critical accounting estimates. Paragraph (b) calls for explaining underlying reasons for material changes, including offsetting changes inside a line item. Paragraph (b)(1) separates cash requirements over the next 12 months from longer-term requirements. Paragraph (b)(3) addresses significant estimation uncertainty with material effects, while the instructions require useful explanation rather than numerical repetition. Interim comparisons are separately specified in paragraph (c). [sec-item303-ecfr]

This is a selected source map, not the complete MD&A requirement or a determination that a particular change is material.

### Original example: a flat total can contain two moving parts

A fictional entity reports USD 1000000 revenue in both years. Its invented product detail is:

| Product | Prior year | Current year | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 400000 | 580000 | +180000 |
| B | 600000 | 420000 | -180000 |
| Total | 1000000 | 1000000 | 0 |

The aggregate change is zero. A increased 45%; B decreased 30%. The table does not explain whether those movements came from price, volume, mix, discontinued products or another cause. “Revenue was unchanged” is therefore an incomplete description of the exercise's data. Whether further disclosure is required depends on materiality and the actual facts, not on this example alone.

For a separate fictional cash-planning packet, list USD 90000 due within the next 12 months and USD 240000 after that horizon. Their total is USD 330000. Calling all of it “next year's funding need” changes the timing information. A responsible analysis would also need the actual obligation terms, available cash, funding sources and restrictions; the arithmetic cannot establish liquidity sufficiency.

## Items 307 and 308 ask different control questions

Item 307 concerns the principal executive and financial officers' conclusions on disclosure controls and procedures as of the report-period end, referring to the applicable Exchange Act evaluation rules. Rule 13a-15(e) describes a disclosure-information process, including getting relevant information to management in time for disclosure decisions. Rule 13a-15(f) separately defines ICFR around reliable financial reporting and external GAAP statements, including specified record, authorization and asset-protection processes. These definitions are related but not interchangeable. [sec-item307-ecfr; sec-rule13a15-ecfr]

For the covered domestic-issuer case, Rule 13a-15 distinguishes quarterly disclosure-controls evaluation, annual ICFR evaluation and evaluation of relevant ICFR changes. Its scope and exceptions still must be checked for the actual issuer. An annual ICFR conclusion does not automatically supply all of the other disclosures. [sec-rule13a15-ecfr, paragraphs (a)–(d)]

Item 308 separates management's annual ICFR report, the applicable auditor-attestation report, and disclosure of relevant quarterly ICFR changes. Paragraph (a)(3) prevents an effective-ICFR conclusion where a material weakness exists. Paragraph (b) has filer-status and emerging-growth-company conditions; Instruction 1 provides a newly public company transition, and Instruction 2 requires support for management's assessment. An auditor-attestation exemption does not by itself erase management's separate reporting analysis. [sec-item308-ecfr]

## Build a scope matrix before drafting conclusions

For the actual report, record the entity, form, fiscal period, first reporting year, filer-status analysis and applicable exceptions. Then assign evidence to each distinct question:

| Question | Starting source | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Does MD&A explain the relevant changes and uncertainty? | Item 303 | Underlying drivers, appropriate comparisons, cash horizons and estimate sensitivity |
| What disclosure-controls conclusion is required? | Item 307 and applicable evaluation rule | Defined process, period-end assessment, participation and support |
| What is management's annual ICFR conclusion? | Item 308(a) | Framework, assessed date, deficiencies, supporting evidence and actual conclusion |
| Is an auditor ICFR attestation required here? | Item 308(b) | Current filer/EGC status, applicable definitions and any transition |
| What changed in ICFR during the relevant period? | Item 308(c) | Actual changes and their evaluated effects |

The matrix is an original research aid, not a substitute for the cited requirements. Do not mark an exemption simply because an issuer is “small”; check the governing definitions and timing. The guide has not independently verified Rule 12b-2 or every alternative rule referenced by these Items.

## Original chronology: one fact can raise several questions

Assume a fictional company moves to a new revenue system on September 10 and approves a reconciliation redesign on September 25. Its report period ends September 30. The first documented execution of the redesigned process is October 5.

Those dates support a chronology. They do not prove that the system change was material, that a control operated before quarter-end, or that a weakness was remediated. The same events might affect the MD&A fact-gathering process, disclosure-controls assessment and ICFR-change analysis, but each conclusion requires its own evidence and criteria. Later evidence may inform an earlier-date assessment; preserve its true date and evaluate its relevance rather than backdating it or dismissing it automatically.

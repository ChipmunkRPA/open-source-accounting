# Startup costs: separate launch spending from the equipment rollforward

## The cost boundary

OCC staff commentary expenses startup and organization activities as incurred, distinguishes fixed-asset acquisition and readiness costs, and treats depreciation assigned to startup use as expense. Its cited FASB topic is ASC 720-15. [OCC BAAS, Topic 9C, questions 1–2](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/banker-education/files/pub-bank-accounting-advisory-series.pdf#page=150).

The practical question is what each cost bought. An “opening budget” label cannot classify every invoice. This original exercise supplies accounting conclusions, then follows them through journals, depreciation and a closing balance sheet. The fictional register and variations were created independently of source scenarios.

## An invented June cost register

Fictional Harborside Bank supplies opening balances of $100,000 cash and $100,000 equity, with no other balances. Their origin is outside the exercise. All listed services are received and obligations incurred by June 30. There are no prepaid services or unsettled estimates. Amounts are dollars.

| ID | Delivered item or activity | Supplied classification | Amount | Settlement at June 30 |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| S1 | Preopening workflow rehearsal | Startup expense | 7,200 | Paid |
| S2 | Launch procedure orientation | Startup expense | 4,800 | Paid |
| S3 | Trial customer routing exercise | Startup expense | 1,800 | Payable |
| S4 | Initial governing document work | Organization expense | 3,200 | Payable |
| E1 | Physical document finishing machine | Equipment cost | 42,000 | Paid |
| E2 | Machine alignment and acceptance work | Equipment readiness cost | 6,000 | Paid |
| E3 | Required physical mounting adapter | Equipment readiness cost | 1,200 | Payable |

E2 and E3 are stipulated necessary physical readiness costs; the labels alone do not establish classification. S4 is this bank's own stipulated organization activity, with no reimbursement arrangement.

S1 through S4 total $17,000 of expense. E1 through E3 total $49,200 of equipment cost. Total incurred costs are $66,200, of which $60,000 has been paid and $6,200 remains payable. Unpaid status does not move a received startup service into a later period in this supplied case.

## Record the costs and their settlement

The following journals separate classification from payment. Debit and credit amounts match within every numbered entry.

1. Record paid startup activities: debit startup expense $12,000; credit cash $12,000
2. Record unpaid activities: debit startup expense $1,800 and organization expense $3,200; credit accounts payable $5,000
3. Record paid equipment costs: debit equipment $48,000; credit cash $48,000
4. Record the unpaid adapter: debit equipment $1,200; credit accounts payable $1,200

After these entries, equipment is $49,200, expense is $17,000, cash is $40,000, and accounts payable is $6,200. The register reconciles independently to both the asset ledger and vendor balances. Keeping invoice IDs visible also prevents recording an unpaid invoice again as a new expense when its cash payment arrives.

Paying the $6,200 immediately after this close would debit accounts payable and credit cash by $6,200. That isolated settlement reduces cash to $33,800 and payables to zero without changing the $17,000 already recognized. Subsequent operations are not modeled.

## Include the equipment used before opening

Assume the machine was ready and available for its intended use on June 1 and used in startup work throughout June. The supplied depreciation policy is straight-line over 48 months with $1,200 residual value and one full month's charge in June. These are exercise assumptions, not useful-life or commencement-date conclusions obtained from the source.

Depreciable cost is $49,200 less $1,200, or $48,000. Monthly depreciation is $48,000 divided by 48, or $1,000. Debit startup depreciation expense $1,000 and credit accumulated depreciation $1,000. Using the machine for preparation does not, under the narrow premise, create a second deferred startup asset out of that charge.

June's complete expense is $18,000: $17,000 of activities plus $1,000 depreciation. Closing net equipment is $48,200. The balance sheet reconciles as cash $40,000 plus net equipment $48,200 equals assets $88,200; payables $6,200 plus equity $82,000 also equals $88,200. Equity is the supplied opening $100,000 less the modeled $18,000 loss. No revenue or other transactions are assumed.

## Two independent sensitivity checks

First, suppose revised evidence establishes that $1,200 within E2 actually purchased another workflow rehearsal. All other assumptions remain unchanged, including payment, readiness date, residual value and useful life. Equipment cost becomes $48,000 and activity expense $18,200. Depreciation becomes ($48,000 less $1,200) divided by 48, or $975. Total June expense becomes $19,175, and net equipment $47,025. Assets are $87,025; liabilities $6,200 plus equity $80,825 equal that amount. The classification change reduces earnings and net assets by $1,175 after the $25 depreciation reduction.

Second, independently retain the original register but assume the machine remains in preparation throughout June, becomes ready on July 1, and carries no June depreciation under the supplied policy. June expense is $17,000, equipment in preparation $49,200, and assets $89,200. Payables $6,200 plus equity $83,000 reconcile. A change in readiness evidence changes this stipulated depreciation calculation; it does not erase the startup services already consumed.

## Evidence and limits

Match invoices to delivered work, identify the responsible entity, document cutoff, and retain equipment acceptance evidence. For mixed invoices, obtain a supported allocation rather than dividing by a convenient percentage. Have the reviewer document classification separately from useful life, residual value and the ready-for-use date. Balanced journals demonstrate arithmetic, not those judgments.

Debt or equity issuance, business acquisitions, software, leases, entity reimbursements and taxes are excluded. This is not a comprehensive capitalization policy or authority to post entries.

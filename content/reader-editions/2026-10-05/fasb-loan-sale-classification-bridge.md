# Loan sale plans: reconcile the credit allowance and the held-for-sale valuation account

## Read the account labels before adding the adjustments

A loan's expected-credit-loss allowance and its held-for-sale valuation account can both reduce a displayed asset, but a transfer workpaper should not treat them as interchangeable balances. Start with the gross basis and identify which adjustment belongs to the current classification.

For the supported noncredit case selected here, OCC reverses the existing credit allowance at transfer to held for sale, then applies the lower-of-cost-or-fair-value measurement using a valuation allowance. On a later return to held for investment, the valuation allowance is reversed and credit losses are assessed separately. [OCC Topic 2E, questions 10, 11 and 13](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/banker-education/files/pub-bank-accounting-advisory-series.pdf#page=55).

## Supply the decisions that arithmetic cannot make

A fictional bank owns two ordinary nonmortgage loans, River and Summit. Both began as held for investment. Qualified analysis is assumed to have established the specific sale decisions and the accounting eligibility of a later return to held for investment. No fair-value-option election applies.

Objective, verifiable documentation is stipulated to show that the sale-date market declines arose solely from changes in interest rates, with no credit-quality deterioration causing those declines. This is an important supplied premise, not a conclusion inferred from the names of the accounts. The alternative credit-driven regulatory charge-off path is excluded.

Separate credit estimates can exist even when the observed market-price change is interest-rate-driven. The existing allowances below are supplied balances, not estimates derived from the sale prices. Later credit estimates also come from an independent workpaper. Ignore interest accruals, fees, principal payments, foreign currency, transaction costs and tax. There is no sale, derecognition conclusion or cash flow in the illustrated transfer entries.

All amounts are dollars. Each loan is measured separately in this exercise; no offset between loans is assumed.

| Loan | Amortized cost | Existing credit allowance | Opening net balance | Sale-date fair value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| River | $1,350,000 | $27,000 | $1,323,000 | $1,296,000 |
| Summit | $820,000 | $12,300 | $807,700 | $779,000 |
| Total | $2,170,000 | $39,300 | $2,130,700 | $2,075,000 |

## Reconcile the transfer in three separate entries

First remove the existing $39,300 credit allowance: debit that allowance and credit credit-loss expense. This releases a credit balance; it does not collect cash or increase contractual principal.

Second reclassify the unchanged $2,170,000 gross basis: debit loans held for sale and credit loans held for investment. This entry alone changes neither total assets nor earnings.

Third establish the held-for-sale valuation allowances. River needs $54,000, calculated as $1,350,000 minus $1,296,000. Summit needs $41,000. Debit other noninterest valuation expense $95,000 and credit the separate held-for-sale valuation account $95,000.

The result is $2,170,000 gross less $95,000 valuation allowance, or $2,075,000 net. The current earnings reduction is $55,700: $95,000 expense less the $39,300 credit-loss release. The asset bridge is the same $2,130,700 minus $55,700.

A shortcut that merely relabels the old $39,300 allowance leaves the valuation balance $55,700 too small. A different shortcut that adds $95,000 while leaving the old allowance attached understates net assets by $39,300. Gross basis, account identity and income effects all need reconciliation.

## Update the price without treating the cost ceiling as a target gain

At the next measurement date, while both loans remain held for sale, their stipulated fair values are $1,323,000 and $803,600. Costs remain unchanged.

| Loan | Required valuation allowance | Release from first measurement | Updated net asset |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| River | $27,000 | $27,000 | $1,323,000 |
| Summit | $16,400 | $24,600 | $803,600 |
| Total | $43,400 | $51,600 | $2,126,600 |

Debit the valuation allowance $51,600 and credit valuation expense $51,600. This example's release is noncash and only reduces a previously recorded valuation adjustment.

As an independent alternative at this same date, suppose River's fair value were $1,377,000. Its required allowance would be zero and its net balance would be $1,350,000. The $27,000 excess of price over cost would not become an additional asset under this stipulated lower-of-cost model. Summit's result would be unchanged. This alternative is not an additional chronological entry in the main case.

## Return one loan to a different measurement path

After the second measurement, Summit alone returns to held for investment. The appropriateness and documentation of that decision are supplied, rather than assumed from management wishing to remove a valuation loss. Its amortized cost remains $820,000.

Reverse Summit's $16,400 held-for-sale allowance by debiting the allowance and crediting valuation expense. Reclassify the $820,000 gross asset to held for investment. A separate, current credit analysis supplies a required $18,040 allowance: debit credit-loss expense and credit that allowance. The new net balance is $801,960.

The net earnings reduction for this transfer back is $1,640: $18,040 less $16,400. Recording only that net amount would hide two different models and leave an unclear allowance history. River retains its $27,000 held-for-sale valuation balance and $1,323,000 net asset.

Combined closing net assets are $2,124,960. The complete earnings bridge is a $55,700 reduction, a $51,600 recovery, and a $1,640 reduction, totaling a $5,740 reduction. Opening net assets of $2,130,700 less $5,740 equal the closing total. Gross loans remain $2,170,000 throughout; closing contra balances are $27,000 plus $18,040, or $45,040.

## Source boundary and review file

Retain dated sale and return decisions, evidence for the interest-rate-only decline, independent credit estimates, gross-basis reconciliation and each allowance rollforward. Matching totals cannot establish the classification, substantiate the market evidence or replace disclosure research.

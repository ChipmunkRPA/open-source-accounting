# ESG evidence: define the claim before choosing a metric

> **AI-assisted editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Original educational material. No standard text or real emissions factors are reproduced. No compliance, materiality, assurance or accounting conclusion is established.

## Start with a proposed claim

For each proposed disclosure, write the entity, reporting period, intended audience, jurisdiction, asserted reporting framework and exact claim. Create separate questions for environmental, social and governance subjects. A precise number is not proof that the chosen subject or boundary is complete.

Example: fictional Juniper Works proposes “our environmental performance improved.” Its worksheet measures only electricity at one site. Rewrite the working claim as “electricity-related illustrative calculation for Site A,” pending boundary and evidence review. Do not generalize to water, waste, biodiversity, workforce or governance performance.

## Evidence and decisions

Maintain separate records for (1) governing criteria and their legal adoption, (2) the organization's facts, (3) measurement methods/factors, (4) judgments and estimates, and (5) approvals. Record why each facility, workforce group or value-chain activity is included or excluded; do not infer a reporting boundary from the financial consolidation list alone.

For a workforce metric, ask whether the population includes employees, contractors, leavers and acquired operations, and whether the count is an average, period-end headcount or full-time equivalent. For a governance metric, define the committee, membership period and the event being counted. Never add headcount to full-time equivalents or infer operating effectiveness from a meeting count. These are original data-quality questions, not paraphrased disclosure requirements.

## Measurement and reporting are separate decisions

A working calculation can be arithmetically correct while its factor is geographically inappropriate, outdated or unlicensed. Preserve factor identifier, version, units, gas coverage, geography, period, source hash/locator and permission. A value labeled CO2 must not be silently relabeled CO2-equivalent. A new factor version may change comparisons; show an explicit bridge before attributing movement to operations.

Financial materiality, impact assessment and jurisdiction-specific reporting decisions need their own criteria and evidence. Do not reuse one materiality result as permission to omit a subject under another framework. Link proposed financial effects to a separate accounting issue file with supported recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure analysis. An emissions quantity is not automatically an expense, liability, provision or journal entry.

## Exceptions and historical comparisons

An acquisition, disposal, missing meter, supplier estimate or methodology change is a decision to investigate, not a zero. Record both reported-period and comparison-period definitions. Distinguish a proposed adjustment from an accepted correction and retain both revisions. State whether a comparison is like-for-like; if not, explain the unresolved difference instead of inventing a restatement policy.

A reviewer should resolve applicable criteria, exact edition/transition, method suitability, completeness and uncertainty before approving a claim. Source-use authorization is separate. ## Worked claim review: a narrower number needs narrower wording

Assume Juniper measures electricity at two operating sites, using only invented teaching factors. A third acquired site has no usable meter data. The preparer drafts: “Group emissions fell 12% and the business is on track.” The file actually compares two sites, excludes the acquisition, and contains no approved target trajectory.

Break that sentence into separately testable claims: the population is the group; the quantity is emissions; the decline is 12%; the periods are comparable; and progress is sufficient for a target. Evidence for one part cannot silently supply the others. A valid calculation on two sites may support a clearly bounded observation while failing to establish group-wide change or target progress.

A defensible fictional working sentence is: “The two-site calculation decreased under the stated method; the acquired site's missing data and the target assessment remain unresolved.” If even the two-site method changed, qualify that comparison too. Do not convert the missing site to zero, infer a legal exemption, or erase it from the boundary register to complete the claim.

## Three original population examples

### Workforce headcount and full-time equivalents

Assume one unit has 80 full-time employees and 20 half-time employees at a specified cutoff. Headcount is **80 + 20 = 100 people**. Under the supplied convention that each half-time employee represents 0.5 full-time equivalent, FTE is **80 + 20 × 0.5 = 90**. These are different quantities. Dividing a count of employees who completed training by 90 FTE would mix units unless a separately justified metric explicitly uses that basis.

If 72 employees completed the stated course, the headcount-based fraction is **72/100 = 72%**. A result of **72/90 = 80%** would not describe the percentage of employees trained. Course attendance, required curriculum coverage and effectiveness are also different claims. No personal records are included in this example.

### Governance meeting counts

Suppose a committee meets four times, but only two meetings include the requested risk topic. “Four meetings held” and “two meetings addressed the topic” can both be true. Neither proves that the committee's oversight was effective. Obtain what was considered, decisions made, unresolved matters and evidence of follow-through before adding an effectiveness claim. Do not infer independent membership solely from a committee title.

### Environmental activity versus purchased evidence

A certificate or supplier statement may concern a different entity, period, quantity or attribute from the measured activity. Preserve its identity and the proposed claim separately. This guide does not determine whether a certificate can be used, retired, transferred or netted under any reporting method. Matching a certificate's number to an activity total does not resolve the criteria or prevent double counting elsewhere.

## A useful review sequence

First define the exact noun and verb in the claim. “Consumed,” “purchased,” “produced,” “avoided,” “reduced,” “estimated,” and “verified” are not interchangeable. Then identify the population, time basis, units, method version and evidence for the comparison. Finally distinguish what happened from why it happened and what is forecast to happen.

If the calculation changes after review, link the revised result to every affected sentence. Retain the original result and the reason for revision. A corrected denominator can change a percentage without a new operational event; a change in a factor can change a calculated quantity without a change in activity. Explain the change before labeling performance better or worse.

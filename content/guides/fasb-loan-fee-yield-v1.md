# Loan origination fees: distinguish cash collected from interest earned

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. No professional review recorded; the brand does not mean Ray Sang personally reviewed this article.

## The accounting idea

A fee paid on the day a loan is made may be part of the lender's yield rather than immediately earned fee income. Which costs qualify for deferral must be assessed separately. OCC's August 2026 Bank Accounting Advisory Series, section 2D, discusses origination fees and costs under ASC 310-20. Its bank-specific staff interpretations are useful context, not a replacement for the Codification.

## Original worked example

Imagine a lender originates a one-year bullet loan with these stipulated facts:

- Contractual principal advanced: $80,000
- Contractual interest payable at maturity: $4,800
- Origination fee collected immediately: $1,600
- Direct origination costs paid immediately: $400, assumed to qualify for deferral
- No prepayment, impairment, modification, ongoing servicing component or other fee

The net deferred fee is $1,200. The initial net carrying amount is $80,000 − $1,600 + $400 = $78,800. The maturity collection is $84,800. Across the year, the lender's interest income in this deliberately simple effective-yield illustration is $6,000: the $4,800 contractual coupon plus $1,200 fee accretion.

The annual effective yield is $6,000 ÷ $78,800 = approximately 7.6142%. The cash coupon rate remains 6% of contractual principal. The two percentages use different denominators and answer different questions.

At origination, the loan's legal principal remains $80,000; the deferred net fee is a carrying-value adjustment. At maturity, before receipt, the net loan amount plus accrued interest totals $84,800. Receipt then clears those balances. This does not imply that interim interest may be recognized only at maturity.

## Three variations that change the analysis

First, suppose $250 of the stated $400 costs is general advertising rather than a qualifying direct origination cost. The example's $1,200 net fee can no longer be used unchanged. Resolve qualification before recalculating yield.

Second, if principal is repaid monthly, place each cash flow on its contractual payment date and solve for the effective yield. Dividing the total fee evenly across months is not automatically the effective-interest result. A spreadsheet should reconcile each opening carrying amount, effective interest, cash received and closing carrying amount.

Third, an early payoff or restructuring can change the remaining accounting. Do not mechanically keep a deferred balance on a loan that no longer exists or call a renewal a new loan based only on its new document date.

## Practical handoff

Give the reviewer the signed terms, fee schedule, cost-support file, principal-versus-carrying-value bridge and dated cash flows. A computation may be perfectly reproducible while the fee's classification is wrong. The classification decision must therefore be visible outside the formula.

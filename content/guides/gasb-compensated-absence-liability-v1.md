# Compensated absences: measure earned leave at the reporting date

## Earned time can create an obligation before payment

An unused leave balance is a count of hours. A financial-statement liability is a measured obligation. Moving from one to the other requires decisions about which hours qualify, how employees are expected to use or settle them, and what compensation and incremental employer costs belong in the amount.

Texas Comptroller application guidance addresses leave earned through past service, accumulation and expected settlement. It also addresses leave already used but not paid. This example concentrates on qualifying annual leave. It does not adopt Texas sick-leave assumptions or treat every balance in a payroll system as automatically recognizable.

## Original scenario: three annual-leave groups

A fictional city's parks department prepares its December 31, 2026 close. Use the government-wide economic-resources perspective first. Assume management has already supported the recognition conditions and expected settlement for these unused annual-leave hours:

- Field crews: 160 qualifying hours at a reporting-date rate of $32 per hour
- Technical staff: 90 qualifying hours at $48 per hour
- Supervisors: 50 qualifying hours at $60 per hour

These are the qualifying hours supported by the leave analysis, not necessarily every hour appearing in the personnel system. No hour is included in more than one group. Assume all qualifying hours settle at the stated rates and there are no special leave categories, pension conversions or employee-specific limits affecting measurement.

The unused-leave wage amount is:

- Field crews: 160 × $32 = $5,120
- Technical staff: 90 × $48 = $4,320
- Supervisors: 50 × $60 = $3,000
- Total: $12,440

For this invented workforce, assume documented incremental employer costs equal 5% of those amounts. The rate is a scenario input, not a payroll-tax rule. Additional costs are $622, producing an unused-leave liability of $13,062.

A separate payroll cutoff report identifies $900 of qualifying leave already used but still unpaid. Those hours were removed from the unused-leave register. Their incremental employer cost is $45 under the same assumption. Add this $945 once, giving a total closing liability of $14,007.

## Reconcile the liability movement

Assume the opening recognized leave liability was $9,800. During the year, $7,600 of previously recognized or newly accrued leave obligations, including their associated employer costs, was settled in cash. There were no opening-balance corrections, acquisitions, transfers or other nonexpense movements.

The required service-and-measurement addition is $11,807:

$14,007 closing liability − $9,800 opening liability + $7,600 settlements = $11,807.

The roll-forward is therefore $9,800 + $11,807 − $7,600 = $14,007. Under the stated assumptions, $11,807 is the leave-related expense for the period. An aggregate illustration debits expense and credits the leave liability for that addition, then debits the liability and credits cash for settlements.

The arithmetic is a reconciliation, not independent evidence that the expense is correct. The leave register must explain the addition through earned benefits, changes in qualifying hours and measurement changes. Also reconcile payroll: if a settlement was initially charged to wage expense, the accounting system needs the appropriate reclassification rather than recording a second expense or a second cash payment.

## Governmental-fund recognition is a separate step

For a separate fund-level illustration, assume the $945 used-but-unpaid amount qualifies as a recognized current expenditure and payable. Assume none of the $13,062 unused-leave obligation qualifies for governmental-fund liability recognition at this date. These are explicit case conclusions, not a rule that all unused leave is excluded everywhere.

The closing leave-related payable in the fund is then $945, compared with the $14,007 economic-resources liability. The $13,062 difference requires explanation in the applicable conversion work. Forecasting that some leave will be used next year does not, by itself, establish this year's governmental-fund recognition. Similarly, a current/noncurrent split in a statement of net position is not a substitute for the fund-level analysis.

## Changed fact: one reporting-date pay rate increases

Suppose the correct supervisor rate at December 31 is $64 rather than $60. No hours or other rates change. The unused-leave wage amount increases by 50 × $4 = $200; associated costs increase by $10. The closing liability becomes $14,217 and the reconciled expense becomes $12,017.

This $210 change is a measurement consequence of the supplied current rate. It is not an extra 50 hours of leave. Preserve that distinction when explaining the movement to someone reviewing staffing costs.

# PPP expense research: replace an obsolete conclusion without erasing the evidence trail

**Ray Sang’s Annotation**

**Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. Professionally unreviewed; no human review is recorded, and the label does not imply that Ray Sang personally reviewed this work. This guide explains the historical change described in a retained 2021 IRS ruling. It is not current PPP, refund, return-amendment or state-tax advice.

## A citation can be genuine and still support an obsolete conclusion

Revenue Ruling 2021-2 expressly obsoletes Notice 2020-32 and Revenue Ruling 2020-27 because of a statutory amendment. The earlier materials had disallowed certain otherwise deductible expenses when their payment resulted, or was expected to result, in covered PPP loan forgiveness. The later ruling says those conclusions no longer accurately state the law following the amendment. [Retained ruling, PDF pages 1–2](https://github.com/ChipmunkRPA/open-source-accounting/blob/38daa93e71a5287a6b372ac70af7f82122f53f73/content/irs-source-pilot/2026-10-04/originals/rr-21-02.pdf#page=1).

This makes a useful research-control example. A workpaper does not become reliable merely because each footnote opens successfully. The researcher must ask whether a later authority changes the proposition, which taxable years it affects, and whether other conditions remain unsatisfied.

## Keep enactment, applicability and publication separate

The retained ruling identifies these distinct events:

| Event | Date or period described in the retained source | What it establishes here |
|---|---|---|
| CARES Act enactment | March 27, 2020 | Historical starting point for the statutory discussion |
| Notice 2020-32 citation | May 18, 2020, in the cited Internal Revenue Bulletin | Location/date of the earlier cited IRS guidance, not a newly verified issuance date |
| Revenue Ruling 2020-27 citation | December 7, 2020, in the cited Internal Revenue Bulletin | Location/date of the second earlier cited item |
| Amending legislation enactment | December 27, 2020 | Statutory change described by Revenue Ruling 2021-2 |
| Amendment applicability | Taxable years ending after March 27, 2020 | The period test stated in the ruling |

The amendment’s taxable-year boundary is not the date when an accountant first reads the 2021 ruling. Nor should it be replaced with a test based solely on the loan date, expense-payment date or forgiveness date. Those dates may matter to other questions, but they are not the period test stated here. [PDF page 2](https://github.com/ChipmunkRPA/open-source-accounting/blob/38daa93e71a5287a6b372ac70af7f82122f53f73/content/irs-source-pilot/2026-10-04/originals/rr-21-02.pdf#page=2).

For a literal boundary check, a taxable year ending March 27 does not satisfy “after”; a taxable year ending March 28 does. This is only a reading of that date condition. It does not establish that either hypothetical taxpayer otherwise qualifies for PPP forgiveness or for a deduction.

## Separate the exclusion from the deduction

The ruling describes the statutory exclusion of covered forgiveness from gross income and the amendment’s protection against denying deductions, reducing tax attributes or denying basis increases because of that exclusion. The causal limitation matters: removing one reason to deny a deduction does not prove all other requirements are met. [PDF pages 1–2](https://github.com/ChipmunkRPA/open-source-accounting/blob/38daa93e71a5287a6b372ac70af7f82122f53f73/content/irs-source-pilot/2026-10-04/originals/rr-21-02.pdf#page=1).

An expense might still have an unsupported business purpose, an incorrect period, a capitalization issue or another limitation. This guide does not resolve those questions. Similarly, protection concerning attributes and basis is not a universal entity-level basis computation. Keep each issue in its own row instead of turning the amendment into a blanket “all PPP costs deductible” checkbox.

## Synthetic case: revise the issue log, not the historical document

A fictional calendar-year company has a 2020 workpaper identifying $96,000 of costs associated with a covered loan. Separately assume that $96,000 of covered loan principal is forgiven and that the loan and forgiveness fall within the retained ruling’s described statutory scope; the cost total alone does not establish the forgiven amount. A preliminary memo proposed disallowing the full $96,000 solely because the loan was expected to be forgiven, relying on the earlier guidance.

For the revised educational exercise, divide the costs into three groups:

- $70,000 has independently established ordinary deductibility and correct timing under stipulated assumptions
- $18,000 has the same separately established support
- $8,000 remains unresolved because the evidence needed for its independent tax treatment has not been supplied

After applying the historical change described by Revenue Ruling 2021-2, the PPP-exclusion-only rationale no longer supports disallowing the first $88,000. The unresolved $8,000 remains unresolved for its separate reason. The resulting evidence classification is $88,000 supported on the stipulated independent grounds and $8,000 requiring further work, not $96,000 automatically approved.

This distinction prevents two opposite mistakes. Retaining the preliminary full disallowance ignores the later statutory change. Approving all $96,000 treats removal of that disallowance rationale as proof of unrelated requirements. The correct revision records which conclusion changed, why it changed, and which question did not change.

## A bounded arithmetic bridge

Assume, solely for illustration, $240,000 of income before these costs and before any forgiveness amount, with no other adjustments. Under the stale memo’s full-disallowance approach, the simplified amount remains $240,000. Under the supported-cost-only exercise, subtracting $88,000 yields $152,000, with the $8,000 issue still open. If later evidence independently supports that remaining cost, the corresponding amount becomes $144,000.

The $96,000 forgiveness amount is not added as gross income under the stipulated covered-forgiveness exclusion. That exclusion and the expense analysis are separate steps; do not deduct the loan forgiveness itself as an additional expense. These figures are scenario arithmetic, not a completed taxable-income or tax-liability calculation. No tax rate, refund amount, journal entry or return position is determined.

## Version the conclusion and preserve its basis

A useful revision record contains the old proposition; its cited authority; the later document; the specific change; enactment and applicability dates; the taxpayer facts used; the revised conclusion; and unresolved issues. Preserve the earlier memo as a historical record while marking that its PPP-only disallowance conclusion was superseded for the scoped analysis.

Do not silently replace its text and leave the old approval attached. A prior review of an earlier conclusion does not become review of the new one. In this batch neither version has a human professional approval. An automated source-status field also does not authorize filing an amended return or contacting a taxing authority.

## Questions that require abstention

Would the same expense treatment necessarily apply on a state return? This federal source does not answer state conformity. Is the company eligible for forgiveness? That is an assumed fact here, not an outcome of reading this ruling. Is a refund claim timely today? The retained source does not supply that procedural analysis. Were the $8,000 costs ordinary, necessary and correctly timed? More evidence is needed.

The operational lesson is to locate the changed proposition, not merely search for the word “obsolete.” A later document may change only one conclusion while leaving adjacent issues untouched. This particular ruling expressly names both earlier IRS items; those predecessor texts were not independently acquired or reviewed in this annotation batch.

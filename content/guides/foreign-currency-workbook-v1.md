# Foreign currency: separate measurement, translation, and rate policy

**AI-assisted editorial draft · v1.0.0 · 2026-10-04 · No professional review recorded.**

Create a currency map before explaining exchange differences or proposing reclassification entries.

## Scope and authority map

Candidate research starting point: **ASC 830**. This is not a complete applicability determination. Confirm the entity, reporting period, adoption status, and current primary text before relying on a rule. The platform has not reproduced the Codification or approved this article as Agent evidence.

## Intake and missing facts

Identify each entity, its local books currency, asserted functional currency, presentation currency, transaction currencies, and consolidation path. Obtain transaction dates, settlement dates, rate sources, historical-rate records, monetary/nonmonetary classifications proposed by management, and changes in the economic environment. Record whether a proposed functional-currency change has actually been approved.

## Reconciliation and evidence controls

Reconcile transaction-level gains and losses separately from translation adjustments. Establish the direction of every rate quotation and the units used in multiplication or division. Compare opening balances, activity, closing balances, settlements, and rates. A residual translation balance should not become a convenient account for every unexplained difference.

## Analysis workstream

Research the appropriate measurement basis for each balance and the conditions for using approximations such as average rates. A month's average is not automatically interchangeable with a transaction-date rate, especially when exchange rates vary materially or transactions are concentrated. Treat long-term intercompany balances, hedges, acquisitions, and highly inflationary environments as separate analysis branches rather than extensions of a simple payable example.

## A failure mode to test

An expense booked on the last day of a month can still represent an underlying pattern of activity that requires investigation. The accounting policy cannot be inferred from a system's default rate table. Similarly, changing an ERP rate type is a configuration action whose accounting effects need testing; it is not itself an accounting conclusion.

## Worked rate-direction example

Assume a fictional invoice is EUR 40,000 and an applicable teaching rate is USD 1.25 per EUR. The USD conversion is **40,000 × 1.25 = 50,000**. The exact reciprocal quotation is **EUR 0.80 per USD**; using that quotation, the same result is **40,000 ÷ 0.80 = 50,000**. Multiplying by 0.80 would produce 32,000 with the wrong unit operation.

Write the dimensions before using a rate. EUR multiplied by USD/EUR leaves USD; EUR divided by EUR/USD also leaves USD. A number without its currency pair and direction is incomplete input. If a source supplies a rounded reciprocal, small differences may result; preserve the actual quote precision and rounding policy instead of forcing an exact reciprocal match.

The example does not choose which rate is appropriate under ASC 830 or another framework. It only shows how to apply a fully specified hypothetical quote. A current website rate is not automatically the rate for an earlier transaction or closing date.

## A simple monthly average can misstate a concentrated population

Assume two fictional transaction groups: EUR 10,000 at USD 1.10/EUR and EUR 90,000 at USD 1.30/EUR. Their individually converted total is **11,000 + 117,000 = USD 128,000**. The simple average of the two quoted rates is 1.20, which would give **USD 120,000** for the EUR 100,000 total, a difference of **USD 8,000**.

The amount-weighted rate for this particular supplied population is **128,000/100,000 = 1.28 USD/EUR**. That result is a mathematical description of these transactions, not evidence that a weighted-average policy is allowed or appropriate for another balance. It does not settle whether amounts are monetary, historical-rate, revenue, expense or translation items.

A reviewer should ask what an average is averaging: calendar days, observations, transaction amounts or another weighting. Two identical labels such as “monthly average” may encode different methods. Missing days, weekends, currency holidays, stale quotes, rate-provider revisions and time cutoffs can also affect the series. Do not infer the policy from the spreadsheet's function name.

## Keep three reconciliations separate

A transaction-currency rollforward answers whether the EUR or other original-currency balance is complete. A conversion rollforward answers how the stipulated rate basis turns that balance into the measurement currency. A consolidation or presentation analysis asks a different question about combining and presenting entities. Do not use a residual in the third to conceal a missing transaction in the first.

For example, opening EUR 60,000 plus invoices EUR 70,000 less settlements EUR 30,000 equals closing **EUR 100,000**. If the closing foreign-currency ledger shows EUR 98,000, the **EUR 2,000** discrepancy exists before any rate is chosen. A more precise exchange rate cannot repair the missing transaction or scope difference. Resolve the original-currency population first, then apply the supported measurement method.

## Exceptions needing their own evidence

A bank fee, discount, withholding, credit note, hedge, intercompany balance or change in asserted functional currency can create movements not explained by a single rate difference. Identify each event and its actual terms. Do not label all residuals “FX,” translate a fee using the invoice's rate without support, or infer hedge accounting merely because a derivative exists. These are research branches, not conclusions about the applicable accounting rules.

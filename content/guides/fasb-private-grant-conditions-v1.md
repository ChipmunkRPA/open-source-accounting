# Private grants received by banks: separate the receipt date from the recognition condition

**Ray Sang’s Annotation**

**Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

## Bound the recipient and the transaction

This guide concerns a nongovernmental donor's grant received by a bank. It is not a nonprofit donor-restriction or restriction-release model. It also excludes governmental grants, whose treatment has separate adoption-status branches in the selected OCC publication.

For the selected bank guidance, cash received subject to donor-imposed conditions remains deferred until those conditions are substantially met. The illustration separates receipt, expenditure and satisfaction dates.

Confirm that the arrangement is a contribution rather than a share purchase or customer transaction. The example expressly assumes no ownership interest is transferred, no product or service is delivered to the donor in exchange, and the donor receives no commensurate benefit. It uses cash already received, not an uncollected promise to give.

## An original dated agreement

Fictional Harbor Bank receives $100,000 on January 10 from a private foundation. The agreement funds a public financial-education program. A separate supported assessment establishes that delivering six specified community sessions by June 30 is a substantive donor-imposed condition, with an enforceable return requirement if that condition is not substantially met.

For this exercise, the condition is stipulated not to be substantially met before the sixth qualifying session has been delivered. This assumption is not a universal substantial-satisfaction rule. The grant provides no per-session entitlement; the stipulated agreement has no additional conditions.

By March 31, three sessions have been delivered, and the bank has incurred and paid $28,000 in program expenses. By June 20, all six qualifying sessions have been delivered, with the necessary evidence available. Another $22,000 of program expense has been incurred and paid. No other cash, receivable, payable, tax or program transaction occurs in this isolated illustration.

## Receipt creates cash without settling recognition

On January 10, the illustrative entry is debit cash $100,000 and credit deferred grant revenue $100,000. Receipt verifies that the asset arrived; it does not establish that the stipulated condition has been satisfied.

Through March 31, record debit program expense $28,000 and credit cash $28,000. Do not release $50,000 of grant revenue simply because three of six sessions have occurred. The agreement in this fixture provides no per-session entitlement, and the assumed condition is not yet substantially met.

The isolated March balances are therefore cash $72,000 and deferred grant revenue $100,000, with cumulative expense $28,000 and no recognized grant revenue. Receiving cash does not prevent this $28,000 loss.

A balance-sheet check gives assets $72,000 minus liability $100,000 = negative $28,000, matching the program's cumulative result. This calculation describes only the isolated transactions, not the bank's total capital or financial condition.

## Recognition follows the stipulated condition

Record the additional $22,000 expense and cash payment as they occur. On June 20, when the condition is substantially met under the stipulated facts, debit deferred grant revenue $100,000 and credit grant revenue $100,000.

| Isolated cumulative position | January 10 | March 31 | June 20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cash | $100,000 | $72,000 | $50,000 |
| Deferred grant revenue | $100,000 | $100,000 | $0 |
| Recognized grant revenue | $0 | $0 | $100,000 |
| Program expense | $0 | $28,000 | $50,000 |
| Program result | $0 | $(28,000) | $50,000 |

The total expense is $28,000 plus $22,000 = $50,000. Closing cash is $100,000 minus $50,000 = $50,000. Recognized revenue $100,000 minus expense $50,000 gives the same $50,000 cumulative result, with no remaining grant liability in this fixture.

Expense incurrence does not, by itself, satisfy the donor condition. Conversely, this example does not invent a requirement to defer all related expenses until the grant revenue is recognized. The two accounting questions need separate support.

## Change the condition, not merely the label

Consider a separate counterfactual agreement that the appropriate assessment establishes has no donor-imposed condition. Under the selected received-grant guidance, the $100,000 would be recognized when received. At March 31, cash would still be $72,000, but the grant liability would be zero and the isolated cumulative result would be positive $72,000 rather than negative $28,000.

The $100,000 difference is a recognition-timing difference, not a change in bank receipts or expenditures. Do not apply this counterfactual merely because a preparer calls the agreement “unrestricted.” Nonprofit restrictions, grant conditions and bank reporting categories are distinct analyses.

For actual work, retain the full agreement, donor identity, return provisions, amendments, condition assessment, performance evidence, expense support and cash reconciliation. An acknowledgment letter may corroborate an event without defining its satisfaction date.

# Analytical procedures: make an expectation that can actually be challenged

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. No professional review recorded; the brand does not mean Ray Sang personally reviewed this article.

## The audit idea

An analytical procedure compares recorded information with an independently developed expectation. A useful expectation has a plausible relationship, dependable inputs and enough precision to detect a meaningful difference. Copying management's recorded total into a second spreadsheet and agreeing the two totals is not independent corroboration.

GAO/CIGIE's Financial Audit Manual section 475 discusses expectations, limits, investigation and supporting evidence in substantive audit procedures. Its detailed federal methodology should not be mistaken for a universal SSARS review program. The computation below can teach analytical thinking, but using it in an audit or review requires the appropriate engagement framework.

## Original worked example

A fictional service company employs 18 salaried staff for all twelve months at $4,000 per month. Three more employees begin July 1 at the same monthly salary. Assume the auditor validates these facts against reliable personnel and pay-rate evidence and excludes payroll taxes, benefits and overtime from the account being tested.

Expected base salary is:

- Full-year staff: 18 × $4,000 × 12 = $864,000
- July starters: 3 × $4,000 × 6 = $72,000
- Total expected base salary: $936,000

The salary account reports $966,000, a $30,000 difference. Management says the entire difference is a year-end bonus. The authorized bonus register and payment evidence support $24,000. That leaves $6,000 unexplained.

Do not change the expectation to $966,000 merely because management supplied an explanation. Update the supported expectation to $960,000 and retain the $6,000 unresolved difference for investigation. The unsupported portion may be an accounting error, a missing fact in the model or a population problem. The arithmetic alone does not decide which.

## Make the threshold explicit before seeing the answer

Suppose the engagement team chose a $5,000 investigation threshold for this particular procedure, based on its professional assessment. The $6,000 residual exceeds that assumed threshold, so the example requires follow-up. The $5,000 is invented for teaching; it is not a standard-prescribed amount or a substitute for materiality.

If the model omitted one employee's unpaid leave, contractor payments misclassified as salaries or a midyear pay increase, its apparent precision would be misleading. A small net variance can also conceal one overstatement offset by another understatement. Consider disaggregating by month, location or employee group when the risk warrants it.

## Document the reasoning that survives recalculation

Retain the chosen relationship, the information sources, validation of inputs, expected amount, comparison limit, explanations, corroborating evidence and remaining work. Explain whether the procedure was intended for risk assessment, substantive evidence or an overall review; those purposes are not interchangeable.

A result within a threshold is one piece of evidence. It is not a guarantee that the balance is correct, that fraud is absent or that the whole engagement can be signed off.

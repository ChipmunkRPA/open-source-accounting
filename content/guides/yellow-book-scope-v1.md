# Government auditing: determine the Yellow Book engagement and version

> **AI-assisted editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Original educational content, not authoritative guidance. Confirm the framework, reporting period, elections, and source versions before use. All company names and transactions in examples are fictional.

## Scope
GAO's Yellow Book addresses government auditing, including financial audits, attestation engagements, and performance audits. Do not assume that every engagement involving a nonprofit or government-related party has identical requirements; identify the law, award, contract, or other basis requiring the work. [gao-yellowbook]

## Applicability note
The 2024 revision applies to financial audits, attestation engagements, and reviews for periods beginning on or after December 15, 2025, and to performance audits beginning on or after that date; early implementation is permitted. The ordinary quality-management timetable is design and implementation by December 15, 2025 and evaluation by December 15, 2026. GAO's current special note permits federal government audit organizations to defer implementation no later than March 16, 2026, with evaluation no later than one year after implementation. Eligibility concerns the audit organization; a government client alone does not establish it. [gao-yellowbook; gao-yellowbook-2024]

## Intake worksheet
Record the engagement type, subject entity, period, governing requirement, applicable other professional standards, prior version used, and early-implementation decision. Separate the engagement's effective date from organization-level quality-management dates. The December 2025 GAO FAQ is GAGAS interpretive guidance and a companion practice aid; it does not replace the standard or the need to identify actual scope. [gao-qm-faq]

## Original planning scenario
A firm begins a performance audit during 2026 but is also completing a financial audit for an earlier period. The team should not choose the applicable edition solely from the report-signing year. Build separate applicability records for each engagement and verify all transition considerations.

## Evidence and review
Create a checklist covering independence questions, competence and training evidence, engagement supervision, reporting requirements, and organization-level quality-management considerations appropriate to the engagement. These are planning categories, not a claim that this starter pack contains every required procedure or requirement.

## Worked routing exercise: determine what the engagement promises

Assume four fictional requests arrive at one practice. Their labels are incomplete descriptions, not actual signed engagements:

| Request | First question | Why the answer matters |
|---|---|---|
| “Audit our city accounts” | Which statements, period, framework and governing requirement are in the engagement? | A financial-statement audit and a performance question involve different objectives and evidence |
| “Check whether the program met its goals” | What evaluation objective, criteria and intended users were agreed? | A report about results is not automatically a financial audit or an attestation engagement |
| “Give assurance on this schedule” | What subject matter, assertion and assurance arrangement are actually authorized? | The schedule's existence does not identify the applicable professional standards |
| “Help management prepare statements” | What service is being provided and what other relationships exist? | Preparation work should not be silently relabeled an audit, and relevant independence questions need evaluation |

The exercise does not classify any request definitively. Instead, it creates an intake record that can be checked against the actual agreement and applicable authority. A procurement document, statute, grant term or contract may identify obligations; a task title or software filter is weaker evidence. Do not assume that a reference to government funds resolves every requirement.

## Worked timing exercise: one year-end, two period starts

For a fictional comparison only, assume both assignments are correctly classified as GAGAS financial audits and neither adopts the 2024 revision early. Assignment C covers July 1, 2025 through June 30, 2026. Assignment D covers January 1 through December 31, 2026. C begins before the December 15, 2025 trigger; D begins after it. A common 2026 period-end year would not justify the same edition decision.

C still needs an explicit record of its applicable predecessor and any transition choices. D's date comparison supports using the 2024 revision under the assumptions, but does not demonstrate that the work meets it. Organization-level quality management is a separate row; it is not postponed automatically merely because an engagement uses an earlier edition.

If an actual performance audit began on December 10, 2025, compare that commencement to the performance-audit trigger, rather than substituting the audited program's fiscal-year start. If the file contains only a contract signature date and a team member's recollection, keep commencement unresolved until the relevant event and support are established. This is a factual issue, not a reason to choose whichever edition makes the checklist easiest.

## A practical applicability record

Record the decision as a chain rather than a checkbox:

1. Identify the obligation or authorized service that calls for the work, with the exact agreement or authority and its scope.
2. Identify the actual engagement type and subject matter. Resolve inconsistent labels between the contract, planning file and proposed report.
3. Preserve dates with their meanings: subject period, commencement, adoption decision, quality-system implementation, evaluation, and report release.
4. Link the exact applicable publication and locator to the specific decision. Keep the publisher's issue date, edition, period trigger and date checked in different fields.
5. Record early implementation or exception decisions with the supporting facts. Absence of a decision is not evidence that early implementation occurred.
6. Identify companion standards and specialized program requirements without claiming they have been reviewed merely because a link is present.
7. Obtain the actual responsible review of the exact packet. If scope or dates change, revisit the affected decision.

This record reduces two common errors: applying a correct rule to the wrong engagement, and applying an obsolete or inapplicable rule to correctly recorded facts. Neither a complete record nor this lesson is an audit opinion.

## Test the evidence statement before release

Compare “the file identifies the edition using the stated assumptions” with “the engagement complies with GAGAS.” The first describes a limited applicability analysis; the second reaches far beyond it. Independence, competence, work quality, supervision, evidence and reporting each require relevant work and decisions. A model-generated checklist can suggest questions, but cannot supply a person's independence assertion or a procedure that never occurred.

If a reviewer receives a new checklist after approving an earlier one, preserve both versions and identify what changed. Record whether the review covered the changed material. Do not copy a signature, timestamp or prior approval to make a changed package appear reviewed.

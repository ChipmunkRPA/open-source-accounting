# Materiality: explain the decision before trusting the percentage

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## What the source supports

SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin 99, dated August 12, 1999, rejects using a percentage as the whole materiality analysis. Its Topic 1.M.1 discusses the information's setting, individual errors, aggregation and qualitative effects. The staff examples include performance trends, contractual thresholds and incentives. The bulletin represents staff views rather than a Commission rule. This article uses that limited principle; it does not adopt the bulletin's historical references as current accounting or auditing standards. [sec-sab99]

The official bulletin was examined on October 4, 2026. That access date does not mean every later legal development, current framework paragraph or entity-specific requirement has been reconciled. Determine the actual reporting framework, filing context and periods before reaching a conclusion.

## A small revenue percentage can obscure the question

All numbers below are invented, in USD. Assume a fictional company reported revenue of 20000000 and pretax income of 40000 before discovering a 30000 omitted expense. For this exercise, the expense belongs entirely to the current year; tax, other errors and uncertainty about the amount are excluded.

| Calculation | Result | What the calculation does not decide |
|---|---:|---|
| Omitted expense / reported revenue | 0.15% | Whether revenue is the informative benchmark |
| Omitted expense / reported pretax income | 75% | Whether all surrounding circumstances have been considered |
| Reported pretax income less omitted expense | 10000 | Whether correction, disclosure or filing action is required |
| Omitted expense / corrected pretax income | 300% | Which denominator should be selected for a real evaluation |

The same 30000 produces different ratios because the questions and denominators differ. Keep both the reported and corrected figures with explicit labels. Do not quietly switch denominators to obtain a preferred answer. A zero or near-zero denominator is an additional reason to explain the economics, not an excuse to return an automatic result.

Add one fictional fact: the prior year's pretax income was 25000 on a comparable basis. Reported current income would suggest a 15000 increase; corrected income would show a 15000 decrease. This is a direction-of-change observation, not a finding that the error is material. It identifies a question the reviewer should address rather than erase with the revenue ratio.

## Build the decision record

Start with the event and the evidence supporting it. Identify the affected accounts, disclosures, periods, legal entity and consolidation level. Distinguish an alleged error from an unresolved estimate judgment; that classification needs its own basis. Record how the error was found and preserve contradictory evidence.

Then explain the benchmarks. What economic feature does each denominator describe? Are interim results, segment information, contractual terms or a particular disclosure relevant? For a covenant, obtain the executed definition and calculation rather than assuming financial-statement income equals contractual income. Avoid inventing a covenant breach from this exercise's numbers.

For each qualitative concern, write a question, an observed fact, missing evidence and its possible consequence. For example: “Could correction reverse the apparent earnings trend?” has a numerical answer under the stipulated facts. “Did management intentionally omit the charge to meet an incentive target?” has no answer without further evidence. Do not treat a plausible motive as a fact.

## Aggregation without disappearing components

Use separate columns for account effects and income effects. An original second scenario has an 18000 revenue overstatement and an 18000 expense overstatement. The net pretax-income effect is zero, but both line-item errors remain 18000. A zero net number cannot establish that the financial statements or disclosures are acceptable. Conversely, a list of individual percentages alone cannot capture their combined effect. SAB 99 addresses both individual and aggregate evaluation; the worksheet should retain both views. [sec-sab99, Topic 1.M.1, Aggregating and Netting Misstatements]

## Completion boundary

A useful draft states the facts accepted for analysis, competing interpretations, calculations, missing evidence and decisions for qualified review. It does not announce “below 5%, immaterial,” select a universal threshold, or turn an AI check into an audit conclusion. Reopen the draft when new errors, revised results or contrary evidence changes its factual basis.

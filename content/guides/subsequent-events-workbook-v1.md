# Subsequent events: separate the underlying condition from the date it was discovered

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## Scope and authority gaps

Record the reporting date, financial-statement issuance or availability date, audit-report date, framework, entity type and purpose of the review. Identify the applicable evaluation endpoint through current authority; do not assume that one endpoint governs initial issuance, reissuance and later-discovered facts.

ASC 855 and applicable auditing requirements are research starting points. Primary ASC text was not accessed. AS 2801 remains link-only and its current version and applicability were not freshly verified. The distinction between an existing condition and a later new condition is a research question to resolve under applicable guidance, not an automatic rule-based conclusion in this article. ## Original chronology packet

Assume a fictional December 31 reporting date. None of the following records has been professionally evaluated:

| Record | Stated event/condition | When learned | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-01 | Customer letter dated January 12 describes payment problems that allegedly began November 20 | January 15 | When the condition actually arose, reliability of the assertion and relevance to year-end measurement |
| E-02 | Incident report describes a fire on January 4 | January 4 | Complete facts, prior conditions, financial effects and disclosure requirements |
| E-03 | Acquisition document signed January 18 references negotiations during December | January 20 | Which rights/conditions existed at December 31 and what the subsequent record establishes |

The letter's date is not automatically the onset date of the customer's difficulty. Likewise, earlier negotiations do not alone establish a completed year-end acquisition. The packet supplies questions, not recognition or disclosure decisions.

## Build one record per event and preserve changes

Track event ID, claimed condition onset, supporting evidence, event date, discovery date, financial effect, report dates and responsible owner. Distinguish a document's preparation date from the period it describes. If an onset date is unknown, say unknown rather than selecting the earliest date in the folder.

Keep initial and later versions of the analysis. A later piece of evidence can change what is understood about an earlier condition; it should not silently overwrite the provenance of the original assessment. Record contradictory evidence and why it changes or does not change a tentative conclusion.

## Separate three workstreams

First establish the facts and their timing. Second research the accounting recognition and disclosure consequences under the actual framework. Third assess the auditor's duties and report implications, if an audit is involved. A disclosure draft does not settle whether an amount should be adjusted, and a management analysis is not an audit procedure merely because it uses an audit-standard reference.

For each proposed conclusion, identify the decisive criterion and the evidence supporting its application. If either is missing, keep the conclusion conditional. Do not present common examples such as a customer bankruptcy or later acquisition as universal classifications independent of facts.

## Completion and handoff

Request the chronology, unresolved condition dates, proposed accounting alternatives, disclosure questions and the last actual search/update performed. An unknown evaluation endpoint prevents a claim that the whole required review period was covered. A completed checklist does not establish that every event was identified.

Do not claim professional approval from a source list or an AI check. Preserve exact revisions and relevant output permissions, especially where legal updates or sensitive company information are involved.

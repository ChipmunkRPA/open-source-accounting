# Revenue research: reconcile the commercial facts before choosing a conclusion

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## Scope before calculation

Start with the entity, reporting framework, customer arrangement and reporting period. Determine which accounting model needs research; Topic 606 is a candidate location, not a completed scope decision. A contract's title, invoice description or payment schedule cannot establish recognition on its own. Primary ASC text was not reviewed for this revision. [fasb-asc]

This guide is an original evidence-organizing method. It does not reproduce a standard's requirements or resolve the accounting judgments for a real transaction.

## Build distinct evidence columns

| Research question | Evidence to obtain | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| What arrangement is operative? | Agreements, amendments, approval and execution evidence | The newest filename automatically governs |
| What is promised and actually delivered? | Scope, operational records, acceptance and dependencies | An invoice line automatically identifies a separate obligation |
| What amounts and conditions exist? | Prices, credits, usage terms, incentives and payment timing | The billed amount is necessarily the accounting transaction price |
| What allocation inputs are supported? | Actual pricing evidence and proposed model | A convenient ratio establishes standalone prices |
| What happened by the reporting date? | Delivery/access records, modifications and remaining duties | Billing, collection and performance occurred on the same day |

The columns are research prompts. The relevant current criteria, exceptions and applicable period must be verified separately through an authorized route.

## Original billing and collection bridge

An invented vendor bills USD 72000 for stated annual platform access and USD 9000 for stated onboarding. The invoice total is USD 81000. Assume the ledger separately records a USD 4000 billing credit and USD 30000 cash collection, with no other movements for this exercise.

The resulting arithmetic bridge is 81000 − 4000 − 30000 = USD 47000 of the stipulated billed balance still uncollected. The pre-collection billed amount after the credit is USD 77000. Keep those labels precise: neither figure is an approved revenue amount, contract balance classification or validated receivable.

If a preparer divides USD 81000 by 12, the result is USD 6750 per assumed month. That is an average of the invoice total, not a supported recognition pattern. The evidence has not established the promises, period of transfer, accounting treatment of the credit or relevant exceptions.

## Resolve contradictions before choosing a model

Suppose operations says access began on June 28 while an acceptance record is dated July 3. Preserve both observations and their meanings. Do not choose one date merely because it falls in the preferred quarter. Ask what actually became available, what acceptance changed and which contractual rights or conditions remained.

For every proposed conclusion, identify the fact that supports it, the actual requirement to be examined and what contrary fact would change the result. Separate recognition, billing, cash and the remaining contractual duties throughout the analysis.

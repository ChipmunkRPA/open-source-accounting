# Non-GAAP communication scope: connect Regulation G, Item 10(e) and Form 8-K

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.0.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## A dated source map, not a filing decision

This guide helps distinguish the rules triggered by a communication's content and route. It assumes a hypothetical domestic operating-company registrant; it does not decide the status of a real entity or approve any adjustment.

The official eCFR pages for Regulation G and Item 10(e) were inspected October 4, 2026 and displayed Title 17 current through **October 1, 2026**. The official SEC Form 8-K PDF then available bore **SEC 873 (02-25)**; selected instructions and Item 2.02 were inspected. A form revision code, an access date and a title-wide current-through date are different facts. No complete post-date amendment or legal-applicability review is claimed.

## First identify the measure and who is speaking

Regulation G defines the covered registrant and non-GAAP financial measure in Rule 101. The definition has exclusions, including specified measures or ratios using permitted underlying inputs and measures required by applicable regulation. Registered investment companies are excluded from the Regulation G registrant definition. Do not decide coverage from a metric's marketing name or from the fact that it contains numbers. [sec-regg-ecfr, 17 CFR 244.101]

For a purported operating metric, list the numerator, denominator and every adjustment. A ratio using adjusted earnings cannot automatically be treated like a pure operating count. For a regulated measure, identify the actual requirement and regulator; voluntary use of a familiar industry label is not proof of an exclusion.

## Then map each communication route

| Route | Starting requirement | Important distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Public material information containing a non-GAAP measure | Regulation G, Rule 100 | Comparator/reconciliation and non-misleading presentation analysis can apply outside an SEC filing |
| Non-GAAP measure included in a Commission filing | Item 10(e) | Adds filing-specific presentation, explanation and restrictions, subject to its scope and exceptions |
| Earnings release within Form 8-K Item 2.02 | Item 2.02 and its Instruction 2 | Expressly applies Item 10(e)(1)(i) to the disclosure |
| Oral or webcast disclosure | Regulation G note and, if relevant, Item 2.02(b) | Website information and an announced location do not alone establish every condition of a separate 8-K exception |

This is a route map, not a complete list of exclusions. The same measure may appear in several places, each requiring review of its actual version and context.

Regulation G Rule 100(a) requires the relevant GAAP comparator and a reconciliation, with different wording for historical and forward-looking measures. Rule 100(b) separately prohibits a materially misleading presentation. Notes address oral presentations, and paragraphs (c)–(d) contain scoped foreign-private-issuer and business-combination exceptions. Rule 102 preserves the separate antifraud-liability question. A completed bridge is not a universal safe harbor. [sec-regg-ecfr]

Item 10(e)(1)(i) addresses comparator prominence, reconciliation, the reason management considers the measure useful and material additional management uses. Paragraph (e)(1)(ii) contains additional filing prohibitions; definitions and exclusions appear later in (e). Read the exact provision that applies instead of treating all portions as interchangeable. [sec-item10e-ecfr]

## Why “furnished” does not mean no requirements

Form 8-K General Instruction B.2 generally treats Item 2.02 information as furnished rather than filed for the specified Section 18 purpose, subject to its stated exceptions. That does not erase Item 2.02's content requirements. Instruction 2 to Item 2.02 expressly invokes Item 10(e)(1)(i). Do not infer that the entire Item 10(e) regime applies identically merely from that one cross-reference, or that furnishing removes Regulation G and other relevant obligations. [sec-form8k]

Item 2.02(a) concerns public releases of material nonpublic results or financial-condition information for a completed quarter or year. Its instructions address additional releases and distinguish disclosures already made in Form 10-Q or 10-K. The general timing instruction uses four business days after the event, unless otherwise specified, and has special counting treatment for weekend or Commission-holiday events. It is subject to stated exceptions and item-specific triggers. This guide does not calculate a real filing deadline. [sec-form8k, General Instruction B.1; Item 2.02(a), Instructions 1–4]

## Original case: the webcast packet is missing one fact

Invented company North Lantern plans a written completed-quarter earnings release at 8:00 a.m. Tuesday and a public webcast at 8:00 a.m. Thursday. Assume a common timezone with no clock change. The interval is exactly 48 hours. No statement about compliance follows from that arithmetic alone.

Item 2.02(b)'s conditional oral/webcast exception includes four components: a complementary presentation initially within 48 hours after a related written release already furnished on Form 8-K; broad public access; the financial/statistical information and required Regulation G information on the website; and a widely disseminated advance announcement with access and website-location information. All conditions must be considered. [sec-form8k, Item 2.02(b)(1)–(4)]

In the exercise, the folder contains the release, a public webcast link and a reconciliation page, but no evidence that the release was furnished before the presentation. The packet therefore cannot establish that the exception applies. An analyst should request the accepted filing record and timing evidence rather than infer filing from the release's presence on the website. Moving the webcast earlier would not fill that evidentiary gap.

Now assume the webcast introduces a materially different adjusted measure from the version in the release. Preserve both definitions, their reconciliations and the actual statements made. The example does not determine whether the presentation remains complementary or whether a further report is required; those questions need actual facts and qualified review.

## Make the review reproducible

Save the specific communication version, channel, time, audience, measure definition, comparator, adjustments and source-based scope conclusion. Link each exception claimed to its individual conditions and evidence. Keep numbers, layout and spoken discussion in the same review packet so a clean spreadsheet does not conceal a different headline or explanation.

A reviewer still needs to evaluate misleading adjustments, labels, tax effects, prominence and consistency. The SEC staff's Non-GAAP Financial Measures interpretations provide additional context, including Questions 100.01–100.06 and 102.10; they are staff views, not a replacement for the rules. [sec-nongaap]

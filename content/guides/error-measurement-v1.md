# Misstatement measurement: track both the closing balance and the year's effects

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## The source question

SAB 108, issued September 13, 2006, discusses how prior-year errors can affect current statements. Topic 1.N, Questions 1 and 2, supports examining both the current-year income effects and the ending balance error, including carryover and reversal effects. Looking only at newly originating errors can miss a reversal; looking only at the closing balance can miss a distorted income statement. Both measurements still require a separate materiality evaluation. These are SEC staff views, not a Commission rule. [sec-sab108]

The official bulletin and SAB 99 were examined on October 4, 2026. Historical references in SAB 108 do not substitute for current ASC Topic 250 or current filing requirements. Primary ASC text was not accessed. This article therefore does not prescribe a correction presentation or decide whether a filing must be amended.

## Define the signs before calculating

This original exercise uses positive amounts for an overstatement of a liability and positive amounts for an overstatement of expense. A negative expense error means expense is understated. All amounts are USD; taxes, asset effects and other errors are omitted by assumption. Real analyses need each affected statement and disclosure, not just these two columns.

Start with an opening liability overstatement of 24000 from earlier years. In the current year, a new unsupported accrual adds 12000. With no other entries, the ending liability error is 36000 and the current-year expense error is +12000.

| Branch | Opening liability error | New accrual | Release through current expense | Ending liability error | Current-year expense error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: no release | 24000 | 12000 | 0 | 36000 | +12000 |
| B: release the prior-year portion | 24000 | 12000 | -24000 | 12000 | -12000 |
| C: release the entire balance | 24000 | 12000 | -36000 | 0 | -24000 |

Each row is an independent hypothetical branch. Add the signed columns: ending liability error equals opening error plus new accrual plus the signed release. Current-year expense error equals the new 12000 expense plus that signed release. Thus Branch B uses 24000 + 12000 + (−24000) = 12000 for the liability and 12000 + (−24000) = −12000 for expense. Subtracting a negative release would reverse its meaning. A debit to the overstated liability and credit to current expense explains the release's negative sign here; it is a stipulated journal path, not an approved posting.

Branch C illustrates why a correct ending balance does not establish correct current-year income: the 36000 release would reverse 24000 of prior-year expense into this year. Branch B illustrates why “12000 new accrual” alone is not a complete current-income description. The bridge must show what actually reversed or was corrected.

## Prepare separate measurements and judgments

Preserve each originating period, account, signed amount and correction already recorded. Reconcile opening differences to the prior workpaper and ending differences to the current accounts. Map each entry to the statements and disclosures it affects, including the tax analysis that this simplified example excludes.

Next evaluate the affected prior periods, current-year statements and proposed correction branch. Apply quantitative and qualitative materiality analysis to the relevant facts; do not add the two measurements together as if they were independent losses. They describe different views of the same error history.

Finally research the available correction and reporting treatments under the actual current authority. A proposed entry, a financial-statement presentation and a securities-law filing obligation are three different decisions. Keep each decision and its supporting authority separate. “Prior year was previously called immaterial” is not a substitute for re-examining it when new facts emerge.

## Boundaries and evidence requests

Ask for the originating records, prior materiality assessments, complete error register, current and prior results, interim information, related disclosures and the actual filing calendar. Show unrelated errors separately before explaining any aggregation. Unexplained netting is not analysis.

Question 3 of SAB 108 addressed initial application for specified 2006 reporting periods. Do not reuse that transition route as a standing permission to post today's old errors to opening equity. Current Topic 250, current SEC requirements and qualified review remain necessary research tasks. [sec-sab108, Topic 1.N, Question 3]

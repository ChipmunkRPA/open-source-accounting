# Government pensions: reconcile expense, contributions and measurement dates

## Funding and expense are different measurements

A contribution records resources paid toward pension funding. Pension expense describes the period's recognized pension cost under the applicable reporting model. A net pension liability is a balance at a measurement date. Deferred outflows and inflows help explain why those three numbers do not move together dollar for dollar.

New York's employer guidance treats these as separate measures. Connecticut's fiscal 2025 financial report separately presents contributions, pension expense and post-measurement contribution deferrals. These are bounded government applications, not a substitute for current standards or a pension actuary's work.

## Original scenario: a supplied employer package

A fictional city participates in a cost-sharing defined-benefit pension plan. Its reporting date is June 30, 2026; the supplied employer package uses June 30, 2025 as the measurement date. Assume the plan type, reporting scope, allocation and use of that measurement date have already been confirmed. The example does not decide their acceptability.

Amounts below are invented, internally consistent inputs from the assumed package. No actuarial valuation or allocation formula is derived here. All relevant pension items are included; assume no restatements, direct equity movements or unrecorded contribution payables.

At the previous reporting date, the city's recognized balances were:

- Net pension liability: $1,200,000
- Deferred outflows: $124,000, comprising $72,000 of post-measurement contributions and $52,000 of other items
- Deferred inflows: $30,000

The current package supplies:

- Net pension liability at the current measurement date: $1,240,000
- Other pension deferred outflows: $92,000
- Pension deferred inflows: $54,000
- Current reporting-period pension expense: $96,000

The city also verifies $78,000 of employer contributions paid during its current reporting year. Assume all $78,000 occurred after the current measurement date and qualifies for the subsequent-contribution deferred outflow. The old $72,000 contribution deferral has rolled into the new measurement-period accounting; it is not added again to closing deferred outflows.

## Build the closing balances before comparing expense

Closing deferred outflows total $170,000: the $92,000 other items plus $78,000 subsequent contributions.

For reconciliation only, define a combined pension burden as liability plus deferred inflows minus deferred outflows. This is an arithmetic subtotal, not a required financial-statement caption. The separate statement elements must retain their identities.

The prior burden is $1,106,000: $1,200,000 + $30,000 − $124,000. The current burden is $1,124,000: $1,240,000 + $54,000 − $170,000. Its increase is $18,000.

That increase equals supplied expense less contributions: $96,000 − $78,000 = $18,000. Equivalently, expense is reconciled by $78,000 of funding plus the $18,000 increase in the combined burden. This agreement checks the supplied package under the stated exclusions. It is not an actuarial method for manufacturing pension expense from incomplete balances.

Looking only at the liability's $40,000 increase would miss the movements in deferred resources. Adding that $40,000 to contributions would suggest $118,000 of expense, overstating the supplied amount by $22,000.

## Keep entries and reporting views connected

Under this case's economic-resources assumptions, recording the current post-measurement contributions debits the relevant deferred outflow and credits cash by $78,000. The remaining entries come from the supported employer schedule, including release of prior contribution deferrals and changes in other pension balances. Do not credit the currently measured liability for the same $78,000 and also retain it as a deferred outflow.

For the governmental-fund comparison, assume all $78,000 of contributions is recognized as current expenditure, with no unpaid contributions or other fund-level pension adjustments. Government-wide expense is $96,000. The results conversion therefore includes $18,000 of additional pension expense relative to the fund amount.

The conversion is not a second bank payment. It reconciles two reporting measures. A preparer should be able to trace cash to contribution records, deferred resources to dated schedules, and expense to the correct employer package.

## Changed fact: an additional contribution after measurement

Suppose the city instead pays $90,000 during the reporting year, including an extra $12,000 after the measurement date. Hold the supplied measured liability, other deferrals and $96,000 expense constant; assume the extra payment qualifies for the same deferral treatment.

Closing deferred outflows become $182,000. The combined burden is $1,112,000, just $6,000 above its opening amount. The reconciliation remains $90,000 contributions plus $6,000 increased burden equals $96,000 expense. Fund expenditure rises to $90,000, leaving a $6,000 results-conversion difference.

Extra cash funding changes cash and the contribution deferral under these assumptions. It does not retrospectively change the earlier measured liability or automatically reduce current pension expense. Its subsequent treatment belongs in the next supported measurement reconciliation.

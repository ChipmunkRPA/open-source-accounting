# Consolidation research: keep economic percentages and decision rights in separate columns

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## Scope and authority status

Identify every legal entity, reporting framework, period, relevant activities and agreements. ASC 810 is a research starting point; current primary text, scope exceptions and applicable model were not accessed. This article does not provide a complete control definition or decide whether an entity should be consolidated. [fasb-asc]

Obtain ownership records, governing documents, decision arrangements, financing, guarantees, options, related-party agreements and amendments. A capitalization table can be one input without describing the entire arrangement.

## Original ownership arithmetic

Assume fictional Investor A owns 60% of Entity B's specified economic interest, and B owns 40% of Entity C's specified economic interest. Under a simple proportional assumption, A's indirect economic percentage is 60% × 40% = 24%.

That 24% is a mathematical result under the stated definition. It is not automatically A's voting percentage, exposure under every contract, power to direct C or consolidation result. A direct 10% economic interest in C, if separately established and nonoverlapping under the same definition, would make the simple economic sum 34%. The nonoverlap and comparability are assumptions requiring evidence.

## Original rights question

A separate governance extract says a 60% holder appoints two of five board members, while specified decisions require consent from another party. The ownership percentage and appointment count describe different things. Two of five is 40% of those seats, but a seat fraction alone does not establish the accounting significance of the decisions, consent provisions or other rights.

Preserve the complete clause, conditions, exercise barriers and contrary documents. Do not infer legal enforceability or substantive power from an abbreviated summary.

## Build a rights-and-economics record

For each right or obligation, identify the party, action, affected activity, effective date, contingencies, evidence and unresolved interpretation. Track changes in rights separately from changes in percentages or cash flows. Funding obligations and guarantees can raise questions not visible in the ownership table.

Research the model, relevant definitions, exceptions and reassessment requirements through authorized current sources. Do not select a voting-interest or variable-interest analysis solely because an arrangement uses one of those words in its marketing materials.

## Requested output and professional boundary

Request the entity map, supported economic calculations, clause-linked rights questions, alternatives and missing documents. Identify what change in facts would require a fresh analysis, without claiming a particular reassessment rule was verified here.

A model-generated summary is not a legal interpretation or professional control assessment. No ownership percentage, management title or balanced elimination entry independently completes the consolidation analysis.

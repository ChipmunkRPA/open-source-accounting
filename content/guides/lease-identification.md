# Lease identification: examine assets, substitution, and decision rights

> **AI-assisted editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Original educational content, not authoritative guidance. **Primary ASC text was not accessed.** Paragraph references are verification tasks, not evidence that current authoritative wording was reviewed. Confirm the framework, reporting period, elections, and source versions before use. All company names and transactions in examples are fictional.

## Purpose
Search for a potential lease inside a commercial agreement rather than relying on its title. Topic 842 is the research entry point. Confirm the relevant definition, scope exclusions, and applicable version using the current primary text; the FASB leases review announcement is background only. [fasb-asc; fasb-leases-pir]

## Contract-reading worksheet
Identify the property or equipment, any named serial number or location, replacement rights, exclusive capacity, period of use, and payments. Request operational explanations of who decides how the asset is used. Distinguish the customer's decisions from protective restrictions, supplier maintenance, and day-to-day operation.

For substitution rights, record both contractual wording and practical evidence. A sentence allowing replacement should not end the analysis. Ask what assets are available to substitute, whether replacement is operationally feasible, and why the supplier would exercise that right. Do not infer economic incentives from boilerplate.

## Original examples
A contract reserves one specifically identified warehouse for a customer's use, while another promises storage service across any facility in a network. These are useful contrasting fact patterns, but the names alone do not determine the answer. A third contract specifies capacity within a larger asset; ask how the capacity is defined and whether it is physically or economically relevant under the applicable criteria.

## Evidence requested
Obtain all schedules, site plans, capacity descriptions, substitution provisions, dispatch rights, service-level commitments, and renewal/termination clauses. Ask the business owner to explain actual use and alternatives, but distinguish current practice from enforceable contractual rights.

## Agent result
Produce an asset-by-asset decision worksheet. Each row records the possible identified asset, relevant control questions, facts supporting and opposing a lease conclusion, missing evidence, and candidate ASC references. Keep the classification conclusion separate from measurement and expense scheduling.

## Stop conditions
Do not generate a liability schedule solely because recurring payments exist. Do not conclude “no lease” solely because the supplier operates or maintains the asset. Where an important right is ambiguous, record a legal/technical question rather than inventing a contractual interpretation.

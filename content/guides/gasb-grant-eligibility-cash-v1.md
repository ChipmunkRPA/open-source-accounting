# Government grants: separate eligibility, timing and cash

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. No professional review recorded; this label does not imply that Ray Sang personally reviewed the work.

## Three questions hiding in one bank deposit

A grant receipt answers a cash question. It does not, by itself, establish the amount earned under a reimbursement award, the period allowed by the award, or the revenue reportable in a governmental fund. Those questions can produce different answers without an error in the bank reconciliation.

Texas Comptroller application guidance distinguishes eligibility conditions from purpose restrictions. Its reimbursement discussion makes allowable expenditure a recognition condition; its governmental-fund guidance adds availability. The explanation here uses those bounded concepts, rather than treating Texas's local procedures as policies for every government.

## Original scenario: a heat-relief program

A fictional county reports on a calendar year. It accepts an award reimbursing qualifying heat-relief services between July 1, 2026 and June 30, 2027, up to $180,000. Assume the arrangement is a nonexchange transaction, the county is an eligible recipient, collection is probable, and no unresolved matching or performance condition remains beyond incurring allowable costs. All amounts are measurable.

At December 31, 2026, the project record contains:

- $98,000 of completed, documented services qualifying under the award
- $14,000 of completed work outside the award's permitted cost categories
- A $20,000 purchase order for services not yet performed; assume it creates no recognized expenditure or payable at year-end
- $70,000 already reimbursed by the grantor

Begin with the government-wide, economic-resources view. The $98,000 is below the $180,000 ceiling, so the eligible grant revenue is $98,000. The cash collected leaves a receivable of $28,000: $98,000 minus $70,000.

A simplified combined grant-side entry is a $70,000 debit to cash, a $28,000 debit to grant receivable, and a $98,000 credit to grant revenue. This combines events for teaching; actual records would recognize receivables and collections on their respective dates. The expenditure or expense for the underlying services is recorded separately.

The completed work totals $112,000. Grant support covers $98,000, leaving $14,000 to other resources under these facts. The unperformed $20,000 order belongs in the commitment analysis, not in this reimbursement receivable. Calling all $132,000 of project activity “grant spending” would overstate eligible revenue by $34,000: the $14,000 excluded cost plus the $20,000 unperformed order.

## Add the governmental-fund lens

Now assume the county has a properly established availability policy under which the $70,000 collected is available, but the outstanding $28,000 is not. This is a supplied accounting conclusion for the example; it is not a universal collection deadline.

Governmental-fund grant revenue is then $70,000, while government-wide grant revenue remains $98,000. The $28,000 recognition difference becomes a revenue-side reconciliation item. It does not mean the grantor owes less, or that the $28,000 is an unallowable cost. A receivable assessment and a revenue-availability assessment answer different questions.

This narrow case does not resolve the balance-sheet classification of cash received before grant eligibility. The selected government page contains inconsistent advance-receipt wording, so that issue needs a separately verified conclusion before anyone records it.

## A restriction can survive revenue recognition

Consider a separate $40,000 cash contribution to the county's cooling-center equipment program. Assume the contribution has no reimbursement condition, its permitted-use period has begun, every other eligibility condition is satisfied, and the only remaining stipulation limits the purpose of spending. The cash is also available under the county's fund policy.

Under these assumptions, both reporting views recognize $40,000 of revenue even if no equipment has yet been purchased. The purpose restriction still matters to the classification and permitted use of the resulting resources. It does not require pretending that no revenue exists until cash is spent. This contribution is independent of the reimbursement calculation above.

## Changed fact: a smaller enforceable ceiling

Suppose the reimbursement award instead has an enforceable $90,000 ceiling at year-end, with every other fact unchanged. Recognizable government-wide revenue is $90,000; the receivable is $20,000 after subtracting the $70,000 collected. The county must cover $22,000 of completed work elsewhere: $112,000 minus $90,000.

Do not reduce the actual service expense merely because the grant covers less. The changed fact affects funding and revenue, not whether the county received the services.

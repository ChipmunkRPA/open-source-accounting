# Revenue recognition: assemble the five-step evidence file

## Scope and conceptual starting point
The FASB revenue project describes guidance for contracts with customers, with contracts governed by other standards excluded from that scope. The project page is background, not verification of the current Codification. Use Topic 606 as the research starting point and confirm scope and applicable amendments in the current primary text. [fasb-revenue-project; fasb-asc]

The familiar five-step framework is a useful way to organize the work: identify the contract, identify performance obligations, determine the transaction price, allocate that price, and assess recognition as the obligations are satisfied. This article supplies an evidence structure; it does not resolve the five accounting judgments for the user.

## Evidence to assemble
| Research area | Evidence requested | Questions that must remain visible |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | Executed agreement, approval, payment terms, amendments | Enforceability, rights, collection assessment, termination |
| Promises | Order form, scope of work, operational explanation | What is actually transferred versus an internal setup task? |
| Price | Fixed fees, credits, usage schedules, rebates | Variable amounts, constraints, timing, other terms |
| Allocation | Standalone sales, pricing methodology, approvals | Are observable inputs available? Are exceptions relevant? |
| Recognition | Delivery, access, acceptance, service logs | When does the customer obtain the benefit/control contemplated by the criteria? |

## Original worked scenario
A fictional vendor invoices 72,000 for a year of platform access plus a 9,000 onboarding fee. Those invoice descriptions are facts about billing. They do not answer whether onboarding transfers a service, whether promises are distinct, or when the performance begins. The research agent should build an 81,000 billing reconciliation but leave allocation and recognition unresolved until the relevant conditions are examined.

## Common traps
Do not equate invoicing with revenue. Do not convert a payment schedule into a recognition schedule without analyzing the promises. Do not treat management's pricing labels as evidence of standalone selling price. Avoid a universal “SaaS means straight-line” rule: first identify what has been promised and the supported transfer pattern.

## Deliverable
Return a step-by-step matrix showing the confirmed fact, the relevant ASC reference, the evidence examined, the provisional conclusion, and the reviewer question. Keep recognition, billing, receivables, cash collections, and contract balances in separate columns.

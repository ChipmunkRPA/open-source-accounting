# Government capital assets: reconcile disposals, proceeds and donations

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. No professional review recorded; this label does not imply that Ray Sang personally reviewed the work.

## The asset register has two balances to remove

Selling equipment moves cash, but it also ends recognition of the equipment's remaining service capacity. Removing only the gross cost can create a false loss if accumulated depreciation is left behind. Recording every dollar of proceeds as a government-wide gain creates the opposite problem: it ignores the carrying amount surrendered.

A donation presents another useful contrast. A government can receive a recognized capital asset without buying it. The absence of an invoice or bank payment does not make the resource valueless.

Texas Comptroller guidance supplies three bounded application points for this case: removal of fully depreciated assets, sale accounting with basis conversion, and measurement of qualifying donations at acquisition value. Its system codes and numerical illustrations are not used here.

## Original scenario: a public-works equipment register

A fictional town has recorded all depreciation through the dates relevant to three year-end transactions. Immediately before those transactions, its government-wide equipment register contains $320,000 of gross cost and $190,000 of accumulated depreciation. Net equipment is therefore $130,000.

Assume there are no impairments, sale fees, interagency transfers or other movements. The following transactions are independent:

1. A retired portable compressor has cost and accumulated depreciation of $56,000 each. It is removed from service with no proceeds.
2. A road-marking unit with $84,000 original cost and $63,000 accumulated depreciation is sold to an outside buyer for $27,500 cash.
3. An unrelated donor transfers workshop equipment with a supported acquisition value of $52,000. The town accepts control for operational use, all eligibility and capitalization conditions are satisfied, and no consideration is paid. Assume receipt at the reporting instant, with no elapsed service requiring depreciation in this case.

These facts deliberately exclude donated equipment intended for sale. Acquisition value is supplied by a supported measurement; it is not assumed to equal the donor's historical cost.

## Record each economic effect

The compressor has zero carrying amount: $56,000 less $56,000. Its removal debits accumulated depreciation and credits equipment by $56,000. Cash and net position do not change. A zero carrying amount does not mean the old cost should remain in the register forever.

The road-marking unit has a $21,000 carrying amount: $84,000 less $63,000. Selling it for $27,500 produces a $6,500 gain. The simplified entry is:

- Debit cash $27,500
- Debit accumulated depreciation $63,000
- Credit equipment $84,000
- Credit gain $6,500

Debits and credits both total $90,500. The sale produces a $27,500 cash receipt but only a $6,500 increase in net position because $21,000 of net equipment leaves the books.

For the qualifying donation, debit equipment and credit contribution revenue by $52,000 in the government-wide view. No cash entry belongs in this transaction. The resource may require a program-revenue or other presentation assessment; this example establishes recognition and measurement, not a universal statement-of-activities caption.

## Reconcile gross and net equipment

The closing gross-cost balance is $232,000: $320,000 − $56,000 − $84,000 + $52,000.

Closing accumulated depreciation is $71,000: $190,000 − $56,000 − $63,000. There is no donated-asset depreciation under the timing assumption. Closing net equipment is therefore $161,000.

A second route reaches the same answer: opening net equipment of $130,000, less the sold unit's $21,000 carrying amount, plus the $52,000 donation, equals $161,000. The fully depreciated retirement contributes zero to this net bridge while still affecting both gross columns.

The three transactions increase net position by $58,500: the $6,500 gain plus $52,000 contribution. Cross-check that against the $31,000 increase in net equipment and $27,500 increase in cash. The two routes agree.

## Keep the governmental-fund presentation distinct

For a governmental fund, Texas's application guidance places the capital-sale proceeds among other financing sources rather than ordinary revenue. Under these stipulated facts, the fund reports the $27,500 sale flow; the government-wide view reports the $6,500 gain after removing the carrying amount.

The donation intended for use does not create spendable cash in that governmental fund. The capital asset and related contribution recognition belong in the economic-resources conversion for this case. Do not add $52,000 to a bank-based spending forecast merely because the government-wide statements recognize revenue.

## Changed fact: a lower sale price

If the road-marking unit instead sells for $18,500, its $21,000 carrying amount is unchanged. The sale produces a $2,500 loss. Net equipment still closes at $161,000, but the three transactions now increase net position by $49,500: $52,000 less $2,500. That also equals the $31,000 net-equipment increase plus $18,500 cash.

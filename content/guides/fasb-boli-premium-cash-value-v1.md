# Bank-owned life insurance: reconcile premiums, cash value and period income

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-05**

AI-assisted editorial draft by Open Source Accounting contributors. Unreviewed: no actual human accounting or legal review is recorded, and the label does not mean Ray Sang personally reviewed this article.

## The narrow accounting question

For the stipulated bank-beneficiary policies, OCC staff commentary measures the asset at realizable cash surrender value and offsets premiums with the period's value change when determining expense or income. Its FASB connection is ASC 325-30, referenced without reproduced Codification text. [OCC BAAS, Topic 5B, question 2](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/banker-education/files/pub-bank-accounting-advisory-series.pdf#page=107).

The exercise below separates three things a close reviewer can reconcile: money paid, the asset still held, and the earnings difference. Every name, amount and sequence is invented for this article. The register is not drawn from the source's fact patterns. It is an arithmetic teaching exercise rather than approval to purchase a policy or post an actual journal.

## An original three policy register

Fictional Linden Bank supplies a register of existing policies labeled Alder, Birch and Cedar. The bank is stipulated to own the entire realizable cash surrender value and be the sole beneficiary. Opening balances already equal supported cash surrender values. All figures are dollars; the dates identify fictional quarters without asserting a real reporting deadline.

| Policy | Opening value | First quarter premiums paid | First quarter closing value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Alder | 120,000 | 18,000 | 134,700 |
| Birch | 85,000 | 12,000 | 99,700 |
| Cedar | 64,000 | 6,000 | 69,100 |
| Total | 269,000 | 36,000 | 303,500 |

For each policy, subtract opening value from closing value, then subtract that change from premiums. A positive remainder is expense in this exercise; a negative remainder is income.

- Alder: value increases $14,700; $18,000 less $14,700 gives $3,300 expense
- Birch: value increases $14,700; $12,000 less $14,700 gives $2,700 income
- Cedar: value increases $5,100; $6,000 less $5,100 gives $900 expense

The total asset increase is $34,500. Premiums of $36,000 therefore produce a net $1,500 expense. Cash paid is not itself the period's final earnings charge. Equally, the $34,500 value increase cannot be treated as net earnings while ignoring the premiums.

## Build the close in separate steps

Use the following teaching journals, with policy-level supporting schedules. Debit and credit totals balance at every step.

1. Record premiums paid: debit BOLI premium expense $36,000; credit cash $36,000
2. Bring assets to closing values: debit BOLI assets $34,500; credit BOLI premium expense $34,500
3. Separate Birch's $2,700 credit balance from the expense account: debit BOLI premium expense $2,700; credit BOLI income $2,700

The final teaching ledger contains $4,200 expense and $2,700 income, yielding the same $1,500 net charge. The third step is a transparent disaggregation for this exercise, not a conclusion about mandatory presentation or permissible netting across policies. Actual financial-statement and regulatory line mapping requires its own assessment.

The complete first-quarter bridge is $269,000 opening asset plus $36,000 cash premiums less $1,500 net expense equals $303,500 closing asset. Cash falls $36,000 while the insurance asset rises $34,500, so total assets fall $1,500. With no other modeled transactions or liability changes, earnings and equity also fall $1,500.

## Follow the same policies through another quarter

In the second quarter no premiums are paid. Supported closing values become Alder $137,100, Birch $98,500 and Cedar $70,300, totaling $305,900. These are stipulated independent measurements, not projected returns.

Record Alder's $2,400 increase by debiting its asset and crediting BOLI income. Record Birch's $1,200 decrease by debiting BOLI expense and crediting its asset. Record Cedar's $1,200 increase by debiting its asset and crediting BOLI income. Each pair uses that policy's amount.

Second-quarter income of $3,600 less expense of $1,200 gives $2,400 net income. Across both quarters, the asset rises $36,900, cash premiums total $36,000, and cumulative net income is $900. The combined bridge is $269,000 plus $36,000 plus $900 equals $305,900. This also reconciles first-quarter expense of $1,500 followed by second-quarter income of $2,400.

## Test the measurement separately from cash

These alternatives each replace one first-quarter input; they are not combined and do not revise the second-quarter example.

- If Alder's supported closing value were $131,700, portfolio closing value would be $300,500. The value increase would be $31,500 and net expense $4,500, an additional $3,000 charge with no change in premiums
- If Birch's supported closing value were $103,700, portfolio closing value would be $307,500. The increase would be $38,500 and net income $2,500, a $4,000 earnings improvement with unchanged cash payments

First-quarter earnings break even at a total closing value of $305,000: opening $269,000 plus premiums $36,000. This is a mathematical threshold for the supplied case, not a valuation estimate or an assurance that value grows with premiums.

## Evidence and boundaries

Obtain dated insurer statements, the contract terms establishing the bank's realizable amount, premium remittances and the prior close's policy ledger. Confirm that each statement belongs to the same policy and reporting date; distinguish supported surrender amounts from forecasts or other quoted balances. Preserve a policy-by-policy explanation for every difference before considering aggregate presentation.

Split-dollar arrangements, employee benefits, death claims, policy sales and taxes are excluded. The calculations do not establish contractual ownership, regulatory eligibility, recoverability, disclosure completeness or the treatment of any excluded transaction.

# Equity offering costs: reconcile cash, paid-in capital and an abandoned attempt

**Ray Sang’s Annotation**

**Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-05**

## Separate the offering outcome from the cash movement

A payment register alone cannot explain an equity raise. Both a completed issue and an unsuccessful proposal can consume cash, but their costs need different destinations in the selected OCC bank-accounting response. Directly related successful-offering costs reduce proceeds within equity; unsuccessful-offering costs enter current expense.

This exercise begins after the relevant cost-eligibility assessments. A cost's proximity to a financing date is not proof of its accounting treatment. Separate identifiers prevent confusing the evidence for different transactions.

## An original three-attempt register

Fictional Juniper Reach Bank has two separately completed plain common-stock issuances and a third, independently abandoned attempt. The shares all have $2 par value. The transactions have no linked terms or shared costs. An independently supplied assessment concludes that the listed completed-issue costs qualify for deduction from that issue's proceeds in equity.

Here, all listed services are first incurred, recognized and paid on the stated completion or abandonment date. Share proceeds are received in full on each completion date. There are no earlier costs, prepayments, liabilities, refunds or deferred balances to analyze. All amounts are dollars. Other operations and opening equity are excluded.

| Attempt | Outcome and date in 2026 | Shares issued | Price per share | Gross proceeds | Identified costs paid |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Grove | Completed May 31 | 30,000 | 18 | 540,000 | 25,200 |
| Harbor | Completed September 15 | 16,000 | 25 | 400,000 | 18,400 |
| Ridge | Abandoned August 31; no stock sold | 0 | — | 0 | 13,200 |
| Total | Separate outcomes | 46,000 | — | 940,000 | 56,800 |

Grove's $540,000 proceeds split into $60,000 common stock at par and $480,000 additional paid-in capital before costs. Deducting its $25,200 eligible costs leaves $454,800 of additional paid-in capital and $514,800 net cash.

Harbor's $400,000 proceeds split into $32,000 common stock and $368,000 additional paid-in capital before costs. After its $18,400 eligible costs, additional paid-in capital increases $349,600 and cash increases $381,600. Ridge has no proceeds and contributes no shares or paid-in capital; its $13,200 cost produces expense and a cash outflow.

## Keep the journals identifiable

Each entry separates receipts from disbursements. “Additional paid-in capital” is abbreviated APIC in the later bridge.

1. Grove proceeds: debit cash $540,000; credit common stock $60,000 and additional paid-in capital $480,000
2. Grove eligible costs: debit additional paid-in capital $25,200; credit cash $25,200
3. Ridge abandoned-attempt costs: debit offering-attempt expense $13,200; credit cash $13,200
4. Harbor proceeds: debit cash $400,000; credit common stock $32,000 and additional paid-in capital $368,000
5. Harbor eligible costs: debit additional paid-in capital $18,400; credit cash $18,400

The paid-in-capital charges do not change the number of issued shares or their stated par amount. A net bank deposit alone would not identify these components or establish eligibility.

## Prove the same result three ways

| Incremental bridge | Grove | Harbor | Ridge | Combined |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Net cash movement | 514,800 | 381,600 | (13,200) | 883,200 |
| Common stock increase | 60,000 | 32,000 | 0 | 92,000 |
| APIC increase after costs | 454,800 | 349,600 | 0 | 804,400 |
| Current expense | 0 | 0 | 13,200 | 13,200 |
| Total equity increase, including expense effect | 514,800 | 381,600 | (13,200) | 883,200 |

First, cash increases by $940,000 gross proceeds less $56,800 payments = $883,200. Second, contributed equity increases $92,000 common stock plus $804,400 APIC = $896,400; the $13,200 expense reduces the aggregate equity increase to $883,200. Third, 46,000 issued shares multiplied by $2 par gives $92,000 common stock. The last check starts with share count.

No liability or other asset movement is modeled, so the cash and total-equity changes agree. The expense effect is shown through earnings; this is not a separate instruction to debit retained earnings in addition to recording expense. Such a second charge would count the same loss twice.

## A balanced error can hide in the components

Suppose the bookkeeper expenses every payment. Cash and total equity still reconcile, but expense becomes $56,800 instead of $13,200, and APIC becomes $848,000 instead of $804,400. Earnings are understated and APIC overstated by $43,600. A total-equity check alone misses this classification error.

The opposite error attaches Ridge's cost to Harbor because Harbor completed soon afterward. That would report $791,200 APIC and zero current expense. Relative to the supported case, APIC is understated and earnings overstated by $13,200, while total equity again looks correct. Completion of a different issuance does not change the independently supplied outcome of Ridge.

## Sensitivity: a changed eligibility conclusion

Independently assume new evidence establishes that $3,600 of Grove's listed payment purchased a current-period general administrative service, rather than an eligible issuance cost. All other facts stay fixed. This is a supplied reclassification, not a rule that a particular invoice label always requires expense.

Grove's eligible cost becomes $21,600, APIC becomes $458,400, and administrative expense becomes $3,600. Replace its original cost entry with debit APIC $21,600 and administrative expense $3,600; credit cash $25,200. Combined APIC becomes $808,000 and total expense $16,800. Net cash remains $883,200, as does total equity: $92,000 + $808,000 less $16,800. Eligibility changes components without changing cash.

## Evidence and exact source boundary

Retain completion evidence, share counts, par authorization, subscription receipts, transaction-specific invoices, cost-eligibility conclusions and the abandoned-proposal decision. Reconcile the share register separately from cash and expense. Uncertain links between offerings or unsupported shared-cost allocations require further analysis.

Excluded are deferred interim cost policy, debt/convertible instruments, mixed securities, warrants, employee awards, SEC registrant-specific rules, tax and the source's examples. No intermediate cost-deferral conclusion is asserted.

# Fund balance: identify the constraint before calling money available

**Ray Sang’s Annotation · Original educational explanation · Version 1.0.0 · 2026-10-04**

AI-assisted by Open Source Accounting contributors. No professional review recorded; the brand does not mean Ray Sang personally reviewed this article.

## The accounting idea

The total fund balance is not one unrestricted spending account. Classification communicates the nature of constraints or the form of resources. To understand a balance, ask who imposed the restriction, how it can be changed and whether the amount is spendable at all.

Texas Comptroller's governmental-fund guidance describes nonspendable, restricted, committed, assigned and unassigned classifications. Texas-specific decision makers and account codes are local implementation details. The example below invents a municipality and assumes its actions satisfy the appropriate conditions; it does not decide the legal validity of a real resolution.

## Original worked example

Assume a city's general fund has a positive closing fund balance of $1,000,000, already reconciled to its statements. Its analysis identifies:

- $80,000 in qualifying prepaid resources: nonspendable
- $260,000 constrained by an outside grant: restricted
- $300,000 validly committed to a fire-station project by the city's highest decision-making authority
- $140,000 assigned for vehicle replacement by a properly authorized official
- The remaining $220,000: unassigned

The categories total $1,000,000. This is a classification of a reconciled balance, not an instruction to create five cash accounts. The city's bank balance could differ because receivables, payables and other statement elements also matter.

For this exercise, suppose the $140,000 vehicle assignment is later validly removed and no stronger constraint exists. The classification would shift $140,000 from assigned to unassigned; total fund balance remains $1,000,000 and cash does not change. This is an illustration of economic effect only. Whether a post-year-end action affects year-end classification requires separate analysis.

## Labels are evidence claims

Calling a balance “reserved” or “board designated” does not establish its classification. A worksheet should identify the exact grant clause, law, formal action or delegated authority and the relevant dates. If an amount is already externally restricted, an internal plan for that same amount does not ordinarily turn it into a merely assigned balance.

Equally, an approved purchase order does not create a sixth generic fund-balance category. Investigate the underlying constraints, expenditure recognition and encumbrance policy. Avoid counting the same resource in both a project commitment and a separate reserve schedule.

## A useful decision scenario

A manager proposes spending $400,000 immediately because the fund balance is $1 million. The example shows why that statement is incomplete: only $220,000 has been identified as unassigned. Even that amount is not necessarily the safe cash available today. Cash timing, budget authority, contractual obligations and reserve policy are additional questions.

The correct next step is to produce a constraint-and-liquidity bridge. It should show the proposed use, which resources could support it, what approvals would be necessary and when cash is expected. Do not present an accounting classification as permission to spend.

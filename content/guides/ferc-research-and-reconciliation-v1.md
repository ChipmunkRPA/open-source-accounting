# FERC research: connect the ledger, authority and reporting destination

> **AI-assisted editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Original research aid, not FERC instructions, an accounting determination or a completed regulatory return. All numerical inputs are fictional.

## Define the assignment

Write the legal entity, jurisdiction basis, industry, reporting period, requested return and proposed accounting question. Identify whether the work concerns electric utilities, gas, oil pipelines or centralized services before selecting a source package. This guide does not determine who must file or prescribe a system of accounts. Do not reuse an electric-company pilot for another industry without a separately verified basis.

Keep three linked records: the organization's ledger facts, the applicable regulatory instructions, and the reporting destination. An account title resembling a form label does not prove a valid mapping. Obtain the exact account/instruction locator and exact form schedule, row/column or taxonomy concept for the period. Leave the destination unresolved when the authority is missing.

## Prepare the source package

For every rule, order, instruction or staff explanation, record the issuer, authority class, docket or identifier, exact edition and amendments, original artifact hash, locator and separate issued/available/effective/retrieved dates. Resolve current, superseded, rescinded and historical-use status. A link remaining on an index is not an applicability decision. Staff explanation, company filing practice and governing instructions must remain distinguishable in the evidence file.

Inspect the original instructions and any applicable orders before asserting treatment of construction costs, financing costs, depreciation, retirements or regulatory assets/liabilities. A proposed adjustment labeled “regulatory” does not establish recognition or measurement. Retain its facts, calculation, alternative treatments and unresolved criteria. The limited source passages checked below do not settle those transaction-specific questions.

## Reconcile without concealing differences

Freeze the ledger export with its date, entity, currency, unit scale, sign convention and hash. Record beginning balance, movements and ending balance; trace them to supporting records. Keep proposed bridge items separate from accepted adjustments. Show any remaining difference against a separately sourced target; never use an unexplained plug merely to force agreement.

For each mapping, distinguish gross balance, contra balance and net presentation. Cross-foot both entity and account groupings. A consolidated zero difference can hide offsetting entity errors; a matching net number can hide materially different gross balances. These are arithmetic failure modes, not a determination of materiality or prescribed presentation.

## Exceptions and review questions

An acquisition or disposal may change which entity owns a balance. Intercompany entries can match in total yet refer to different periods. A rounded report can differ from a whole-currency ledger. A prior-period correction may require investigation beyond the current bridge. Resolve each with facts and the relevant instructions, rather than silently netting or carrying forward an old mapping.

A specialist should decide whether each proposed treatment and reporting destination is supported for the entity and period. The reviewer must identify exact source and workpaper revisions, scope, exceptions and date. Source-operation permission, workspace access and professional review are separate. Completing a reconciliation neither posts journals nor files a return; both remain separately controlled actions.

## What the inspected regulatory passages add

The current research route is more specific than a list of links. In the inspected Part 101 General Instructions, paragraph 2(A) addresses records sufficient to identify and analyze transactions; 2(F) separates account order from report-form order; and 3(C) addresses reconciling a utility's own account numbering to the prescribed numbering. These limited facts explain why a ledger-to-report map needs evidence on both sides. They do not identify the proper account for the fictional transaction or establish that Part 101 applies to Cedar.

The FERC accounting index also gives Chief Accountant guidance a specific regulatory role and labels certain historical items rescinded or superseded. Do not classify all staff material as either automatically nonbinding or automatically current. Identify the actual issuance, its authority, subsequent action and the transaction it addresses. A historical link can be useful for research without being a current treatment rule.

## Original worked example: the mapping covers the amount but misses its nature

Assume a fictional ledger group totals 600 thousand USD: asset X is 400 and asset Y is 200. A proposed report map sends all 600 to one line because its title sounds similar. Supporting records later show that X and Y have different described uses and may require separate destination analysis. The 600 tie-out proves arithmetic coverage of that supplied population, not correct classification.

A better evidence record retains two mappings: X → candidate destination A, 400; Y → unresolved destination, 200. The mapped amount is **400**, the unresolved amount is **200**, and together they reconcile to **600**. Do not drop the unresolved item from the denominator and declare the mapping 100% complete. Amount coverage is **400/600 = 66.67%**, rounded, but that percentage is not an accounting assurance level or a materiality conclusion.

If a second candidate maps Y to destination B, the fact that both A and B are valid labels does not resolve which applies. Link the exact instruction and the supporting asset facts to the decision. Preserve the rejected alternative and reason, so a later reader can distinguish a researched judgment from an arbitrary lookup.

## Revision and currentness tests

Use separate identities for the ledger extraction, account map, relevant authority and reporting taxonomy. A taxonomy update can change a concept identifier without changing the underlying accounting. A regulatory order can change the treatment without altering the ledger's local label. A current form index can still link to an older instruction file; the observed August 2022 date on the linked Form 1 instructions page is a reason to verify the reporting-period package, not proof that the package is invalid.

For the reporting period in question, identify the version actually accepted by the filing system and the controlling instructions. Compare the exact proposed output with that package. Do not silently replace an unresolved concept with the nearest text match or assume a machine-valid return is substantively correct.

## A reviewable conclusion

State what reconciled, which mappings were supported, which remain proposed, and what source/date limitations remain. For the fictional 600 example: “The worksheet covers the full supplied balance; 200 remains without a supported destination.” That is more accurate than “FERC mapping complete.” The conclusion should not claim an entity is required to file, a return was accepted or a journal was posted unless the necessary real evidence exists.

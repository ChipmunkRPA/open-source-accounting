Editorial research outline · 2026-10-04 · No publisher standards text included

Start with the taxpayer facts, transaction, tax year and tax issue. A large tax library should preserve dated versions and the relationships among statutes, regulations, rulings, procedures, notices and informal publications. The outlines here are original research questions and do not provide a tax position or a filing instruction.

Substantive guidance should take priority over accumulating blank forms, translated copies or fragments for the sake of a larger count. Preserve each complete original document and its provenance, then separately record its internal sections. A weekly Bulletin is a compilation; a ruling reproduced in that Bulletin should be linked to its standalone release so the same underlying guidance is not misleadingly counted twice. An earlier publication may be useful historical evidence while being inapplicable to the current tax year.

## Research tracks

### Revenue rulings, procedures, notices and Internal Revenue Bulletins

Published federal tax guidance and historical amendments.

- Is this advance guidance or published in the Internal Revenue Bulletin?
- What legislation, regulations and later guidance amend or supersede it?
- What taxable period and taxpayer facts are relevant?

Work product: A dated guidance chain that distinguishes precedent, advance releases and explanatory material.

### Substantive instructions and publications

Practical tax administration guidance and edition history.

- Which tax year, form revision and substantive issue match the problem?
- Does later law override the informal guidance?
- Are language editions or reissued files duplicates for the requested scope?

Work product: An edition-aware reference map; forms and page fragments do not inflate article counts.

## Evidence and review gates

For each substantive conclusion, preserve the exact source identifier, edition or version, relevant reporting period, a precise locator, the fact pattern and any exceptions. Keep a direct quotation, an original paraphrase, a calculation and an assumption distinguishable. Record what source text was actually available and any unresolved access or rights gap.

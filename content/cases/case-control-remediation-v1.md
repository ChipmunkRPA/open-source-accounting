# Control remediation: keep the reporting date, operating date and evidence date separate

**AI-assisted editorial draft · Original educational case · v1.0.0 · 2026-10-01 · No professional review recorded.**

## 1. Scope and the question this packet can answer

This fictional case expands the existing scenario: a monthly review control failed for two months; a revised procedure was approved on September 20; the reporting date is September 30; the first scheduled run of the revised control is October 5. The two-month failure is an exercise premise, not a conclusion inferred merely from a missing signature. No evidence of earlier operation of the revised activity is available in the stipulated September packet.

The learning task is to assemble a defensible chronology and identify what each record can support. It is **not** to decide that a control is effective, a deficiency is remediated, or a particular report must be issued. The engagement type, reporting year, applicable framework/version, relevant assertions, financial magnitude and evaluation criteria remain unresolved. Month/day dates below belong to one fictional year; they do not select the version of an auditing standard.

The records, identifiers and counts below are invented for this exercise. They are not real company records or procedures performed by this library. A document's assertion is not independent verification of its contents. Proposed work in the worksheet remains proposed until an actual person performs and records it.

## 2. Two evidence packets, with no silent backdating

Packet A contains the records stipulated to be available on September 30. Packet B is an explicitly later addition assembled on October 7. “Record available” means the stipulated first availability to the fictional preparer; it is separate from the packet assembly date and is not an independently authenticated timestamp. Preserve both packet identities instead of overwriting Packet A's chronology.

| Record | Packet | Event or assertion date | Record available | Stipulated content | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-01 | A | 07-31 and 08-31 | 09-12 | Issue log identifies the two missed monthly reviews | Which accounts/assertions, complete populations and other controls were affected? |
| P-02 | A | 09-20 | 09-20 | Approval of revised procedure version C-02 | Does the design address the identified risk, and how would it produce evidence? |
| P-03 | A | 09-23 | 09-23 | Training attendance record | Who was authorized and competent to operate the activity? Attendance alone does not answer that |
| P-04 | A | 09-28 | 09-29 | Ad hoc retrospective worksheet with the 12 rows in section 3 | Its scope, selection method, work performed, criteria and supervision are not established by the row count |
| P-05 | A | 10-05 scheduled | 09-24 | Calendar invitation for the first revised monthly run | A scheduled event is not a completed event |
| P-06 | A | 09-30 | 09-30 | Management's note says the redesign is approved and the first run is scheduled | The note contains no completed C-02 run or operating-effectiveness conclusion |
| P-07 | B | 10-05 claimed operation | 10-06 | New review file covers transactions dated 09-01 through 09-30 and claims a C-02 run on 10-05 | Verify actual execution, review precision, exceptions and the completeness/accuracy of inputs |
| P-08 | B | 10-07 sign-off | 10-07 | A later sign-off refers to P-07 | Identity, authority, scope and actual work behind the sign-off require evidence |

The sequence is unambiguous within the hypothetical: September 20 approval precedes September 30, while the scheduled October 5 run and October 7 sign-off follow it. The transaction period in P-07 is September; its claimed operating date is October. These are different fields. A filename such as “September review” cannot replace either date.

At the Packet A cutoff, P-05 supports a plan. P-07 and P-08 were not yet available. In Packet B, keep the later information and its stated dates; do not relabel the October operation or sign-off as a September event. This is **not** a blanket rule that later-obtained evidence can never inform an earlier-date assessment. Its relevance, any additional procedures, and the implications for reporting require the actual engagement facts and applicable requirements. This exercise supplies no such conclusion.

P-04 is a separate retrospective activity. Its existence does not rewrite the premise that the earlier monthly reviews were missed, and its recorded date does not make it an operation of C-02. Determine what P-04 actually tested before using it for any other purpose.

There are **12 worksheet rows, 10 distinct August packet IDs and zero listed July packet IDs**. These counts describe the supplied records. They do not establish whether any procedure was sufficient, who performed it or whether the selections represent the population.

| Calculation | Exact conditional ratio | Displayed percentage | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worksheet rows / stated August packets | 12 / 90 | 13.33% | Row-count ratio; mislabeling it “distinct packets inspected” would double-count the two copies |
| Distinct listed August packets / stated August packets | 10 / 90 | 11.11% | Distinct-record ratio within the stipulated August list |
| Worksheet rows / combined stated packets | 12 / 200 | 6.00% | Includes duplicate rows and changes the denominator to two periods |
| Distinct listed packets / combined stated packets | 10 / 200 | 5.00% | Conditional two-period record ratio; the listed items all come from August |

Percentages are independently rounded to two decimal places, half even, solely for display. No ratio is a statistical confidence level, error rate estimate, acceptable sampling threshold, financial-magnitude measure or effectiveness score. The arithmetic cannot select the appropriate population or establish completeness. The two missed monthly reviews are another unit entirely; dividing two control events by 200 transaction packets would not produce a meaningful control-effectiveness percentage.

The phrase “no exception recorded” is narrower than “no error exists.” The worksheet does not state the test criteria, full work performed, selection rationale, relevant assertions or treatment of contrary evidence. Ten listed items cannot be silently presented as inspection of all 90 August packets or all 200 combined packets. The July gap stays visible even though the combined ratio can be calculated.

For each work row, retain exact record ID/version/locator, the assertion being tested, what was actually done, who did it, execution and evidence dates, findings, contrary information, limitations and the exact revision reviewed. Keep a proposed procedure's future tense until performed. A checked box or attachment count cannot populate these fields by inference.

## 5. Five questions to work through

These additional practice questions use draft reasoning prompts, not professionally adjudicated answers.

1. **What can Packet A say about the October 5 activity?** It records a scheduled first run. It supplies no completed C-02 operating record as of its September 30 cutoff. Preserve the distinction between a plan and an event.
2. **Does P-07's September transaction period make its October operating date September?** No. Keep the covered transaction period, claimed performance date, record availability and sign-off date as separate fields. Evaluation of later evidence remains an unresolved professional task.
3. **Why are 12 rows and 10 distinct packets both retained?** The copies explain the worksheet's physical row count; they do not add distinct packet IDs. In real work, verify whether repeated IDs represent copies or different procedures before classifying them.
4. **What is missing from a claim that the 5.00% ratio proves the control worked?** Population completeness and relevance, actual procedure/selection evidence, appropriate criteria, timing, precision, competence, other controls and the required professional evaluation. The ratio itself proves none of those.
5. **What changes if actual earlier operation is subsequently documented?** Preserve the new record and its provenance, reassess the chronology and affected claims, and obtain the necessary current review. Do not overwrite the old packet or automatically mark the whole deficiency remediated.

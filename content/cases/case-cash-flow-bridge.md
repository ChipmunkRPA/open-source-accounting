# Worked case: a balance-sheet movement is not a cash-flow line

**AI-assisted editorial draft · v0.6.0 · 2026-09-27 · No professional review recorded.**

A fictional reconciliation exercise with stipulated classifications; the arithmetic is not a GAAP classification opinion.

## Facts

Opening equipment is $400,000 and closing equipment is $590,000. Additions consist of $120,000 paid in cash and $100,000 financed directly by a lender to the vendor. Equipment with carrying amount $30,000 is removed. Assume no depreciation or other movement for this deliberately simplified bridge. Cash proceeds from disposal are $25,000.

## Reproduce the bridge

The asset bridge is $400,000 + $120,000 + $100,000 - $30,000 = $590,000. The $190,000 increase is not the cash purchase amount. Under the exercise's stipulated classification, cash investing movements from the equipment purchases and sale total -$120,000 + $25,000 = -$95,000. The financed purchase has no cash movement in the entity's stipulated cash population; its disclosure treatment still needs authoritative research.

## What remains unresolved

Investigate whether the lender actually paid the entity before the vendor, the cash population used, fees, acquisition-related items, restricted funds, and the nature of the disposal. Changing those facts may change the working analysis. The $5,000 difference between carrying value and disposal proceeds is a separate calculation, not another cash outflow.

## Expected Agent behavior

The Agent should reproduce the bridge, identify the missing financing-flow facts, and avoid presenting the balance-sheet increase as cash capital expenditure. It should label the classification assumption rather than cite nonexistent source text. A reviewer should confirm the transaction population and classification before a memo is approved.

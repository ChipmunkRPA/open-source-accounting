# Government-audit evidence case: two engagements, three clocks and conditional edition choices

> **AI-assisted editorial draft — not professionally reviewed.** Original fictional exercise, version 2.0.0. This case teaches evidence organization and conditional reasoning. It summarizes selected verified timing provisions and applies them conditionally to fictional facts. It does not document actual audit procedures or certify conformity.

## Scope and the decision that remains open

The fictional Birch Review Group has two assignments involving a local government. A preparer proposes using one government-auditing checklist because both reports may be signed in 2026. The exercise asks whether the file supplies enough evidence for that decision. It does not supply the governing agreements needed to decide whether each assignment is required to follow GAGAS. The official source checks below supply limited timing context; entity-specific applicability still requires evidence.

Government auditing is the research setting. The AUDIT library label does not resolve whether a financial audit, performance audit, attestation engagement, review or another assignment is required. Nor does it turn this case into a GASB accounting-policy example, a FASAB federal-accounting conclusion or a program-specific compliance audit.

## Three clocks, not one report-year shortcut

Create separate rows for the subject period, the engagement's relevant commencement event and organization-level implementation timing. Add the proposed or actual report date as a distinct field. The labels are an evidence-organizing device, not a claim that every requirement uses these triggers.

Assignment A supplies a subject-period start and end. Assignment B supplies a purported commencement date. The verified source triggers below allow conditional comparisons. The actual assignment classification, scope decision and commencement evidence still determine whether those comparisons can be used in a real engagement.

An honest draft finding is: **The packet does not support selecting one edition for both assignments.** It identifies different timing inputs and unresolved applicability evidence. The conditional answers below explain how an older financial-audit period and a newer performance-audit commencement can lead to different edition choices. Report year and download date do not replace the identified triggers.

## Reusable engagement and edition worksheet

Use one row per engagement and one row per separately relevant organizational obligation. Keep original records and access restrictions outside this teaching template.

| Field | Record or leave unresolved |
|---|---|
| Identity and scope | Engagement ID, organization, subject entity, assignment type, governing agreement and actual reporting framework |
| Timing observations | Subject-period start/end; commencement event and definition; proposed versus actual report date; organization-level timing recorded separately |
| Classification | Confirmed user assertion, assumption or unknown for each input; corroboration and reviewer decision tracked in different fields |
| Authority package | Exact publication/edition, amendment or erratum, authoritative locator, original artifact hash and issued/effective/retrieved dates |
| Applicability | Exact candidate provision and trigger; early-implementation choice; exceptions and unresolved entity/organization conditions |
| Companion material | Separate standard, FAQ, staff explanation, firm checklist and entity practice; a checklist title is not the governing text |
| Evidence coverage | What was actually inspected; unavailable sections/components; third-party inserts; extraction and completeness limits |
| Operations and access | Source-specific display/model/export permissions; workspace/seat access; retention restrictions; no permission inferred from an open URL |
| Proposed conclusion | Conditional mapping or abstention, alternatives, missing facts and named follow-up owner |
| Review record | Actual authorized reviewer, exact packet and source revisions/hashes, review date/scope/decision; leave blank until performed |
| Release boundary | Draft, approved and released states separately recorded; release authority and remaining limitations |

A filled worksheet is not evidence that independence, competence, supervision, quality management or reporting procedures have been completed. Those categories require their own applicable requirements, work and review. Source rights, subscription, workspace access and professional approval remain separate from a classification chosen in the interface.

## Counterexamples and stop conditions

### The date is known, the rule is not

Suppose a new fictional log corroborates Assignment B's January 12 date. That resolves a factual question about the log, not the applicable edition. The governing trigger can now be compared conditionally, but organization scope, the relevant event and any adoption conditions still require evidence. Do not promote F-05 or F-06 to confirmed because F-03 gained support.

### The checklist changes after the draft

A coordinator replaces checklist C-1 with C-2 and says the latter contains updated procedures. Preserve C-1 and the original mapping. Record the new checklist's revision/hash and identify changes before making a new mapping. A newer filename is not evidence of completeness, applicable authority or a reviewer decision. Approval of a prior packet does not automatically approve its changed successor.

### The commencement date is disputed

A second log labels December 2, 2025 as commencement, while the first calls January 12, 2026 the start. Retain both observations and their event definitions. Do not silently choose the date producing a preferred edition. Prepare alternative conditional mappings, identify what event each record describes and abstain from a final applicability conclusion until the dispute and governing definition are resolved.

### The organization is assumed to qualify for an exception

A proposed organization-level exception is not an engagement-level conclusion. Leave F-06 unknown until actual scope evidence and the exact provision are reviewed. Do not infer eligibility from “government work,” firm size, the client name or an earlier engagement's checklist. No exception is prescribed or established by this exercise.

## Four study questions with draft expectations

1. **Can a shared 2026 report year settle both editions?** No. The verified provisions use different timing inputs, and the packet still lacks actual scope/adoption decisions; report year alone does not resolve them.
2. **Does corroborating the start log settle the quality-management question?** No. It addresses one factual input, while organizational applicability and authoritative timing remain separate.
3. **Should a revised checklist inherit a prior review?** Record a new revision and identify the reviewed scope. Do not claim an actual review of changed material from the old decision.
4. **What can be released from this exercise now?** An explicitly incomplete educational evidence map with missing-information questions, not a claim of standards conformity or procedures performed.

The four questions are inside this case and do not increase the separate question-bank count. ## Sources and verification: selected current timing facts

AI editorial checking on October 4, 2026 inspected the [2024 Yellow Book](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-106786.pdf) [gao-yellowbook-2024], physical page 2 (unnumbered introductory notice), and the [current GAO Yellow Book page](https://www.gao.gov/yellowbook) [gao-yellowbook], “Current Yellow Book” and “Special Note for Federal Government Audit Organizations.” The 2024 revision uses periods beginning on or after December 15, 2025 for financial audits, attestation engagements and financial-statement reviews, and audits beginning on or after that date for performance audits. Early implementation is permitted.

GAO's current page separately describes a limited deferral for federal government audit organizations: quality-management design and implementation may be deferred no later than March 16, 2026, with evaluation no later than one year after implementation. The ordinary timetable is design and implementation by December 15, 2025 and evaluation by December 15, 2026. The federal note does not say every auditor of a government client qualifies.

The [December 15, 2025 quality-management FAQ](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108710/index.html) [gao-qm-faq], introduction and Section I, is GAGAS interpretive guidance. Its discussion connects quality objectives, assessed risks and responses. A worksheet can organize that reasoning; a completed worksheet alone cannot prove the system operated.

These selected checks are not a full Yellow Book, companion-standard or amendment review. The government-work reproduction notice in the Yellow Book, physical page 8, also cautions about separately copyrighted components. This article copies no diagrams, examples or complete publication. Source access does not create a product source grant or human professional review.

## Apply the verified timing facts to the fictional packet

For the following conditional conclusions, assume the described engagement types are correct, the assignments are within GAGAS, and no early implementation is elected. These additional assumptions are explicit; they do not retrospectively corroborate F-01 or F-03.

| Decision | Original reasoning from the supplied facts | Remaining evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Assignment A: period beginning July 1, 2024 | Its subject period begins before December 15, 2025, so the 2024 revision is not mandatory by that period-start trigger; determine the otherwise applicable predecessor and any actual early implementation | Agreement, actual period and documented adoption decision |
| Assignment B: performance audit beginning January 12, 2026 | Its supplied commencement is after December 15, 2025, so the 2024 trigger is met if that is the relevant actual start | Engagement authorization, planning records and the meaning of the event recorded |
| Both reports proposed for 2026 | A shared report year does not change either comparison | Actual report dates remain separate completion facts |
| Birch's quality-management deadline | The ordinary schedule is the starting point; client work involving a local government does not demonstrate federal-audit-organization status | Evidence about Birch itself and any applicable exception |

A real file should not resolve Assignment A by printing the old checklist and stopping. The file needs the exact predecessor edition, other applicable standards, work already performed and a documented adoption decision. Similarly, identifying the 2024 trigger for B is only the start of planning under that edition, not proof that every requirement has been met.

### Borderline dates expose the mistaken shortcut

Create two additional fictional financial-audit periods beginning December 14 and December 15, 2025. Under the verified period-start wording and the assumptions above, the second meets the mandatory 2024 threshold and the first does not. A performance audit actually begun December 14 and reported in July 2026 is a different comparison from one begun December 15. This one-day boundary exercise tests the trigger, not whether either engagement complied.

A report dated January 2026 covering a year that began January 2025 is still a period beginning before the threshold. Treating every “2026 report” as a 2024-revision engagement would therefore misclassify at least this supplied scenario. Conversely, ignoring early implementation would make an unconditional predecessor conclusion too strong.

## Build a quality-risk response that can be tested

Suppose Birch's fictional risk assessment identifies that an understaffed team may finish a complex engagement without a suitably experienced review. Merely storing a policy titled “all work is reviewed” does not tell a reviewer whether that risk was addressed. Build an original evidence chain:

1. State the intended outcome and the concrete circumstance that could prevent it; distinguish the likelihood and consequence from a vague label such as “staffing risk.”
2. Describe the proposed response: who reviews what, which skills they need, when the review occurs, and how unresolved matters prevent release.
3. Define evidence of implementation separately from evidence of operation. A signed policy may support approval of the design; dated review records and resolved comments address what happened on an engagement.
4. Describe monitoring that could find a missed review. Do not assume absence of complaints means absence of deficiencies.
5. Retain the actual evaluation, deficiencies and corrective decisions, with their dates. A future training plan is not evidence that a past review occurred.

This chain is an original teaching aid. It is not an exhaustive list of Yellow Book quality-management requirements or an engagement quality review checklist. Actual criteria and responsible people must come from the applicable requirements and organizational decisions.

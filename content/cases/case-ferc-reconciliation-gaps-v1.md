# Worked case: a balanced bridge can still contain unresolved mappings

Cedar Grid is a fictional entity. These balances illustrate a research worksheet only; they are not mapped to an actual FERC account or form. No jurisdiction, filing category, applicable instruction or reporting basis has been confirmed. All numbers are in thousands of USD, using the same period solely for this exercise.

## Proposed bridge and residual

Two preparer proposals would add 30 and subtract 12 from the ledger gross grouping: 1,200 + 30 - 12 = **1,218**. Against the target of 1,210, residual (proposed result minus target) is **+8**. Retain that residual for investigation. Do not create an unsupported -8 entry and call the work complete. The accepted bridge is still unknown because neither proposal has supporting authority or approval.

If an actual reviewer later rejects the +30 proposal while the -12 remains only proposed, the remaining candidate is 1,188 and the residual is -22. Preserve this as a new scenario, not a replacement for the original ledger or a claim that -12 is accepted. The sign convention must be visible in every scenario.

Net amounts reveal another check: the initial ledger is 1,200 - 300 = 900, while the proposed gross bridge implies 1,218 - 300 = 918. The target net 910 differs by 8 under the assumed unchanged contra balance. If contra balances also need adjustment, this shortcut is invalid; analyze them separately.

## Counterexample: identical net balance, different gross presentation

A second fictional source shows gross 1,240 and contra 340, yielding net 900. The first ledger also yields net 900, but each gross/contra figure differs by 40. Matching net totals does not resolve source completeness, account classification or presentation. Obtain underlying detail and applicable instructions rather than accepting the tie.

## Counterexample: entity differences cancel

For an unrelated fictional comparison, North reports 500 versus target 480, and South reports 700 versus target 720. Combined balances equal 1,200 on both sides. Entity differences are +20 and -20: net zero, gross absolute differences **40**. Investigate both; do not infer that an intercompany reclassification is permitted or that either target is correct.

## A rollforward exposes timing and sign errors

Use a separate fictional asset-group dataset, still in thousands of USD: opening gross 1,100; additions 150; retirements 40; transfers in 20; transfers out 30. Assume those are the only movements and all belong in the same period and population. The expected closing gross is **1,100 + 150 − 40 + 20 − 30 = 1,200**. Each label is supplied for arithmetic, not a FERC classification.

If the ledger extract closes at 1,210, the unexplained difference is **+10** using ledger minus expected. A preparer suggests an unrecorded transfer, but no record supports it. Keep the residual at 10 and request evidence. Renaming it “transfer” does not establish occurrence, date, entity, account or authority.

Now supply a separate supported correction to the input: transfers out were 20 rather than 30. The revised rollforward is **1,100 + 150 − 40 + 20 − 20 = 1,210**. Preserve the original source and the correction evidence; do not edit the earlier 30 to hide the reason the worksheet changed. A balanced revised rollforward addresses the numerical mismatch only, not the correct regulatory mapping.

## Two bridges should not share an unexplained adjustment

Suppose a proposed gross increase of 30 and proposed contra increase of 10 are both included in a new scenario. Gross becomes 1,230; contra becomes 310; net is **920**. Relative to the original net 900, the net increase is **20**, not 30. If the worksheet subtracts the contra change twice, it would incorrectly show 910 and appear to match the original target.

Keep gross and contra movements separately traceable, with a net calculation derived from them. An adjustment applied to one side of a bridge must not be repeated inside a second netting step. The original 1,210/300 target is still a supplied workpaper target, not a validated regulatory amount.

## Rounding can explain a difference only when the rule and inputs explain it

For a separate example, assume two source balances are 1,499 and 1,499 whole USD and the display rule rounds to the nearest thousand. Rounding each line first gives 1 + 1 = **2 thousand**. Summing first gives 2,998 USD, displayed as **3 thousand**. The one-thousand displayed difference results from these specified operations; it is not an unexplained journal difference.

Preserve unrounded inputs and the required reporting convention. This example does not establish which convention a real FERC form requires. Do not label every small residual “rounding”; reproduce the actual calculation and assess whether a disclosure or adjustment is needed under the applicable instructions.

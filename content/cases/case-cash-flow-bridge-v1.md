# Worked case: an equipment balance change is not the same number as a bank movement

**AI-assisted educational draft · v1.1.0 · 2026-10-04 · AI editorial checks only; no human professional review.**

## Fictional facts and scope

All amounts are USD. Opening equipment carrying amount is 400000. Stipulate additions of 120000 paid through the entity's selected bank population and 100000 paid directly by a lender to the vendor, without a bank movement in that population. Equipment carrying amount of 30000 is removed. Disposal cash proceeds are 25000. No depreciation or other movement is included in this deliberately simplified case.

The payment path, population and any investing classification are exercise assumptions. Current ASC 230 and other relevant requirements were not accessed; this is not a classification opinion or an actual statement of cash flows. [fasb-asc]

## Separate the three bridges

| Question | Stipulated calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Equipment closing balance | 400000 + 120000 + 100000 − 30000 | 590000 |
| Equipment balance increase | 590000 − 400000 | 190000 |
| Net selected-bank movements for these purchases/disposal | −120000 + 25000 | −95000 |

The USD 190000 asset increase is not the USD 120000 bank purchase payment or the USD 95000 net outflow. The direct lender-paid addition affects the stipulated asset bridge without entering the selected bank-movement bridge.

The difference between the removed carrying amount and proceeds is 30000 − 25000 = USD 5000. It is a separate comparison, not an additional cash payment. No accounting loss classification or indirect-method adjustment is determined here.

## Counterexample: change the financing path, keep the asset facts

In a separate hypothetical branch, assume the lender first deposits 100000 into the entity's included bank account and the entity then pays that 100000 to the vendor. Add a +100000 bank receipt and a −100000 bank payment.

Those two new legs net to zero, so the overall net bank movement for the stipulated events remains −95000. Gross bank purchase payments become 220000 instead of 120000, while the equipment closing balance remains 590000 under the same asset assumptions. Identical net totals therefore do not prove identical gross flows or classification.

Do not select the direct-payment or through-bank branch from an account caption. Obtain the actual settlement records, payer/recipient identities and cash-population scope.

## Missing evidence and output

A real workpaper also needs the complete acquisition/disposal records, fees, restrictions, other components, depreciation and applicable requirements. An omission from this small example is not evidence of a zero real-world amount.

Return the signed asset bridge, separate bank movements, financing-path alternatives, assumptions and unresolved source/classification questions. Do not add a balancing cash line to force a balance-sheet change into a cash-flow category.

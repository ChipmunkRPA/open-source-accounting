# Worked case: positive ending cash can conceal a funding gap

**AI-assisted editorial draft · v0.6.0 · 2026-09-27 · No professional review recorded.**

A fictional liquidity scheduling exercise, not a prediction of solvency or an assurance conclusion.

## Facts and assumptions

Assume opening unrestricted cash of $800,000. Cash outflows are $150,000 per month for months one through six. A $500,000 financing receipt is expected at the end of month six, after that month's payments. The receipt is not committed. No other flows occur in this simplified case.

## Reproduce the schedule

Month-end cash before the financing is $650,000, $500,000, $350,000, $200,000, $50,000, and negative $100,000. Including the assumed financing leaves month-six ending cash of $400,000. The positive final amount conceals an inability to fund all earlier payments under the stipulated timing. A scenario excluding the uncommitted receipt remains negative $100,000.

## Research needed

Determine payment dates, available credit, restrictions on cash, obligations that can legally be deferred, and evidence supporting management's plan. Confirm the relevant evaluation horizon and accounting criteria from current authority. Do not label the scenario itself as a completed going-concern assessment.

## Expected deliverable

The Agent should produce both scenarios, the first funding-gap date, the uncommitted-financing flag, and missing evidence. It should not turn an expected equity raise into an available financing source, conceal the intramonth shortfall, or promise that the company will remain solvent.

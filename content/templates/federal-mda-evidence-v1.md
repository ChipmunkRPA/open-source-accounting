## Worked example: higher throughput can coexist with worse quality

Assume a fictional federal service unit processes applications. Costs are USD for the same annual periods and organizational scope. For this exercise, total costs include rework and relate to the reported service population; completed and accepted counts have consistent definitions across years. These are supplied assumptions, not assertions established by a ledger or performance system.

| Metric | Prior year | Current year |
|---|---:|---:|
| Service cost | 1,200,000 | 1,320,000 |
| Completed applications | 1,500 | 1,650 |
| Applications passing the stated first-pass quality check | 1,425 | 1,485 |
| Completed applications failing that check | 75 | 165 |

Calculations, using unrounded values until final display:

- Costs increased **120,000**, or **10.00%**; completed applications increased **150**, also **10.00%**.
- Cost per completed application remained **800.00** in both periods.
- First-pass acceptance rate declined from **95.00%** to **90.00%**, a **5.00 percentage-point** decline. This is not a 5% relative decline; the relative decline is about **5.26%**.
- First-pass accepted applications increased by **60**, or **4.21%**, despite the lower acceptance rate.
- Total cost divided by first-pass accepted applications increased from **842.11** to **888.89**, or **5.56%**, rounded. This ratio includes all supplied costs; it is not the marginal cost of an accepted application, a valuation, or a claim that rejected applications consumed no resources.

**A balanced draft observation:** “The unit completed more applications while cost per completed application remained unchanged. First-pass acceptance fell, and total cost per first-pass accepted application increased. The records supplied for this exercise do not establish the cause of the quality decline or its subsequent resolution.”

Calling this an unqualified efficiency improvement would omit conflicting evidence. Conversely, saying every result worsened would omit the increase in accepted applications. Neither ratio tells us whether the service achieved its mission, how long applicants waited, or whether errors had significant consequences.

## Explanation bridge: mathematical attribution is not a causal finding

For the same fictional data, a volume-first arithmetic bridge at the prior average cost per completion is:

**1,200,000 + (1,650 − 1,500) × 800 = 1,320,000.**

The arithmetic residual is zero. That does not prove that volume caused every dollar of additional cost. Wage changes, case complexity, automation spending, rework and allocation changes could offset one another. This decomposition uses a fixed comparison rate, not an estimated marginal-cost function. Label it as an arithmetic bridge and request records supporting any causal narrative.

A proposed explanation that a new tool improved efficiency needs implementation dates, usage evidence, comparable case mix, cost classifications and outcome data. Even if costs reconcile perfectly, do not state that a management control operated effectively or that the tool caused the outcome without the relevant evidence. A favorable second model answer is not independent corroboration.

## Exceptions that require a different comparison

**Changed denominator.** Suppose the current-year “completed” count includes withdrawn cases that the prior definition excluded. The cost-per-completion comparison is not comparable as stated. Preserve the original series, quantify the definition change where evidence permits, and present a clearly labeled comparable series with reconciliation. Do not silently overwrite history or force a trend by relabeling counts.

**Zero or missing baseline.** If a new program has no prior-year activity, percentage growth from zero is undefined. Report absolute changes and explain the new scope. Missing data is not zero. If an outcome count is zero, do not display zero cost per outcome or divide by zero; retain the actual cost and explain why the ratio is unavailable.

**Timing mismatch.** Current-year costs and applications approved from earlier-year cohorts do not automatically form a meaningful efficiency ratio. Specify whether the measure is a period workload measure or a cohort measure; reconcile timing and exclusions. A count received after year end is not automatically evidence of a year-end condition.

**Incomplete control evidence.** A management narrative may need an assessment of controls, but this workbook supplies none. Record the required assessment owner, supporting reports, exact scope and unresolved deficiencies. Do not fill the blank with “effective,” infer an audit opinion from an operational metric, or claim remediation from a planned action.

## Reusable workpaper

Use one record per proposed narrative claim. A source URL alone does not support an entity-specific statement.

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Claim and purpose | Exact proposed sentence; decision/use it serves; whether it describes fact, calculation, judgment, forecast or hypothesis |
| Reporting scope | Entity and components, period, framework, currencies/units, materiality judgment and applicable instructions |
| Source records | Ledger/performance-system snapshot IDs, hashes, exact page/cell/query locators, owners and access restrictions |
| Population | Numerator and denominator definitions, inclusions/exclusions, period/cohort basis, missing values, prior-year comparability |
| Reconciliation | Ties to financial/performance reports; adjustments and their evidence; original and comparable totals kept separately |
| Calculation | Formula, unrounded inputs, rounding rule and reproducible result; division-by-zero and missing-data handling |
| Explanation | Evidence supporting causes; plausible competing explanations; distinction between arithmetic attribution and causation |
| Balance and uncertainty | Positive and negative outcomes, confidence limits where supported, known measurement gaps and unresolved contradictions |
| Forward-looking material | Assumptions, scenario date, owner, dependencies and uncertainty; clearly separated from achieved results |
| Controls and compliance | Actual management assessment and its scope, applicable obligations, deficiencies and remediation evidence; otherwise unresolved |
| Review | Actual authorized reviewer, exact reviewed revision/hash, date, scope and decision; leave unperformed reviews blank |
| Release | Approved narrative revision, supporting evidence links, source-operation/export checks and remaining conditions; no automatic filing |

Preserve alternatives and rejected explanations with reasons. Distinguish annual cost, budget authority, obligations and cash disbursements; they are not interchangeable denominators or causes. For example, a spending increase alone does not establish an expense increase. Resolve classification under applicable evidence rather than inserting a convenient number into the template.

## Study questions and stopping points

1. Does unchanged cost per completed application prove overall performance improved? No. Quality, outcomes, timing and case mix still matter; the first-pass acceptance rate declined here.
2. Is the five-point rate decline the same as a 5% relative decline? No. The relative decline is 5 divided by 95, about 5.26%.
3. Does a zero residual in the volume bridge prove volume was the only cause? No. It is arithmetic under an assumed rate; causal attribution needs evidence.
4. Can a preparer use zero in place of an unavailable prior-year count? No. Record the missing information and withhold the unsupported percentage comparison.
5. Does this SIG acquisition or workbook approve an entity's MD&A or control assessment? No. Source rights, actual entity evidence, applicability, authorized management decisions and independent professional review remain separate.

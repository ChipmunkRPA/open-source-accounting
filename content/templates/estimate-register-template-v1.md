## Original worked record: a probability-weighted teaching estimate

Assume a fictional model assigns three mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcomes: zero at probability 0.50; USD 40,000 at 0.30; and USD 100,000 at 0.20. The probabilities are invented teaching inputs, not management judgments validated by evidence. The exercise does not select an accounting recognition or measurement model.

The probabilities sum to **0.50 + 0.30 + 0.20 = 1.00**. The weighted amount is **0 × 0.50 + 40,000 × 0.30 + 100,000 × 0.20 = USD 32,000**. That number is not the most likely individual outcome, which is zero under the supplied inputs. Choosing expected value, most likely amount or another method is an accounting question the arithmetic does not answer.

If a proposed management adjustment adds USD 8,000, the final proposed amount is **USD 40,000**. Show the 32,000 model output and 8,000 adjustment separately. A final number matching a desired target does not substantiate the overlay or establish that it should be recognized. Retain the rationale, contrary evidence, authority and actual reviewer decision for each component.

## Sensitivity: change a coherent set of assumptions

In a separate sensitivity, shift 0.10 probability from the zero outcome to the 100,000 outcome, leaving the middle probability at 0.30. The new probabilities are 0.40, 0.30 and 0.30, still totaling one. The weighted amount becomes **USD 42,000**, a **USD 10,000** increase from 32,000. This is a deterministic sensitivity, not a confidence interval or a prediction that the change will occur.

Changing one probability without offsetting another would make the set inconsistent unless the model explicitly supports a different structure. Overlapping events also invalidate a simple mutually-exclusive-outcome interpretation. Record the actual probability model and dependencies rather than normalizing numbers mechanically to make them sum to one.

## Model risk can dominate arithmetic precision

Suppose a model uses average cost per claim and expected claim count. A precise multiplication can still be misleading if high claim counts tend to coincide with higher severity. Multiplying separate averages can ignore that dependency. Define the joint scenarios or other supported method, identify exclusions and show how uncertainty is handled. This lesson does not prescribe a distribution, discount rate, risk adjustment or accounting technique.

A spreadsheet cell labeled “best estimate” should identify the model, population, measurement date and significant assumptions. Different models can produce the same amount for different reasons. Conversely, a model revision can change an estimate even when no new economic event occurred. Keep model changes, new evidence, judgment changes and error corrections in separate categories for analysis.

## Retrospective comparison without hindsight

Assume a later settlement is USD 50,000. Comparing it with the original 32,000 model output gives a **USD 18,000** difference, but that difference alone does not prove the original estimate was unreasonable. Examine what information was available at the measurement date, how the model used it, and whether later events changed the facts.

Likewise, a later settlement matching the estimate does not validate an unsupported method. A poor method can be lucky. Preserve both favorable and unfavorable back-testing evidence, distinguish a single observation from a pattern, and do not tune historical assumptions after the fact to make the model look accurate.

## Evidence and release decisions

For each significant input, record its factual basis, owner, date, uncertainty and relationship to the measurement objective. Preserve contradictory observations, rejected methods and the actual reason for judgmental adjustments. An AI explanation can help organize those questions; it does not create management's intent, corroborate a forecast, choose the required model or provide independent professional review.

## Reviewer questions

- Can every significant input be reproduced?
- Were conflicting sources retained?
- Are retrospective results kept separate from information available at the original date?
- Does the conclusion fit the model actually required?

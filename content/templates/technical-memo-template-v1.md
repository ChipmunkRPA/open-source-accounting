Do not use “reviewed” as an undifferentiated label. An arithmetic check, an AI critique, a rights decision and a qualified person's technical review answer different questions. A typed name is not evidence that someone performed a review.

## 1. Decision question

State the question without embedding the desired answer. Example: “Which evidence is needed to determine how the supplied amendment affects the reporting-period analysis?” is a research question. “Explain why our existing treatment is correct” presupposes the result and should be reframed before analysis.

## 4. Competing analyses

For each plausible alternative, identify supporting facts, contrary facts, decisive requirements and the result if a key assumption changes. Keep accounting, tax and legal issues separate. State why an alternative was rejected; do not dismiss it because it is inconvenient or a peer used different wording.

## 5. Calculations and proposed entries

Record each input's unit, sign, date, source and precision. Show the formula and a reconciliation to the underlying population. State excluded matters. Journal entries remain proposals until properly approved and posted through a separate authorized process.

Original teaching check: an analyst multiplies 80 units by a stipulated USD 25 per unit to obtain USD 2000. If only 60 units belong in the confirmed population, 60 × 25 gives USD 1500 and the difference is USD 500. Arithmetic can expose that dependency; it cannot decide which population or price is valid, whether an amount should be recognized, or which accounts to use.

## 6. Conditional conclusion and release checklist

Write: [proposed conclusion] depends on [verified facts and criteria] and remains subject to [specific unresolved matters]. Identify the decision owner and next evidence action only if actually assigned. Do not remove limitations merely to produce a short executive summary; summarize the decisive ones there.

Before release, verify citations, calculations, factual conflicts, scope exclusions, export permissions and the exact reviewed revision. Preserve a human review declaration only for the text actually reviewed. A later revision needs its own applicable review; an earlier record is historical evidence of that earlier review, not proof of approval of new wording.

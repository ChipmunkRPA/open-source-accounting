# ASC 210 lesson 5: separate a long-term restricted cash fund from current resources

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Draft reader edition 0.1.1 · October 5, 2026**

Original educational draft. Not professionally reviewed. Current authoritative requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. Topic and lesson numbers organize this reading series; lesson numbers are not ASC paragraph numbers.

## Focused question

When does a long-term restriction keep a cash balance out of current resources?

## One concept

The word cash describes the asset's form. It does not establish that the money is available for ordinary current operations. A binding restriction can reserve a fund for a long-term purpose or prevent access beyond the applicable current-classification horizon. The restriction's purpose and release conditions therefore need examination before the balance is grouped with current resources.

For a limited example of actual reporting practice, [Fluence Energy's March 31, 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1868941/000110465926056304/flnc-20260331x10q.htm) describes noncurrent treatment for restricted funds associated with long-term uses or extended unavailability. That is the company's disclosed policy, not SEC approval or a complete authoritative rule for every entity. The fictional illustration below does not reproduce its transactions or amounts.

## Small fictional example

Fictional Amber Kite Services has $37,000 in an unrestricted operating account and $63,000 in a separate escrow account at December 31, Year 1. Under the stipulated enforceable escrow terms, the second account can be used only to repay principal due on March 31, Year 4. Amber Kite cannot withdraw or redirect it before that date. Assume a one-year classification horizon, a continuing ordinary reporting basis and no special provision that changes the conclusion.

The $63,000 fund serves the long-term repayment purpose and is unavailable for current operations under these facts. It is presented as a noncurrent restricted asset in this simplified example. The $37,000 operating balance remains current. The classification split preserves $100,000 of total cash assets; it does not create a loss or remove the escrow balance from the entity's assets.

If Amber Kite has $18,000 of other current assets, the stated balances produce $55,000 of current assets: $37,000 plus $18,000. Including the escrow in that subtotal would produce $118,000, overstating current assets by $63,000. Total assets would be unchanged by that classification error, assuming the escrow was counted only once.

The escrow does not disappear against the borrowing merely because both relate to the same repayment. Any permission to offset an asset and liability is a separate question, outside this lesson.

Change the facts: management merely names an ordinary bank account Future Projects and can freely redirect its funds. The account name alone does not establish the binding restriction assumed above. Conversely, a restricted fund intended to settle a current obligation is not automatically noncurrent merely because a restriction exists. Each case requires its own purpose, timing and applicable-guidance analysis.

## Common mistake

Treating every bank balance as available current cash, or treating every restricted balance as noncurrent. Either shortcut replaces the actual conditions with an account label.

## Check the decision

Would a proposed request to release the escrow make the $63,000 currently available if the required approval has not been obtained?

No, under the stated terms. A hoped-for change is not the same as an effective release. If circumstances later change, the facts and the relevant reporting-date requirements need reassessment.

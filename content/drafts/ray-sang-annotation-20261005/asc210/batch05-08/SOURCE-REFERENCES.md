# Source references and limits

References checked October 5, 2026. These links support only the limited observations identified below. The lesson scenarios, amounts and explanations are original; no source passages or illustrations are reproduced.

## Lesson 5: restricted cash

[Fluence Energy, Inc., Form 10-Q for March 31, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1868941/000110465926056304/flnc-20260331x10q.htm), Note 2, Cash, Cash Equivalents, and Restricted Cash, printed page 12.

Verified observation: the issuer describes noncurrent presentation for cash restricted for long-term purposes or extended unavailability. The disclosure is evidence of its stated policy. It is not a complete current-authority check, an SEC endorsement or proof of another entity's result.

## Lesson 6: operating cycle

[Northrop Grumman, policy disclosure for June 30, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1133421/000113342126000034/R18.htm), Basis of Presentation.

Verified observation: the issuer uses customer-contract duration to define an operating cycle longer than a year for certain current assets and liabilities. The lesson's particular twenty-month cycle and trade-payable facts are invented. The issuer's disclosure does not prove those assumptions or supply all classification conditions. Embedded taxonomy locators are not adopted as verified ASC mappings.

## Lessons 7 and 8: elementary liability and working-capital concepts

[SEC, Beginners' Guide to Financial Statement](https://www.sec.gov/reportspubs/investor-publications/investorpubsbegfinstmtguidehtm.html), dated February 4, 2007, Balance Sheets and Financial Statement Ratios and Calculations sections.

Verified observations: liabilities can include future goods or services owed to customers; working capital subtracts current liabilities from current assets. This is an introductory guide, not a current comprehensive accounting standard. The detailed fictional service schedule and payment-date calculations are separate illustrations.

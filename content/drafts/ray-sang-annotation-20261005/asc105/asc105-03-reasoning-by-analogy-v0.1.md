# ASC 105 foundational lesson 3: test the accounting feature that makes an analogy relevant

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

When a transaction lacks directly applicable guidance, first consider authoritative guidance for similar transactions or circumstances before using nonauthoritative literature to support a treatment. A useful analogy depends on the economic and contractual feature that drives the accounting. Similar names, payment amounts or industries provide much weaker support.

The preparer must also consider the limits of the candidate guidance. A model designed for a tightly defined population may rely on facts absent from the new transaction. An express prohibition on applying a model by analogy cannot be overcome by a general statement that the economics seem similar. Any real analogy needs the candidate guidance's scope and restrictions checked.

Analogy is therefore an argument with a stated connection and stated weaknesses. It is not a claim that two transactions are identical in every respect.

## Work through an analogy that breaks

Willow Fabrication, a fictional manufacturer, prepays $27,000 to reserve six production slots at a supplier. Three facts are stipulated: Willow can direct the supplier to deliver its specified parts during the reservation period; the supplier must make the reserved capacity available; and Willow receives no ownership interest in the supplier.

A preparer suggests treating the payment like an equity investment because both arrangements provide a future economic benefit. That shared feature is too broad. Many assets provide future benefits; it does not follow that they all use the same recognition or measurement model. The absence of an ownership interest is a material difference for this proposed comparison.

Another preparer suggests examining models involving advance payments for goods or services. This comparison is more closely connected to the stipulated rights, but it remains a candidate. The team must first finish its direct-guidance search and understand cancellation, transferability, enforceability, whether any underlying asset is controlled, and what happens if Willow does not use a slot.

Assume a separate, properly supported analysis eventually establishes equal consumption of a prepaid benefit as each of the six slots is used. The illustrative amount per slot is $4,500. After two slots are used, consumption would total $9,000 and the unconsumed amount would be $18,000 before considering any separately required loss or other adjustment. Those amounts illustrate the assumed consumption pattern. They do not prove the candidate accounting model is appropriate.

## Change one feature and test again

Suppose the supplier can cancel every slot at will and keep the money without providing any substitute or refund. That change may affect whether Willow actually has the enforceable benefit assumed above. Reusing the same six-slot schedule would conceal the change in rights.

Alternatively, suppose Willow receives freely tradable ownership units and distributions tied to the supplier's results. The analysis would need to address those added features. The word “reservation” on the invoice could no longer be treated as a complete description of the arrangement.

For each proposed analogy, write the accounting feature being compared, the similarities supporting it, the differences that could defeat it, and the restrictions that must be checked. If a crucial fact is unresolved, the conclusion remains conditional.

## Check your understanding

Two arrangements both require a $27,000 payment today. Is that a strong reason to use the same accounting model?

No. The rights obtained and obligations incurred are more informative than the payment's size or timing alone. The amount may be relevant to measurement after the appropriate model is established.

# ASC 105 foundational lesson 7: identify the SEC reporting layer without treating every SEC document alike

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

An entity's reporting obligations can include an SEC layer in addition to the accounting requirements relevant to the underlying transaction. Whether that layer applies depends on the entity, the report being prepared and the relevant legal or regulatory requirements. “U.S. GAAP” and “all requirements for this SEC filing” should not be treated as interchangeable claims.

The character of an SEC-related document also matters. Commission rules, staff interpretations, staff manuals, comment letters and another registrant's filings do not all serve the same purpose or carry identical authority. A page hosted by a regulator may be highly useful without resolving the authoritative accounting question on its own.

Entity status should be recorded precisely. Different accounting amendments may use different defined populations. “Private company,” “public business entity,” “SEC registrant” and other specialized categories should not be swapped casually; the definition relevant to the particular requirement controls.

## Work through a peer-disclosure comparison

Fictional North Quay Tools prepares a financial report for a lender. It sells similar products to a listed company and finds that company's annual report. The listed company includes a detailed presentation and several additional disclosures in its filing.

North Quay's preparer proposes copying that presentation because the companies operate in the same industry. Similar business activity makes the peer filing useful for generating questions. It does not establish North Quay's complete obligations or show why the peer included every item.

Some information may respond to a broadly applicable accounting requirement. Other information may depend on the peer's SEC reporting obligations, transaction facts, materiality judgments or voluntary presentation choices. Those possibilities must be separated before drawing a conclusion about North Quay.

Now suppose North Quay begins preparing a registration statement. The task has changed. A memo prepared only for the lender's original reporting purpose does not become filing-ready by changing its cover page. The team must identify the financial statements, periods, presentation, disclosures and other information required for the new purpose and determine who must review them. This lesson does not identify those requirements or set a filing timetable.

## Evaluate a comment-letter example carefully

Assume a staff comment asks the listed peer to explain the basis for a particular disclosure. The existence of that question does not establish that the staff rejected the treatment, accepted it, or announced a universal rule for every company. The response, subsequent correspondence, company facts and relevant requirements may all matter.

A useful research note records the narrower observation: a particular issue was raised in a particular context. It then states the separate accounting or reporting proposition that still needs support. This avoids converting an interesting example into an unsupported command.

The same discipline applies to a summary. If it compresses a rule, a staff view and a company disclosure into “the SEC requires,” the summary has removed a distinction that may determine how the information should be used.

## Check your understanding

North Quay finds no matching disclosure in one peer's filing. Does that prove North Quay can omit it?

No. The peer's facts, reporting period, obligations and materiality assessment may differ. An apparent omission in one report does not establish permission for another entity.

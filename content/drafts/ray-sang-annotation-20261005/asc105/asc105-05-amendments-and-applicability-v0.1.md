# ASC 105 foundational lesson 5: separate an announced amendment from the rule for this reporting period

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

A standard-setting announcement and an accounting requirement applicable to an entity's current statements answer different questions. An Accounting Standards Update communicates amendments to the Codification. It is not a freestanding substitute for identifying the applicable codified guidance, including its effective-date and transition provisions.

Issuance tells the reader when an amendment was released. An effective date tells the reader when specified entities must apply it under the stated conditions. An early-adoption provision may permit earlier application, but permission must be established; it cannot be inferred from the fact that the update is available. Transition explains how the change enters the financial statements and may require more than changing the current-period journal entry.

Entity category and reporting period can change the answer. Two entities reading the same announcement on the same day may be required to apply it in different periods.

## Work through a fictional timeline

The following dates describe an invented training rule called **Practice Change P**. Practice Change P is not an ASU, does not have an ASC identifier and is not evidence of any actual amendment.

Stipulate that Practice Change P is issued on March 10, Year 1, applies to annual reporting periods beginning after December 15, Year 2, and does not permit early adoption. Ignore interim-period and transition complications for this first calculation.

- A calendar-year entity's Year 2 annual period began January 1, Year 2. That date is before the threshold, so the stipulated rule would not yet apply to that period
- Its Year 3 annual period begins January 1, Year 3, which is after the threshold. That is the first annual period to which the stipulated rule applies
- A June 30 year-end entity's period ending June 30, Year 3 began July 1, Year 2. That beginning date is also before the threshold
- Its next annual period begins July 1, Year 3 and ends June 30, Year 4. That is its first annual period after the stipulated threshold

The different first year-ends follow from the same beginning-date condition. Looking only at whether a balance-sheet date falls after December 15, Year 2 would produce the wrong answer for the June year-end entity.

## Add a transition condition

Now stipulate that the training rule also requires an opening adjustment in the first year of application. A preparer cannot implement that instruction simply by applying the new method to transactions occurring after the first day of that year. The opening balances and the stipulated transition method would also need analysis.

Conversely, if the training rule required prospective application to a defined population of new transactions, automatically revising every historical balance could be wrong. “The rule changed” does not identify how prior balances or comparative information should be handled.

For a real amendment, retain the actual issuance information, entity categories, annual and interim applicability, early-adoption conditions, transition method and any later changes. Do not substitute these fictional dates or simplified conditions for an examined amendment.

## Check your understanding

Practice Change P was published in Year 1. Does the absence of early adoption allow an entity to apply it to Year 1 merely because the new method seems better?

No. Under the stipulated facts, the entity has no early-adoption permission. Publication alone does not provide it.

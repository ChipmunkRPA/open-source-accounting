# ASC 105 foundational lesson 1: identify what makes an accounting answer authoritative

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

An accounting conclusion needs a reporting basis before it can be described as compliant. The reporting basis determines which rule set answers the question. For nongovernmental entities preparing U.S. GAAP financial statements, the FASB Accounting Standards Codification is the central authoritative accounting source. SEC requirements also matter for entities and reporting obligations within the SEC's jurisdiction. An explanatory answer, accounting-firm guide or another company's policy does not acquire the same authority merely by describing that material accurately.

Authority and usefulness are different properties. A preparer may learn a concept from a clear educational explanation and still need to establish the applicable authoritative requirement. Likewise, an authoritative requirement may be irrelevant because the entity, transaction or reporting period falls outside its scope.

## Work through one reporting-basis problem

Juniper Bicycle Repair, a fictional privately owned business, receives $18,000 on December 1 for services to be provided over six months. Its owner asks whether all $18,000 is December revenue. The bookkeeper's worksheet recognizes cash received as revenue because it was designed for a particular cash-based management report.

That worksheet establishes how the management report was prepared. It does not establish the U.S. GAAP result. For a GAAP analysis, the preparer must examine the customer's enforceable rights, the promised services, the timing of performance, any refund terms and the applicable revenue guidance. The fact that cash arrived first does not itself answer when revenue is earned under that model.

For illustration only, stipulate that a properly completed accounting analysis establishes six equal monthly service periods beginning December 1 and revenue recognized evenly during those periods. December revenue would then be $3,000, with $15,000 remaining for subsequent periods. The arithmetic follows from the stipulated accounting conclusion: $18,000 ÷ 6 = $3,000. This lesson does not verify that the contract actually qualifies for that conclusion.

The owner may still use the cash-based report for its stated management purpose. It should be identified by its actual basis. Changing the label to “GAAP” would not change the accounting underneath it.

## Why the distinction changes the work

First identify the reporting entity and the statements being prepared. A government, a nongovernmental business, a nonprofit organization, a tax return and a management cash report may involve different requirements or reporting bases. A familiar account name such as “revenue” does not settle that choice.

Then identify the authority for the particular treatment. Keep three separate statements in the file: the reporting basis selected, the authoritative accounting proposition that applies, and the evidence that the transaction satisfies that proposition. An educational source can help explain the connection, but it cannot supply missing contract facts or certify compliance.

## Check your understanding

Juniper's lender requests GAAP statements, and management supplies the cash-based worksheet with a note explaining its method. Has that note established GAAP compliance?

No. The requested reporting basis still governs the accounting analysis. An explanatory note does not, by itself, resolve a departure from that basis. Whether any difference is material requires a separate assessment; it should not be assumed from the small size of the business.

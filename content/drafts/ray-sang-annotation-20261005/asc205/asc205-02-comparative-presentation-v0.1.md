# ASC 205 presentation lesson 2: compare like amounts across reporting periods

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

Comparative presentation is useful only if the reader can understand what each number includes. A changed caption, a different reporting-period length or a movement between line items can create the appearance of economic change even when the underlying amounts have not changed. The preparer needs to explain the basis of comparison and apply the relevant requirements for any change in presentation.

A reclassification changes where a previously recognized amount appears. An accounting-policy change, an estimate revision and an error correction can have different consequences. Their treatment cannot be selected merely by labeling the change a reclassification. General presentation provides the organizing question; the requirements governing changes and errors need separate consideration.

Consistent presentation also does not mean preserving an unsuitable layout forever. A more informative layout may be appropriate. The work is to support the new layout, determine how comparative information should be handled, and make the resulting comparison understandable.

## Work through a misleading expense trend

Fictional Maple Instruments presents Year 2 and Year 1 income statements side by side. Its originally issued Year 1 statement reported $410,000 in production costs and $120,000 in administrative expenses. The production-cost amount included $30,000 of distribution costs.

In Year 2, Maple presents distribution costs as their own $36,000 line. Year 2 production costs, excluding distribution, are $428,000, and administrative expenses are $126,000. Assume that a separate accounting analysis supports the three-line presentation and the corresponding reclassification of the comparative Year 1 amount. There is no recognition or measurement error, inventory effect, tax effect or change in total expense in this illustration.

On the comparable basis, Year 1 production costs are $380,000, distribution costs are $30,000, and administrative expenses are $120,000. Year 1 total expense remains $530,000. Year 2 total expense is $590,000: $428,000 + $36,000 + $126,000.

Production costs on the comparable basis rose $48,000, or approximately 12.63%. Comparing $428,000 with the unreclassified $410,000 would instead suggest growth of approximately 4.39%. That smaller percentage mixes two definitions of the line item. It is arithmetically calculable but does not measure the intended like-for-like change.

Distribution costs rose $6,000, or 20%. Administrative expenses also rose $6,000, or 5%. These changes add to the $60,000 increase in total expense. The reclassification explains the categories; it does not erase any expense or improve either year's total profit.

## Describe the change accurately

The explanatory note should identify what moved and why the presentation changed, with the detail required for the actual reporting circumstances. It should not imply that Year 1's accounting was erroneous when the supported conclusion is only a presentation change. Conversely, an omitted liability cannot be cured by describing its eventual recognition as a caption change.

The preparer should also examine the period labels. A twelve-month Year 2 and a nine-month comparative period do not become directly comparable because they share the same headings. Clearly identify their lengths and evaluate the additional explanation required. This lesson does not prescribe a universal number of comparative years; that depends on the reporting context and applicable requirements.

## Check your understanding

May Maple show Year 1 production costs of $380,000 but leave out the corresponding $30,000 distribution amount because the new line did not exist in the old statement?

No. Under the stipulated reclassification, the $30,000 moves to the new category and remains in total expense. Leaving it out would change the reported total rather than explain its presentation.

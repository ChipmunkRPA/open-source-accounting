# ASC 205 presentation lesson 4: separate discontinued results without changing total income

**Ray Sang Annotation**

**Original educational draft · Version 0.1.2 · October 5, 2026**

This lesson uses an original fictional example. No FASB or ASC text was retrieved or reconstructed for this lesson. Current requirements, amendments and paragraph mapping have not been verified. No human professional review is recorded. The lesson number is editorial, not an ASC paragraph number.

## The specific accounting idea

Once discontinued-operations classification has been established, presenting the qualifying results separately helps readers distinguish continuing activities from the disposed or disposing activity. The separation does not delete the activity's results from the entity's total net income.

The familiar presentation distinguishes income from continuing operations from discontinued results after the related income-tax effects. Determining those tax effects is a separate accounting task. A convenient percentage cannot be imposed simply to make the discontinued-operations line easy to calculate.

Comparative presentation also matters. When the applicable requirements call for comparative results to reflect the discontinued classification, the preparer uses a consistent basis so that current continuing operations are compared with the corresponding prior continuing activities. This does not, by itself, mean that the original prior-year recognition was an error.

## Work through the income bridge

Fictional Brookline Components disposes of its specialty-tools operation during Year 2. Assume that a separate, fully supported analysis has established discontinued-operations classification and all relevant recognition and measurement conclusions. The illustration has no noncontrolling interests, other comprehensive income or other complications.

For Year 2, the continuing business has $640,000 pretax income and $160,000 related tax expense. Its income from continuing operations is therefore $480,000.

The discontinued activity has a $90,000 pretax operating loss and a $30,000 pretax disposal loss. Assume the tax analysis separately establishes a $30,000 tax benefit attributable to those discontinued losses. The discontinued result is a $90,000 loss after tax: −$90,000 − $30,000 + $30,000 = −$90,000.

Total Year 2 net income is $390,000: $480,000 continuing income less $90,000 discontinued loss. A cross-check reaches the same answer. Total pretax income is $520,000, and total tax expense is $130,000, producing $390,000 net income.

For the comparative Year 1, assume that the same activity contributed $60,000 after-tax income and the continuing business earned $400,000 after tax. The comparative presentation therefore distinguishes $400,000 of continuing income and $60,000 of discontinued income, totaling $460,000.

Continuing income improved by $80,000 between the years. Total net income fell by $70,000. Both statements are true because the discontinued result moved from $60,000 income to a $90,000 loss, a $150,000 deterioration. The bridge is $80,000 − $150,000 = −$70,000.

## Keep classification and measurement separate

The example assumes the disposal loss was already measured correctly. Presenting it in a discontinued category does not determine its measurement date, justify the amount, or authorize combining unrelated restructuring costs with it.

Likewise, a comparative presentation does not permit moving every cost once associated with the sold operation out of continuing operations. Retained corporate costs, continuing arrangements and particular adjustments need analysis under the actual requirements. The fact that a cost appeared in a divisional management report is not sufficient evidence for its financial-statement classification.

A reader should also be able to understand what is included in the discontinued amount through the required supporting information. The single after-tax number is an important presentation result, not a reason to discard the operating, disposal and tax analysis behind it.

## Check your understanding

May Brookline describe $480,000 as total Year 2 net income because that is the amount earned by continuing operations?

No. Under these stipulated facts, $480,000 is income from continuing operations. Total net income includes the $90,000 discontinued loss and is $390,000. The caption must match the measure.

## Eisner v. Macomber

**Ray Sang’s Annotation** · Original decision brief, not the court’s text

252 U.S. 189 (1920) · Supreme Court of the United States · 1920-03-08

Docket: 318

Topics: income-realization, constitutional-tax

**Material facts:** A shareholder received a proportional stock dividend that left her ownership share unchanged. Congress had sought to tax that distribution as income.

**Issue:** Could this proportional stock dividend be taxed as income without apportionment?

**Holding:** The Court held that the stock dividend before it was not taxable income under the Sixteenth Amendment.

**Reasoning:** The distribution represented the same proportional corporate interest rather than a separate gain available to the shareholder.

**Limits:** Do not extrapolate this stock-dividend result into a rule invalidating every tax on undistributed income. Modern statutory exceptions and later decisions require separate analysis.

**Authority:** Nationwide federal precedent, subject to the holding and later law

**Dated history check:** Glenshaw Glass limited the usefulness of Macomber’s income formulation outside its facts. Moore (2024) upheld attribution of entity-realized income and expressly left the broader constitutional realization question unresolved.

**Check scope:** Retained original opinion plus specifically linked cases/primary materials only. Not a complete citator, appeal-docket audit, or current-law certification. Current House U.S. Code pages returned maintenance notices during this batch.

## James v. United States

**Ray Sang’s Annotation** · Original decision brief, not the court’s text

366 U.S. 213 (1961) · Supreme Court of the United States · 1961-05-15

Docket: 63

Topics: income-realization, penalties-criminal

**Material facts:** An embezzler omitted misappropriated funds from his returns during years when Wilcox had treated such funds as outside taxable income.

**Issue:** Are embezzled funds income, and could the prior contrary precedent support his criminal conviction?

## Crane v. Commissioner

**Ray Sang’s Annotation** · Original decision brief, not the court’s text

331 U.S. 1 (1947) · Supreme Court of the United States · 1947-04-14

Docket: 68

Topics: basis-debt

**Material facts:** A taxpayer inherited an apartment building subject to a mortgage, took depreciation, then sold the property subject to the debt.

**Issue:** Is basis limited to equity, and is debt relief included in amount realized?

**Holding:** Basis was not reduced by the mortgage merely because the taxpayer lacked personal liability; the disposition included the mortgage in amount realized.

**Reasoning:** The statute addresses the property interest, not only the owner’s net equity. Debt’s treatment must be consistent with the benefits included in basis.

**Limits:** Crane reserved the problem of debt exceeding property value; Tufts later addressed that nonrecourse-debt issue.

**Authority:** Nationwide federal precedent, subject to the holding and later law

**Dated history check:** Read with Tufts (1983), which resolved the debt-exceeds-value issue reserved in Crane. Current debt-discharge and basis provisions still need transaction-specific review.
